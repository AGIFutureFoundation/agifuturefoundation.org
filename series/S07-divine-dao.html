What this series is

Divine DAO is Series 7 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. That sentence, together with the name itself, is very nearly everything verified that can be said about this series. Its name implies a decentralised organisation concerned with the divine — with meaning, faith, community, and the sacred — and this page describes that as a planned focus, because no doctrine, membership, community, practice, partner, publication, or programme for Divine DAO is verified in the files. This is a thin page on purpose: where there is almost nothing verified to say, the honest page says so plainly instead of filling the space with invention.

The domain the name implies is worth sketching carefully, and it needs more care than most, because it touches questions that no agent and no landing page can settle. A DAO — a decentralised autonomous organisation, governed in part by its members through shared rules — concerned with the divine could mean many things: a community that gathers around shared spiritual practice, a forum for discussing questions of meaning and value, a steward of texts or traditions, or a way of coordinating charitable or communal work. Each of those is a different undertaking, and no verified document says which of them Divine DAO intends. The name alone does not choose between them, and this page does not choose on the series’ behalf.

What can be said without inventing anything is what such a series would have to get right if it were built, and the first item on that list is a boundary. Questions of doctrine, belief, and spiritual authority belong to humans — to a community and its recognised leaders — not to agents, not to a vote weighted by tokens, and not to a landing page. The Foundation’s own materials make this boundary explicit for the neighbouring AGI Fellowship church: agents operate day-to-day work under human board and minister direction, and agents do not enact doctrine, ordain, sign, or move funds. The same boundary would have to apply here, in writing, before Divine DAO did anything substantive: who decides matters of belief and practice, how those decisions are made, and what agents may and may not do in that space. No such document exists for Divine DAO today.

There is one distinction that must be stated plainly, because a reader could easily miss it. Divine DAO is not the AGI Fellowship. The Fellowship is a separate, filed California nonprofit religious corporation (Entity No. B20260229217, Articles filed 2026-05-13, EIN assigned 2026-05-18), with its own site at agifellowship.church, and it is partnered with — not a subsidiary of, not a series of — the Foundation’s other work. Divine DAO, by contrast, is a name in the W3 internal list, with no filing verified. The two should not be confused, and this page does not present Divine DAO as a church, a fellowship, or a place of worship. If Divine DAO ever becomes a community with a stated faith or practice, that statement will need to come from its human members in writing, not from this page.

Metaverse connection

No anchored metaverse fabric yet. Divine DAO has no verified anchored world: no coordinates, no fabric JSON, and no metaverse location are recorded for this series in the materials reviewed. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one — it would send a reader, or a builder, to a place that does not exist.

What would be needed for a metaverse presence is the same sequence that the anchored series went through, and it has not started for Divine DAO: a decision about what a world for this series would be for, a fabric definition published at a checkable address, the assets that fabric references hosted at the paths it expects, and a verified render in Artemis. After that, a live metaverse-server endpoint would be needed before anything could be attached on RP1 — and zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series, so there is no RP1 presence to describe for this one either. A metaverse world for a series concerned with meaning and community would need its own care before it existed: a gathering space is not a congregation, a rendered temple is not a sacred place, and any such world would need to say, in the world itself, what it is and what it is not. No such design exists, so none is claimed, and no scripture is quoted or invented on this page.

What exists today — and what is planned

What exists today

One thing is verified: the name and number — Divine DAO, Series 7, in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. No doctrine, membership, community, practice, partner, publication, or metaverse fabric is verified for this series. That is the complete verified inventory, and it is short. No scripture, testimonial, or endorsement is quoted on this page, because none is verified.

What is planned

Everything else is planned, not existing: the formation of W3 LLC and the designation of this series; a human Steward Council and operating rules; a defined scope (which kind of community or practice, if any); any statement of belief or governance of belief; any funding; and any metaverse presence. Each of those would need its own document, decision, and human signature before it could be stated as fact here — and for this series, the human decisions about belief and practice would need to come first, before any technical build.

The most useful next step for Divine DAO would be the smallest one that adds real information: a written statement from named humans, saying whether this series is intended to become a community at all, and if so, what kind — with the boundary stated in the same document that agents do not enact doctrine, ordain, sign, or move funds. Until that document exists, this page will remain thin, and that thinness is the accurate record — not a draft to be padded, but a faithful account of a series that is currently a name and a planned domain.

Governance — planned, not yet operating

The planned governance model for the W3 series, as described on the Foundation site, is DAO-managed day-to-day work with human decision-making at every point that matters: agents may draft proposals, prepare research, and operate routine systems, but humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. A Steward Council of humans is intended to hold that gate. No Steward Council membership, no signed votes, no seated manager, and no operating agreement for Divine DAO is verified in the files, so none is named here. Until those human roles are seated in writing, there is no one who can speak for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series — and this page does not do so.

What governance would have to answer first. Before Divine DAO could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. Listing the questions is not the same as answering them, and this page deliberately does not pretend otherwise.

What would have to happen before this series could operate

A named series is not a formed series. For Divine DAO to move from a line in an internal list to an operating venture, several distinct steps would each have to happen, in order, with documents to prove them. First, W3 LLC itself would need to be formed and evidenced by a filing that can be checked. Second, this series would need to be designated in writing as a series of that LLC, with its own name, purpose, and manager recorded. Third, the series would need its own operating rules, its own human stewards, and its own segregated accounting — so that its money, liabilities, and records are not mixed with any other series or with the Foundation. Fourth, any bank account, contract, hire, or public claim would need a named human signer acting under those written rules. Fifth, any fundraising would need its own counsel-approved documents, its own issuer, and its own securities compliance — it could not borrow the Foundation’s nonprofit status, and the Foundation, as filed, cannot offer equity or a SAFE. None of those steps is verified as done for Divine DAO. This section is included so that a reader can see the gap plainly, rather than mistaking a polished landing page for a finished company.

That gap is also why this page carries no timeline. There is no launch date, no funding date, and no hiring date stated here, because none has been set in a verified document. A date on a landing page would look like a commitment; without a filing, a budget, and a human team behind it, it would be a decoration, not a plan. When a real date exists, it should appear here with the document behind it cited.
