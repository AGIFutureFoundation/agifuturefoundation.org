What this series is

Lyka DAO is W3 Series 26, and this is the thinnest page in this group of series pages — deliberately so. The April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Lyka DAO — Series 26” and gives no description at all: no domain, no product, no team, no partner, no technical scope, and no statement of what “Lyka” refers to. There is no verified material beyond the name and the number, and this page will not invent any. A reader who wants to know what Lyka DAO does is entitled to a plain answer, and the plain answer today is that it has not been published.

That thinness is itself information, and it is worth stating why rather than papering over it. A series name without a published focus cannot be evaluated: there is no mission to check against the Foundation's public-benefit standard, no market to validate, no risk to assess, and no partner to contact. Until a human steward writes down what Lyka DAO is for — in a sentence that a stranger could test — the series cannot pass even the first stage gate of the Foundation's intended studio model, which requires an idea to be stated before it can be validated. The name alone is not an idea.

What can be said is what the structure around the name implies. “DAO” in the W3 naming pattern denotes a series intended to be DAO-managed day to day: agents operating within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds. That is the governance pattern for the series network as a whole, not a fact about Lyka DAO's subject matter. It tells a reader how the series would be run if it were formed; it says nothing about what it would run. Similarly, its place as Series 26, between Mypet.AI (Series 25) and Genomic DAO (Series 27), is a numbering fact from the internal list, not a statement that the three series are related in subject. They are neighbours on a list, nothing more.

It is also worth saying what Lyka DAO is not, to prevent the name from being misread. It is not a formed company, not a funded venture, and not an investment of any kind; no terms, no token, no membership, and no offering exist. It is not a live DAO: there is no wallet, no contract, no chain, no token, no members, and no votes on record for this series. It has no product, no users, no revenue, and no partners, and this page claims none of those things. If a focus for Lyka DAO is published in future, this page should be rewritten from that publication — not from speculation — and the rewrite should be dated so a reader can see what changed and when.

The honest next step for Lyka DAO is therefore a small, human one, not a technical one: a named person writing a one-page statement of purpose and submitting it to the Foundation's mission gate. That statement would need to answer four questions in plain language: what problem does this series exist to solve, for whom, why is a DAO-managed series the right vehicle for it, and what would count as success or failure in the first year? Until those questions have written answers, Lyka DAO remains a name awaiting a purpose, and this page will keep saying so rather than filling the gap with invented detail. A thin honest page is more useful than a full invented one, because an invented page would mislead exactly the people — potential stewards, partners, and reviewers — whose trust the series would need most.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the Foundation's intended venture-studio model, each W3 series is DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents operate the series within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. That division is deliberate and non-negotiable in the model. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute. Every material decision — forming the series, opening a bank account, signing a partnership, spending money, or taking on an obligation — requires a named human's decision and signature.

The Foundation sits above the W3 series as a mission gate, not as an owner in the ordinary commercial sense: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and the Foundation's board must remain independent. Control in the intended model runs through written mission agreements and standards the series agree to, with counsel to design the exact structure. The Foundation's public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — is the standard every series is meant to be measured against, and the Windfall Clause framework is planned to apply across the W3 series: a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold (to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP) would flow to humanity through global public goods. The threshold, measurement, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today, and no series page states them as settled terms.

Governance of a series, once designated, would also need a written operating agreement, a named Steward Council, a compliance calendar, and separate accounts and records per series. None of that exists for this series yet, because the series itself does not exist yet as a legal entity. The governance described on this page is the intended model, not a description of a operating body. Anyone reading this page who wants to help shape that governance should contact the Foundation (see below) rather than assuming any of it is already in force.
