What this series is

Blockchain is Series 6 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. That sentence, together with the name itself, is very nearly everything verified that can be said about this series. Its name implies blockchain work — ledger, record-keeping, and decentralised coordination infrastructure — and this page describes that as a planned focus, because no chain, protocol, token, smart contract, application, partner, or publication for this series is verified in the files. This is a thin page on purpose: where there is almost nothing verified to say, the honest page says so plainly instead of filling the space with invention.

The domain the name implies is worth sketching carefully, because the sketch itself shows how much is still undefined. A series called simply “Blockchain” could be infrastructure for the other series: a shared ledger for records, votes, or provenance; a way of making DAO decisions auditable; or a platform on which other series build. Or it could be a venture in its own right, building blockchain applications for outside users. No verified document says which of those it is, and this page does not choose on the series’ behalf. The breadth of the name is itself information: unlike Cognition.X or Diagnostic.X, which name a field of application, this series names a technology, and a technology without a stated application is a tool waiting for a job.

What can be said without inventing anything is what a blockchain series in this family would have to get right if it were built. It would need to say which chain or chains it uses and why, what is recorded on-chain and what is deliberately kept off it, and who can read and write each kind of record. It would need to be clear about tokens: whether any token exists, what it does and does not confer, and whether it is a security — questions that need counsel’s written advice, not a landing page’s assurance. It would need to explain how its governance works in practice, since a DAO that cannot be audited or challenged is a slogan, not a governance system. None of those statements exists for this series today, and their absence is the current state of the series, stated here as such.

There is one neighbouring fact that needs to be handled explicitly, because it could easily confuse a reader. The internal list also contains three other blockchain-named series: Curable.Blockchain DAO (Series 17), BioDAO.blockchain DAO (Series 18), and Genome.Blockchain (Series 28). No verified document explains how Blockchain (Series 6) relates to any of them — whether it is intended as shared infrastructure beneath them, a separate effort that happens to share a technology, or a naming overlap that was never resolved. This page does not merge them, does not assign their domains to this series, and does not claim any shared work between them. If the relationship is ever defined in writing, this page should be updated to say what it is and cite the document. Until then, the four names stand side by side in the list, unexplained, and the general Blockchain series remains the least defined of the four.

Metaverse connection

No anchored metaverse fabric yet. Blockchain has no verified anchored world: no coordinates, no fabric JSON, and no metaverse location are recorded for this series in the materials reviewed. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one — it would send a reader, or a builder, to a place that does not exist.

What would be needed for a metaverse presence is the same sequence that the anchored series went through, and it has not started for this series: a decision about what a world for this series would be for, a fabric definition published at a checkable address, the assets that fabric references hosted at the paths it expects, and a verified render in Artemis. After that, a live metaverse-server endpoint would be needed before anything could be attached on RP1 — and zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series, so there is no RP1 presence to describe for this one either. A metaverse world for a blockchain series would most plausibly be a demonstration or governance space — a place to show how records, votes, or provenance are kept — rather than a world with its own separate purpose, and any such use would need to be labelled as a demonstration, not as a live system. No such design exists, so none is claimed.

Governance — planned, not yet operating

The planned governance model for the W3 series, as described on the Foundation site, is DAO-managed day-to-day work with human decision-making at every point that matters: agents may draft proposals, prepare research, and operate routine systems, but humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. A Steward Council of humans is intended to hold that gate. No Steward Council membership, no signed votes, no seated manager, and no operating agreement for Blockchain is verified in the files, so none is named here. Until those human roles are seated in writing, there is no one who can speak for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series — and this page does not do so.

What governance would have to answer first. Before Blockchain could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. Listing the questions is not the same as answering them, and this page deliberately does not pretend otherwise.

What would have to happen before this series could operate

A named series is not a formed series. For Blockchain to move from a line in an internal list to an operating venture, several distinct steps would each have to happen, in order, with documents to prove them. First, W3 LLC itself would need to be formed and evidenced by a filing that can be checked. Second, this series would need to be designated in writing as a series of that LLC, with its own name, purpose, and manager recorded. Third, the series would need its own operating rules, its own human stewards, and its own segregated accounting — so that its money, liabilities, and records are not mixed with any other series or with the Foundation. Fourth, any bank account, contract, hire, or public claim would need a named human signer acting under those written rules. Fifth, any fundraising would need its own counsel-approved documents, its own issuer, and its own securities compliance — it could not borrow the Foundation’s nonprofit status, and the Foundation, as filed, cannot offer equity or a SAFE. None of those steps is verified as done for Blockchain. This section is included so that a reader can see the gap plainly, rather than mistaking a polished landing page for a finished company.

That gap is also why this page carries no timeline. There is no launch date, no funding date, and no hiring date stated here, because none has been set in a verified document. A date on a landing page would look like a commitment; without a filing, a budget, and a human team behind it, it would be a decoration, not a plan. When a real date exists, it should appear here with the document behind it cited.
