What this series is

Locator.X is Series 8 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. Its name points at location: finding things, finding people, finding places, and knowing where something is in relation to everything else. That is the domain the name implies, and it is stated here as a planned focus, because beyond the name, the series number, and its anchored metaverse world, no product, service, dataset, partner, or publication for Locator.X is verified in the files. The page is therefore built from the name, the anchor, and the questions a location series would have to answer — not from a product that exists.

The domain the name implies is broad, and the breadth is worth naming because it shows how much is still undefined. Location work could mean navigation for people moving through a city, or finding an object, a vehicle, a building, or a service. It could mean mapping — building and keeping the record of where things are — or it could mean search: answering “where is” in a way that is fast, accurate, and respectful of privacy. It could serve travellers, delivery, emergency response, or simply someone trying to find a place they have never been. No verified document says which of those Locator.X intends, and this page does not choose on the series’ behalf. A location service that does everything is a slogan; a real one picks a user, a place, and a question, and does that well.

What can be said without inventing anything is what a location series would have to get right if it were built, and the first item is privacy. Location is personal information in a direct and sometimes sensitive sense: where a person is, where they have been, and where they go can reveal health, belief, relationships, and routine. A series in this domain would need, before it collected anything, a written answer to what location data it collects, from whom, with what consent, how precisely, how long it keeps it, who can see it, and how a person opts out or deletes it. It would also need to say what it does not track, and to mean it. No such answer exists for Locator.X today. The absence of a location-data policy is not a detail to be filled in later; for this domain, it is the first thing that would need to exist, and its absence is part of why this page describes the series as planned rather than operating.

The neighbouring domains help to sketch, without inventing, where such a series might matter. A city-scale environment (SmartCiti.X, Series 10) needs to know where its places, vehicles, and people are. Finding and using places (ParkingSmart.X, Series 12) is location work in a specific setting. Moving things to people (Get.Delivery DAO, Series 22) depends on knowing where both ends of a delivery are. Learning in a place (Cognition.X, Series 1) can be location-aware. Locator.X, if it is built, could sit beneath those neighbours as shared location infrastructure, or alongside them as its own service. That is a statement about the shape of the domain, not a claim that any collaboration exists: no joint work with any neighbouring series is verified.

Metaverse connection

Anchored world: Locator.X, 21.09705311564314, 142.69254864512746. This is one of the verified anchored worlds in the Foundation’s metaverse work: a Locator.X fabric with those coordinates. Its fabric JSON is live on GitHub Pages under the WorldForge-Browser my-fabric directory (fabric JSON), but the referenced assets behind it — on the order of two hundred GLB model files plus the map.wasm module — are not hosted there, so the world does not render fully in Artemis yet. That is the verified state: a published fabric definition, an anchored location, and an incomplete render because the assets it points to are not in place.

What that means in practice is that the world exists as a plan and a partial build, not as a place a visitor can enter today. The next step, if it is to be visitable, is specific: host the referenced GLB assets and map.wasm at the paths the fabric expects, then verify the render in Artemis. Until that is done, any image or description of the world should be labelled as a concept or a partial build, not as a live world. And the world is not on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1, no fabric or service for this series has ever been attached there, and the RP1 Attach wizard needs a live metaverse-server endpoint before any attach can happen.

For a location series, the anchored world is a fitting illustration of both the promise and the limit. A world with coordinates is, in a small way, already a location claim: this place, at these numbers, exists in the Foundation’s map. But a coordinate on its own is not a location service. It does not tell a visitor how to get there, what is nearby, or what is there when they arrive, and it says nothing about real-world places at all. The anchored fabric is a location for a future build, not evidence of a working locator, and this page keeps that distinction visible.

Governance — planned, not yet operating

The planned governance model for the W3 series, as described on the Foundation site, is DAO-managed day-to-day work with human decision-making at every point that matters: agents may draft proposals, prepare research, and operate routine systems, but humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. A Steward Council of humans is intended to hold that gate. No Steward Council membership, no signed votes, no seated manager, and no operating agreement for Locator.X is verified in the files, so none is named here. Until those human roles are seated in writing, there is no one who can speak for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series — and this page does not do so.

What governance would have to answer first. Before Locator.X could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. Listing the questions is not the same as answering them, and this page deliberately does not pretend otherwise.

What would have to happen before this series could operate

A named series is not a formed series. For Locator.X to move from a line in an internal list to an operating venture, several distinct steps would each have to happen, in order, with documents to prove them. First, W3 LLC itself would need to be formed and evidenced by a filing that can be checked. Second, this series would need to be designated in writing as a series of that LLC, with its own name, purpose, and manager recorded. Third, the series would need its own operating rules, its own human stewards, and its own segregated accounting — so that its money, liabilities, and records are not mixed with any other series or with the Foundation. Fourth, any bank account, contract, hire, or public claim would need a named human signer acting under those written rules. Fifth, any fundraising would need its own counsel-approved documents, its own issuer, and its own securities compliance — it could not borrow the Foundation’s nonprofit status, and the Foundation, as filed, cannot offer equity or a SAFE. None of those steps is verified as done for Locator.X. This section is included so that a reader can see the gap plainly, rather than mistaking a polished landing page for a finished company.

That gap is also why this page carries no timeline. There is no launch date, no funding date, and no hiring date stated here, because none has been set in a verified document. A date on a landing page would look like a commitment; without a filing, a budget, and a human team behind it, it would be a decoration, not a plan. When a real date exists, it should appear here with the document behind it cited.
