What this series is

Cognition.X is Series 1 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and it is the series whose purpose is the easiest to state from the Foundation’s own published words: learning. As stated on the Foundation site, Cognition.X is connected to Flow Zone education — the idea that learning works best when a person is engaged at the right level of challenge, neither bored nor overwhelmed, and that an agent-assisted environment can help hold a learner in that zone. That sentence is site copy, not a filing and not a product description, but it is the one published anchor this series has, and this page treats it as such: a stated direction, not a delivered system.

The domain the name implies is cognition itself: how people learn, remember, reason, and apply what they know, and how agentic systems might support those processes rather than replace them. In a learning context that could mean tutoring that adapts to a student, practice that adjusts as mastery grows, explanations pitched at the learner’s actual level, or tools that help a teacher see where a class is stuck. None of those tools is verified as built for this series. The honest statement is narrower: the series is planned around learning and understanding, and the specific products, audiences, and methods are still to be defined in writing by the humans who would steward it.

What makes Cognition.X first in the list is worth noting without over-reading it. Series numbers in the April 2026 list are an ordering in an internal printout, not a ranking of priority, funding, or readiness, and no document explains why Cognition.X is Series 1 rather than Series 10 or Series 33. It is reasonable to observe that learning sits upstream of almost everything else the Foundation plans — diagnostics, robotics, city systems, and research all depend on people who understand them — but that is an observation about the domain, not a claim about this series’ status. Cognition.X has no verified team, budget, curriculum, or learner count, and this page does not invent one.

A learning series also carries a particular responsibility that this page should name plainly. Education shapes what people believe and can do, so a series that builds learning tools would need to answer questions about accuracy, about whose knowledge it teaches, about how it handles a learner’s mistakes without shaming them, and about what data it keeps on a learner and who can see it. Those are governance questions, not features, and they belong to the human stewards, not to the agents that might one day operate the tools. No such governance document exists for Cognition.X today, which is itself a reason the series is described here as planned rather than operating.

Metaverse connection

Anchored world: Cognition.X Campus, 45.483127839031816, 18.572453605834877. This is one of the verified anchored worlds in the Foundation’s metaverse work: a Cognition.X Campus fabric with those coordinates. Its fabric JSON is live on GitHub Pages under the WorldForge-Browser my-fabric directory (fabric JSON), but the referenced assets behind it — on the order of two hundred GLB model files plus the map.wasm module — are not hosted there, so the campus does not render fully in Artemis yet. That is the verified state: a published fabric definition, an anchored location, and an incomplete render because the assets it points to are not in place.

What that means in practice is that the campus exists as a plan and a partial build, not as a place a visitor can walk through today. The next step, if the campus is to be visitable, is unglamorous and specific: host the referenced GLB assets and map.wasm at the paths the fabric expects, then verify the render in Artemis. Until that is done, any image or description of the campus should be labelled as a concept or a partial build, not as a live world. And the campus is not on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1, no fabric or service for this series has ever been attached there, and the RP1 Attach wizard needs a live metaverse-server endpoint before any attach can happen.

The connection between the campus and the series’ learning purpose is easy to imagine and important not to overstate. A campus in a metaverse could host classes, practice spaces, and demonstrations that a flat web page cannot — that is the reason a learning series would want a world at all. But imagining a use is not the same as having built it, and this page keeps the two apart: the learning purpose is stated site copy, the campus is an anchored but incompletely rendered fabric, and the combination of the two into a working Flow Zone classroom has not been verified.

What is planned

Everything else is planned, not existing: the formation of W3 LLC and the designation of this series; a human Steward Council and operating rules; learning products or curricula; any learners, teachers, or partner institutions; any funding; and a fully rendering campus on a live metaverse service. Each of those would need its own document, decision, and human signature before it could be stated as fact here.

Between the two columns sits the work itself. The most useful next step for Cognition.X would not be a bigger claim but a smaller, checkable one: a written statement of scope from a named human steward, saying who the first learners would be and what the first learning tool would do. Until that document exists, the honest size of this series is exactly what the left-hand card says — a name, a direction, and a partial campus — and this page is content to leave it at that.

Governance — planned, not yet operating

The planned governance model for the W3 series, as described on the Foundation site, is DAO-managed day-to-day work with human decision-making at every point that matters: agents may draft proposals, prepare research, and operate routine systems, but humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. A Steward Council of humans is intended to hold that gate. No Steward Council membership, no signed votes, no seated manager, and no operating agreement for Cognition.X is verified in the files, so none is named here. Until those human roles are seated in writing, there is no one who can speak for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series — and this page does not do so.

What governance would have to answer first. Before Cognition.X could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. Listing the questions is not the same as answering them, and this page deliberately does not pretend otherwise.

What would have to happen before this series could operate

A named series is not a formed series. For Cognition.X to move from a line in an internal list to an operating venture, several distinct steps would each have to happen, in order, with documents to prove them. First, W3 LLC itself would need to be formed and evidenced by a filing that can be checked. Second, this series would need to be designated in writing as a series of that LLC, with its own name, purpose, and manager recorded. Third, the series would need its own operating rules, its own human stewards, and its own segregated accounting — so that its money, liabilities, and records are not mixed with any other series or with the Foundation. Fourth, any bank account, contract, hire, or public claim would need a named human signer acting under those written rules. Fifth, any fundraising would need its own counsel-approved documents, its own issuer, and its own securities compliance — it could not borrow the Foundation’s nonprofit status, and the Foundation, as filed, cannot offer equity or a SAFE. None of those steps is verified as done for Cognition.X. This section is included so that a reader can see the gap plainly, rather than mistaking a polished landing page for a finished company.

That gap is also why this page carries no timeline. There is no launch date, no funding date, and no hiring date stated here, because none has been set in a verified document. A date on a landing page would look like a commitment; without a filing, a budget, and a human team behind it, it would be a decoration, not a plan. When a real date exists, it should appear here with the document behind it cited.

Flow Zone tutor scope paper

A one-page written scope for an adaptive tutoring approach that holds a learner at the right level of challenge, neither bored nor overwhelmed, with agents assisting rather than replacing the teacher. The paper would name the first learners, the first subject area, and what the tutor would and would not do. It would be written for human stewards to approve before any tool is built.

What success would look like: A scope paper exists that a named human steward has read and approved, naming the first learners and the first learning tool.

Teacher view for stuck points

A proposal for a tool that helps a teacher see where a class is stuck, so the teacher can step in with the right help at the right time. The proposal would cover what the teacher sees, what stays private to the learner, and how the view is used in a real lesson. No such tool is verified as built for this series.

What success would look like: A written walkthrough of the teacher view has been reviewed by a named teacher, who confirms it would help in a real lesson.

Practice that adjusts with mastery

A proposal for practice sets that grow harder as a learner shows mastery and ease off when a learner struggles, keeping the learner in the Flow Zone. The proposal would state the mastery rules in plain language so a learner and a teacher can both understand them. It would be a design first, not a product claim.

What success would look like: A small practice set exists with written mastery rules, reviewed by a named teacher for fairness and clarity.
