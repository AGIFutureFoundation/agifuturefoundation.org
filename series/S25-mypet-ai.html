What this series is

Mypet.AI is W3 Series 25. The name is the whole of the verified description: the April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Mypet.AI — Series 25” and gives no further description, no product, no team, no partner, and no technical scope. This page therefore describes only a planned focus, drawn from what the name implies, and labels it as planned throughout. Nothing here describes a product that exists, a service that is offered, or a company that has been formed.

The planned focus implied by the name is pet-related artificial intelligence: AI built around companion animals and the people who care for them. That is a wide lane, and the honest thing to say is that its boundaries have not been set. A pet-focused AI series could, in principle, cover companion and care tools for pet owners; support for veterinary and shelter work; or pet-related content and community. Each of those is a different product, a different regulatory posture, and a different set of partners, and none of them has been chosen. The first real decision for this series — before any build, any hire, or any spend — would be to write down, in one page, which of those lanes it is in and which it is not, and to have a human Steward Council approve that scope.

What can be said with confidence is what the series would have to get right if it ever operates in this lane. Anything touching animal health would need to stay firmly on the support side of the line: general information and organisation, not diagnosis or treatment, with licensed veterinarians as the decision-makers and with clear escalation to a vet for anything medical. Anything touching pet owners' data — photos, locations, health notes — would need consent-first handling, with sharing off by default and a plain-language explanation of what is stored and why. And anything presented as companionship, for the pet or the owner, would need to be honest about what it is: software, not a substitute for human or veterinary care. None of these are policies in force; they are the standards the series would be expected to meet before it could claim to be aligned with the Foundation's public-benefit mission.

The pet lane also illustrates why the Foundation's stage-gate model matters. In the intended venture-studio model, a series moves through stages — idea, validate, build, launch, operate and scale, exit or wind down — and only passes a gate when a human review says it has. Mypet.AI is at stage zero: an idea, a name on a list. Validation would mean talking to real pet owners, shelters, and veterinarians and learning whether there is a problem worth solving that this series is well placed to solve; it would not mean building first and finding users later. That discipline is the difference between a series and a slide.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the Foundation's intended venture-studio model, each W3 series is DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents operate the series within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. That division is deliberate and non-negotiable in the model. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute. Every material decision — forming the series, opening a bank account, signing a partnership, spending money, or taking on an obligation — requires a named human's decision and signature.

The Foundation sits above the W3 series as a mission gate, not as an owner in the ordinary commercial sense: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and the Foundation's board must remain independent. Control in the intended model runs through written mission agreements and standards the series agree to, with counsel to design the exact structure. The Foundation's public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — is the standard every series is meant to be measured against, and the Windfall Clause framework is planned to apply across the W3 series: a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold (to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP) would flow to humanity through global public goods. The threshold, measurement, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today, and no series page states them as settled terms.

Governance of a series, once designated, would also need a written operating agreement, a named Steward Council, a compliance calendar, and separate accounts and records per series. None of that exists for this series yet, because the series itself does not exist yet as a legal entity. The governance described on this page is the intended model, not a description of a operating body. Anyone reading this page who wants to help shape that governance should contact the Foundation (see below) rather than assuming any of it is already in force.

Open questions this series must answer before it can proceed

  1. Which lane is Mypet.AI in — owner tools, veterinary support, shelter support, or something else — and which lanes is it explicitly not in?
  2. Who is the human steward accountable for animal-welfare and data decisions, and what veterinary advice has been taken before any health-adjacent claim is made?
  3. What would validation look like: which pet owners, shelters, or veterinarians would need to say this is worth building before any build begins?

These are not rhetorical questions. Each one needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series passes its first stage gate. If you can help answer one of them, the contact below is the route.
