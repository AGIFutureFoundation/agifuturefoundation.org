What this series is

Curable.Blockchain DAO is W3 Series S17, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. The name itself is the most that the files reviewed say about it: two ideas joined together — "Curable," pointing toward health conditions that can be treated or cured, and "Blockchain," pointing toward a shared, tamper-evident record — governed as a DAO. Everything beyond that on this page is a description of a planned focus implied by the name, clearly marked as such, not a report of work already done.

The planned focus, as the name implies it, would be health work where a trustworthy shared record matters: a condition is diagnosed, a treatment is given, an outcome is observed, and each step is recorded in a way that the patient, the clinician, and any later reviewer can check without having to take any single party's word for it. That is the kind of problem a blockchain record is often proposed for — not because a ledger treats anyone, but because treatment only helps the next patient if the record of what was tried and what happened survives intact, in order, and attributable to the people and institutions involved.

It helps to say plainly what "curable" does and does not promise here. It does not promise that any particular disease is curable, and it does not promise that this series has a treatment, a drug, a device, or a clinic. No condition, no therapy, no trial, no clinician, and no patient group is named in the files reviewed for this series. The word "curable" in the name is an orientation — toward conditions where treatment can change the outcome, rather than conditions where only monitoring or comfort is possible — and the series, if it is ever built, would need to name its first condition, in writing, before any of the rest could be judged.

The "Blockchain" half of the name is equally a direction, not a system. A blockchain record for health work would need to answer questions this page cannot answer yet: who writes to the record, who can read it, how a patient consents and withdraws consent, how a mistaken entry is corrected without rewriting history, and how the record complies with health privacy law in every jurisdiction where it operates. Those are design decisions for a future governing document, not details that can be filled in by enthusiasm. A ledger that nobody trusts, nobody can correct, or nobody is allowed to read is worse than no ledger at all.

The "DAO" half of the name states the intended governance shape: day-to-day operation by agents within human-set limits, with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council, as described in the "How it fits" section below. That model is planned for the whole W3 programme. It is not operating for this series today, and no DAO vote, proposal, treasury, or token for this series is claimed anywhere on this page.

Readers should therefore treat this page as a placeholder with a direction, not a prospectus. It says: here is a name, here is what the name points toward, here is what is not yet known, and here is what would have to happen before the name becomes a working series. That is the most that honesty allows at this stage, and it is deliberately more useful than a page full of invented detail that would have to be retracted later.

Metaverse connection

This series has no anchored metaverse fabric yet. That sentence is the whole of what can honestly be said about its metaverse connection today: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are in the files reviewed for this series, and this page invents none of them.

Zero worlds are live on RP1. That is the position for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to this series, and it should not be read as a failure of this series in particular — it is simply where the programme stands. A series page that claimed a live world, a set of coordinates, or a fabric URL without a file to cite would be inventing a fact, and this page will not do that.

What would be needed for this series to gain a metaverse presence is concrete and checkable: a chosen location with published coordinates, a fabric JSON file describing the world, hosted assets (models, textures, and any interactive elements) that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach that can be opened and verified by a reviewer. Until all four exist and can be cited, the correct statement remains "no anchored metaverse fabric yet," and any future update to this section should name the coordinates, link the fabric file, and give the date the RP1 attach was verified — not merely assert that a world exists.

Whether this series even needs a metaverse presence is an open question, not a settled one. Some series are naturally spatial — a training ground, a clinic, a city, a delivery network — and some are naturally not. The decision should be made series by series, in writing, with a reason: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the series works in documents, code, and human conversation and does not need a world. Either answer is defensible. What is not defensible is assuming a world exists because the programme talks about worlds in general.

For this series in particular, a metaverse presence would only make sense if there were a spatial task for it — for example, a shared space where clinicians, researchers, and patients could meet, review a case, or walk through a treatment pathway together. No such space exists, no such use has been agreed, and no coordinates or fabric have been produced. If a spatial use is ever agreed, it should be described first in words, with the people it serves named, and only then built — a world built before its purpose is written down tends to become a demonstration rather than a tool.

What exists today vs. what is planned

What exists today

  • A name and a series number (S17) in the April 2026 internal W3 series list.
  • A planned focus implied by the name: curable health work plus blockchain record-keeping, governed as a DAO.
  • The Foundation's filed nonprofit status and its intended W3 governance model, described on this page.
  • No formed company, no designation, no bank account, no EIN, no staff, no trial, no treatment, and no patient group for this series.

What is planned

  • A written mission naming the first condition or class of conditions, and what "curable" means for it.
  • A record design: who writes, who reads, how consent and correction work, and how privacy law is met.
  • A Steward Council and named human stewards, with decision limits in writing.
  • A metaverse decision: anchor a fabric with coordinates and a verifiable RP1 attach, or record in writing that no world is needed.
  • Any clinical or research work only under qualified human direction, with ethics review and counsel, before anything touches a patient.

How it fits

This series sits in the W3 programme's health and life-sciences group, alongside its closest neighbours. Series S4, Curable.X, shares the "curable" orientation and would be the first place to check for overlap: if S4 is the health venture and S17 is its blockchain-record DAO, the two would need a written agreement saying which does what, so that neither duplicates the other nor leaves a gap between them. Series S6, Blockchain, is the programme's general blockchain series, and S17 would need to say whether it builds on S6's work, uses its own ledger, or both — again, in writing, before building.

Other related series include S3 Diagnostic.X (diagnosis precedes any claim of curability), S14 AskDoc.X (clinical questions and answers), S5 BioPharma.Go (therapies, if any are ever involved), S18 BioDAO.blockchain DAO (the neighbouring blockchain DAO in the life sciences), and S28 Genome.Blockchain (genomic records, where they bear on a curable condition). None of those relationships is a partnership today. Each is a conversation to be had, and an agreement to be written, if and when the series involved become active. Listing them here is a way of preventing duplicate work later, not a claim that joint work is happening now.

In tier terms, this series would belong to the commercial engine tier — the W3 Wyoming series network — under the Foundation's mission gate at the apex. The Foundation does not own the series in the way a parent company owns a subsidiary, and this page does not claim that it does. The relationship, if it ever becomes real, would be set out in a written mission agreement, and the Foundation board would remain independent, as a California nonprofit board must.

Under the intended structure described in the Foundation's planning documents, a W3 series would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. That is a plan, not a description of something operating today. No Steward Council for this series is named in the files reviewed, no operating agreement for this series is in the files reviewed, and no agent has authority to sign for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series or the Foundation.

The boundaries in that intended model are deliberately narrow. Agents would prepare work, draft documents, keep records, and coordinate — they would not enact doctrine or charters, hire or fire, offer securities, sign contracts, or move funds. Humans would execute every step that creates a legal or financial obligation. If this series ever moves from planned to active, those boundaries would need to be written down first, in the series' own governing documents, and approved by the Foundation board and by counsel — not assumed from this page.

Governance also means the Foundation's role stays a mission gate, not ownership in the ordinary commercial sense. The Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, and its board must remain independent. Control over a series, if any is ever exercised, would be through written mission agreements, not through treating a nonprofit as a subsidiary of a for-profit head or a series as a pocket of the Foundation. That distinction matters, and it is why this page keeps saying "intended" and "planned" instead of describing a live reporting line.

What would need to happen next

The list below is the same honest checklist that applies to every planned W3 series: it is the work that would have to be done, in writing, before this series could be described as anything more than planned. It is not a roadmap with dates, and it is not a promise that any of it will happen.

  1. Confirm the series exists in law. A Wyoming SOS search for W3 LLC, and a written per-series designation for this series, would have to be produced. Until then, the series name is a name on an internal list, not a legal entity.
  2. Write the focus down. A one-page mission statement for this series, approved in writing by the Foundation board, stating what it will and will not do — so that later work can be checked against it instead of drifting.
  3. Name the humans. A Steward Council and a named human steward, with their roles, limits, and decision process written down. No series should operate on unnamed stewards.
  4. Settle the metaverse question. Either anchor a fabric with coordinates, a fabric JSON file, and hosted assets, and attach it to RP1 — or record in writing that this series has no metaverse presence and does not need one. Either answer is acceptable; an unexamined assumption is not.
  5. Separate money from the start. If the series ever holds funds, it would need its own bank account and ledger, its own EIN where required, and a written agreement with the Foundation. No pooling, no shared pot, no informal transfers.
  6. Publish the record. Designation, mission, stewards, and any metaverse anchor should be published or filed where a reviewer can check them, so that the next version of this page can replace "planned" with a dated, sourced fact.

None of those steps has a date on this page, because no date has been set in the files reviewed. A step with no date is a step still to be scheduled, not a step that is late — and this page will not invent a timeline to make the plan look further along than it is.
