What this series is

Gonstead.X is W3 Series S13 in the Foundation's intended W3 network. Its name is the only verified detail about it beyond its number and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. This page therefore describes it only as a planned series whose focus, as the name implies, would be the Gonstead approach to chiropractic care: the Gonstead method of chiropractic assessment and adjustment, and the education, tools, and practice support built around it. That is a description of a domain, not a claim that any clinic, practitioner, patient, product, or training programme exists today under this name.

The name points at a specific, named tradition inside chiropractic care, and that specificity carries obligations this page should state plainly. The Gonstead approach is a method used by licensed chiropractors; it is not a generic wellness label, and a series carrying its name would need to be clear about what it is and what it is not. It would need qualified, licensed human clinical leadership from the start, and it would need to respect the scope of practice, licensing, and regulation that govern chiropractic care in every place it operated. Agents would not diagnose, treat, adjust, or advise patients; agents would support the human-led work — education, records, scheduling, research support — within limits set by licensed humans. Nothing on this page claims a treatment, a health outcome, a patient result, or a clinical endorsement for this series, and no such claim should be read into the name.

The questions a future steward would need to answer are therefore clinical and regulatory before they are commercial. What exactly would Gonstead.X offer — practitioner education, assessment tools, practice support, patient education, or something else — and under whose licence and supervision? Which jurisdiction's rules would govern it, and who would be accountable when those rules are tested? How would it handle the boundary between education and clinical advice, so that no reader mistakes general information for a diagnosis or a treatment recommendation for their own condition? What health data would it hold, if any, and under what consent, security, and retention rules? How would it avoid presenting the Gonstead method, or any chiropractic method, as a cure or a guaranteed outcome? None of those questions has a verified answer today. Listing them is a record of the work that remains before this planned series could become a real one.

One further boundary deserves emphasis. A health-related series must not present itself as diagnosing or treating any condition, and this page does not do so. If Gonstead.X is ever formed, its public materials would need to carry that boundary in plain language on every page that touches health, and its clinical content would need review by licensed professionals before publication. The planned focus described here — the Gonstead approach, supported by agents under human clinical governance — is a direction for future stewards to consider, with counsel and clinical review, not a service a reader can use today.

The one verified source document: an expired Chinese utility-model patent

Since this page was first built, one source document for this series has been verified, and it is recorded here at exactly the strength the verification supports. VERIFIED (source document uploaded to the Foundation’s files: a 13-page patent PDF, “Upper body positioning device for non-operative spinal treatment apparatus”; full text cross-checked against the Google Patents record, checked 2026-11-08; research brief in the network’s bio-research files, dated 2026-11-07): Chinese utility-model patent CN 204233258 U, for a hand-held remote control device for the bed body of non-surgical spinal treatment equipment. Filed 2014-10-22; granted and published 2015-04-01; inventor Ren Song; applicant and patentee Anhui Ruide Exxon Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., of Hefei, Anhui, China (originally Anhui Ryzur Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., name changed 2015-09-30); IPC class A61F 5/042; five claims. Later cited by CN108451684A/B and CN108451683A (2018).

What the patent covers is narrower than its placement in this series might suggest, and the limits are stated in the same passage. It covers remote, wired control of a split-bed traction and spinal-decompression table: bed translation and separation, three-axis positioning of upper-body (armpit) supports, lumbar-airbag inflation, and a flat-to-upright tilt, all from one physical-button handset. It does not cover any chiropractic adjustment technique, any Gonstead assessment or adjustment method, any diagnostic function, any sensor or feedback loop, any treatment protocol, or any outcome — and the patent never mentions Gonstead. The connection to this series is by programme placement (Series 13, device design and technique documentation), not by the patent’s content. The conditions and physiological effects listed in the patent’s background are the patent’s own assertions in a utility-model specification; Chinese utility models are granted without substantive examination, and those assertions are not peer-reviewed evidence and are not repeated here as findings. No efficacy or treatment claim is made for the device, and none may be made for this series on the basis of this document.

Its legal status is also verified, and it is not a live right. VERIFIED (Google Patents legal-status field and legal events, checked 2026-11-08): the patent is Expired — Fee Related; termination of the patent right for non-payment of the annual fee was recorded 2022-01-04 with a termination date of 2020-10-22, ahead of its anticipated expiration of 2024-10-22 (the ten-year utility-model term from filing). Expiry is recorded here as a fact about the document, not as a legal conclusion: it is not a freedom-to-operate opinion, related rights were not checked, the record covers China only, and any device built on this design would still need its own regulatory clearance and counsel’s confirmation before any commercial use. The patentee is a third party: nothing here implies any assignment, licence, endorsement, partnership, or agreement between the patentee and this series or the Foundation, and none is evidenced.

What this document changes for the series is a research agenda, stated as planned work and nothing more: device design using the expired patent as a prior-art baseline for a modern treatment-table positioning reference design; documentation of the Gonstead method as published in the chiropractic literature, reviewed by licensed practitioners before any publication; a cited, systematic evidence review for traction and decompression tables and for table-positioning ergonomics and safety, reporting null and negative results alongside positive ones; and regulatory and scope mapping before anything is built for use. No prototype exists, no licensed clinical lead has been appointed, no evidence review has been completed, and no regulatory filing has been made. Chiropractic is a regulated, licensed practice: this series’ planned work is device design, technique documentation, and evidence review only, under licensed human leadership, and agents do not diagnose, treat, adjust, or advise patients.

What is planned

The plan, as far as it can honestly be stated today, is to keep this series as a named, planned focus until licensed human clinical leadership, with counsel, decides whether it should be formed at all — and if so, what exactly it would do and under what clinical oversight.

  • Decide, in writing, whether Gonstead.X should be formed, and if so, whether it would offer practitioner education, assessment support, practice support, patient education, or some combination — and which of those it would refuse.
  • Appoint licensed human clinical leadership and define the scope-of-practice, licensing, and regulatory rules that would govern every activity, before any clinical or patient-facing work is considered.
  • Set the boundary between education and clinical advice in writing, including what the series will not claim, will not diagnose, and will not treat.
  • Commission a privacy and consent design for any health data the series would hold, before any data is collected, and choose a governance form — series designation, operating agreement, and Steward Council appointments — with counsel, if formation is approved.
  • Only then, decide whether a metaverse presence would serve the series' practitioners or learners, and build it to the four-part standard described in the metaverse section above.

Each step would need its own document, decision, and, where required, counsel's review. None is presented here as done, scheduled, or promised by a date, because no date is verified.

How it fits

Tier. This series is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation's planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series, including this one, as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series' designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain this page can cite, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series' future.

Related series. The nearest neighbours in the numbering are Series S12 (ParkingSmart.X) and Series S14 (AskDoc.X), and the wider group on this site runs from S09 through S16. Numbering proximity is the only relationship this page claims: the series in this group share a place in the same internal list and the same intended W3 structure, and no operational partnership, shared customer, shared product, shared clinic, or shared funding between this series and any other series is claimed. Any future relationship with AskDoc.X (Series S14), whose name also touches health, would need its own written agreement and would be cited here only then; the two names' shared health domain does not by itself create a partnership.

Governance for this series, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be DAO-managed: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed series is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for this series. There is no Steward Council roster, no operating agreement, and no published set of agent limits for this series to point to today. Naming the intended model here is a statement of intent about how the series would be run if it were formed — it is not evidence that the model is already running. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, so control would run only through those written agreements, designed with counsel.

Three practical consequences follow. First, no agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one. Second, any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement for this series would need its own legal documents, its own issuer, and counsel review — the series name alone authorises nothing. Third, the public record for this series should grow only as real documents are created: a designation, an operating agreement, a council appointment, a signed agreement. Until then, the honest public record is this page's status banner.
