What this series is

AskDoc.X is W3 Series S14 in the Foundation's intended W3 network. Its name is the only verified detail about it beyond its number and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. This page therefore describes it only as a planned series whose focus, as the name implies, would be health questions and health information: a place where a person could ask a health question, receive general information, and be guided toward appropriate care. That is a description of a domain, not a claim that any service, clinician, patient, answer, or care pathway exists today under this name.

The planned focus needs its most important boundary stated first, before any description of what the series might do. AskDoc.X would not diagnose, would not treat, and would not replace a clinician. A health question service that blurred that line would be dangerous, and this page does not blur it. General health information, screening support, and referral guidance are different things from diagnosis and treatment, and a future series under this name would need to keep that difference visible on every page, in every answer, and in every interaction — including a clear route to urgent help when a person's situation requires it. Agents would support the work — organising information, drafting general educational material for human review, helping a person find a clinician or a service — within limits set by licensed humans. Agents would not give a person a diagnosis, a treatment plan, or a personal medical recommendation.

The questions a future steward would need to answer follow directly from that boundary. What exactly would AskDoc.X offer — general health education, help understanding a clinician's explanation, help finding care, or something else — and what would it refuse to answer? Who would write and review its health content, under whose licence and supervision, and how would errors be caught and corrected? How would it handle urgent situations, and what would it tell a person who needs help now rather than information? What health data would it hold, if any, and under what consent, security, and retention rules — and how would a person see, correct, or delete what it holds about them? How would it serve people in different languages, with different literacy, and with different access to care, without overstating what it can do for any of them? None of those questions has a verified answer today, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

It is also worth stating what AskDoc.X is not. It is not the Foundation's AURA Maternal project, which is a separate, screening-support and referral project with its own pages and its own scope, and nothing from that project — its modules, its data, its partners, or its status — is claimed for AskDoc.X here. The two projects must stay separate in the public record, and any future relationship between them would need its own written agreement and would be cited here only then. AskDoc.X, on the evidence available today, is a named, planned focus in health information and care navigation, with no verified service to report and no health claim to make.

The biology in depth

AskDoc.X is, by its name, a place where health questions are asked and answered. Answering health questions is a distinct field from diagnosing or treating: it is health information — explaining a condition, a test, or a treatment in plain language, pointing to sources, and stating what is known and what is not. The hard parts are sourcing, limits, and handover: an answer must say where its information comes from, must not present general information as personal medical advice, must recognise when a question needs a clinician rather than a page, and must be checked by qualified humans rather than left to an unsupervised model. A wrong health answer is not a minor error, and the design of any such service has to start from that fact.

Longevity questions would fall in the same frame: research into healthy ageing is a live field with open questions, and any answer about it would have to present the evidence and its limits together, never as an established outcome. This page claims no longevity outcome or therapy, because none is verified for this series.

What is verified for this series is an absence, stated plainly. AskDoc.X is S14, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. A name search over all 90 public repositories in the Foundation account, checked 2026-11-06/07, found no repository for this series — no code, no dataset, no question-answering system, no clinician review record. No model, dataset, accuracy figure, partnership, or timeline is verified for AskDoc.X, and none is claimed here. This series is not diagnosis or treatment, and this page does not present it as either.

Planned work — a written mission naming the health-information scope and its limits, a sourcing and review design with qualified human oversight, a Steward Council, and a handover route to clinicians — is described in the sections above as planned, not existing.
