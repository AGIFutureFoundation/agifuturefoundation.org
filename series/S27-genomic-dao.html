What this series is

Genomic DAO is W3 Series 27. The April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Genomic DAO — Series 27” and gives no further description, so everything about its scope on this page is a planned focus drawn from what the name implies, labelled as planned. What the name implies is a serious and narrow lane: the governance of genomic data — who consents to its use, under what terms, for what purposes, and with what benefit returning to the people the data came from.

Genomic data is unlike most data a series might handle, and the planned focus has to start from that fact. A genome identifies a person and their relatives; it cannot be changed if it leaks; and its interpretation changes as science advances, so consent given for one use may not cover a use invented five years later. A DAO in this lane would therefore be, first and foremost, a consent and stewardship body, not a data marketplace. Its job, as planned, would be to hold the line on three questions: did the person whose data this is agree to this specific use, can that agreement be withdrawn, and does the benefit of the use flow back to the people and communities who contributed? Any design that cannot answer those three questions in plain language would fail the Foundation's public-benefit standard before it started.

The companion series Genome.Blockchain (Series 28) is the natural counterpart: where Genomic DAO would govern the decisions, Genome.Blockchain would hold the verifiable record of them. That division — decisions in one series, records in another — is a planned separation of powers, not an operating arrangement, and it would need a written agreement between the two series defining exactly what each one holds, who can see what, and what happens when they disagree. The neighbouring life-sciences series — BioDAO.blockchain (Series 18), Curable.Blockchain (Series 17), and Diagnostic.X (Series 3) — mark the wider field this series would work inside: biology, cures, and diagnostics. Genomic DAO's distinct contribution, as planned, is not doing genomics itself but governing how genomic data is used by others.

Because the stakes are high, the bar for this series is higher than for most. Before any data is handled, the series would need counsel on the applicable law in every jurisdiction it touches, a written consent architecture with granular, revocable permissions, a data-minimisation rule (hold the least data needed, for the shortest time needed), and a named human accountable for breaches and for saying no to uses that fail the consent test. It would also need a clear statement that it does not diagnose, treat, or advise individuals on the basis of their genome: screening support and research governance are the planned lane, and clinical claims are outside it. None of these protections exists today. They are requirements the series would have to satisfy to earn the right to operate, and this page lists them so that a future reviewer can check whether they were met — not to imply that they already have been.

Validation for Genomic DAO would look different from validation for a consumer product. It would not be measured first in users or volume, but in trust: would people with genomic data to share, and researchers who need it, accept this DAO as a fair steward? That is a question that can only be answered by talking to both sides — patient communities, research institutions, and privacy experts — and by publishing the governance rules for public comment before any data flows. A DAO that launches its rules and its data handling at the same time has the order backwards. The planned sequence is rules first, published and reviewed; data second, with consent; and benefit-sharing terms third, agreed before value is created rather than negotiated after it.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the Foundation's intended venture-studio model, each W3 series is DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents operate the series within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. That division is deliberate and non-negotiable in the model. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute. Every material decision — forming the series, opening a bank account, signing a partnership, spending money, or taking on an obligation — requires a named human's decision and signature.

The Foundation sits above the W3 series as a mission gate, not as an owner in the ordinary commercial sense: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and the Foundation's board must remain independent. Control in the intended model runs through written mission agreements and standards the series agree to, with counsel to design the exact structure. The Foundation's public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — is the standard every series is meant to be measured against, and the Windfall Clause framework is planned to apply across the W3 series: a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold (to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP) would flow to humanity through global public goods. The threshold, measurement, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today, and no series page states them as settled terms.

Governance of a series, once designated, would also need a written operating agreement, a named Steward Council, a compliance calendar, and separate accounts and records per series. None of that exists for this series yet, because the series itself does not exist yet as a legal entity. The governance described on this page is the intended model, not a description of a operating body. Anyone reading this page who wants to help shape that governance should contact the Foundation (see below) rather than assuming any of it is already in force.

The biology in depth

Genomics is the reading and interpretation of genomes: the complete DNA of an organism, and how variation in that DNA relates to traits, disease risk, and response to treatment. The field runs from sequencing — producing the raw letters — through analysis — finding variants, genes, and pathways — to interpretation, where a finding has to be weighed for evidence, uncertainty, and clinical meaning. Proteomics, the study of the proteins a genome produces, and drug development, where a genomic finding becomes a therapeutic target, sit alongside it. Genomic data is identifiable by nature: a genome identifies its owner, so consent, access control, and the right to withdraw are central to any honest genomic programme, and they have to be designed before data is collected, not after.

Longevity appears in this field as a research direction: the genomic study of ageing, and whether variation in ageing-related genes points to interventions. The platform repository below lists longevity among its claimed swarm categories. That is a claimed category in a README, not verified built capacity, and this page claims no longevity outcome, therapy, or timeline. Any future longevity work would be presented as research, with its evidence and its limits stated together.
