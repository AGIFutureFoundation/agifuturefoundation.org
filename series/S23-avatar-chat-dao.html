What this series is

Avatar.Chat DAO is W3 Series S23, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. The name gives a clear planned focus in outline: conversation — chat — carried on through avatars, the visible, embodied representations of the people (or agents) speaking. As with every series in this group, the name is the most the files reviewed say about it, and everything beyond the name on this page is a description of a planned focus implied by the name, marked as such.

The planned focus, as the name implies it, would be a place and a set of rules for talking through avatars: who you appear as, how you are identified, what you can say, and what happens to the conversation afterwards. An avatar is not a costume that hides who is speaking; at its best, it is a representation that carries identity, expression, and presence into a space where bodies cannot go. A chat DAO for avatars would therefore be, in principle, a community with a visible form — and the hard questions are the community questions, not the graphics questions: who may speak, as whom, and under what rules.

Those questions are worth stating, because they are the ones a future governing document would have to answer before anyone could usefully join. Can one person have many avatars, and can one avatar be used by many people? How is identity verified, and what happens when an avatar is used to impersonate someone? What is recorded, who can read the record, and can a participant delete what they said? What happens when a conversation becomes abusive, deceptive, or harmful — who moderates, by what process, and with what appeal? None of those questions is answered in the files reviewed, and this page does not answer them by invention. A chat space without written answers to them is a space waiting for its first dispute to define it, badly.

The DAO element is, for this series, unusually direct: the community is the product, so the governance of the community is the governance of the series. In the intended programme-wide model, agents would operate day-to-day within human-set limits, and humans would decide, sign, and move funds through a Steward Council. For a chat DAO, the human decisions include the moderation rules, the identity rules, and the record rules — and they would need to be written down and published before the doors open, so that anyone joining knows the rules they are joining under. No community, no members, no moderators, no conversations, and no avatars are claimed for this series anywhere on this page, because none exists in the files reviewed.

It is also worth distinguishing this series from a generic chatbot or a messaging app. A chatbot answers questions; a messaging app carries messages between named people. An avatar chat DAO, as the name implies it, would be a shared space where presence matters — where who you appear as, and how you appear, is part of the conversation.

Until then, the honest description is a planned focus: conversation through avatars, governed as a DAO, with its identity, moderation, and record rules still to be written. Anyone who wants to help write those rules, or who has a community that needs such a space, should use the contact section below — with a named human behind the proposal, and a specific use in mind, rather than a general enthusiasm for avatars.

Metaverse connection

This series has no anchored metaverse fabric yet. That sentence is the whole of what can honestly be said about its metaverse connection today: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are in the files reviewed for this series, and this page invents none of them.

Zero worlds are live on RP1. That is the position for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to this series, and it should not be read as a failure of this series in particular — it is simply where the programme stands. A series page that claimed a live world, a set of coordinates, or a fabric URL without a file to cite would be inventing a fact, and this page will not do that.

What would be needed for this series to gain a metaverse presence is concrete and checkable: a chosen location with published coordinates, a fabric JSON file describing the world, hosted assets (models, textures, and any interactive elements) that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach that can be opened and verified by a reviewer. Until all four exist and can be cited, the correct statement remains "no anchored metaverse fabric yet," and any future update to this section should name the coordinates, link the fabric file, and give the date the RP1 attach was verified — not merely assert that a world exists.

Whether this series even needs a metaverse presence is an open question, not a settled one. Some series are naturally spatial — a training ground, a clinic, a city, a delivery network — and some are naturally not. The decision should be made series by series, in writing, with a reason: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the series works in documents, code, and human conversation and does not need a world. Either answer is defensible. What is not defensible is assuming a world exists because the programme talks about worlds in general.

For this series in particular, a metaverse presence is the most natural of any in this group — avatars are, by definition, embodied presences in a shared space, and a chat DAO without a space for its avatars to stand in would be a chat DAO in name only. That makes the absence of a fabric here worth stating clearly, rather than glossing over: this series has no anchored metaverse fabric yet, no coordinates, no fabric JSON, no hosted avatar assets, and no RP1 attach. If a world is ever built for it, the avatars themselves — how they look, who controls them, and how identity is shown — would be the first design decision, and it would need to be made in writing, with the people who will use them, before a single asset is hosted.

What exists today vs. what is planned

What exists today

  • A name and a series number (S23) in the April 2026 internal W3 series list.
  • A planned focus implied by the name: conversation through avatars, governed as a DAO.
  • The Foundation's filed nonprofit status and its intended W3 governance model, described on this page.
  • No formed company, no designation, no bank account, no EIN, no community, no members, no moderators, no avatars, and no conversations for this series.

What is planned

  • A written mission: who the space is for, what conversations it hosts, and what it explicitly does not do.
  • Identity, moderation, and record rules, decided by humans, published before anyone joins.
  • A Steward Council and named human stewards, with decision limits in writing.
  • Avatar design: how identity is shown, who controls an avatar, and how impersonation is prevented.
  • A metaverse decision: a world with coordinates, a fabric JSON, hosted avatar assets, and a verifiable RP1 attach — or a written record that the series works in text and needs no world.

How it fits

This series sits in the W3 programme's presence and communication group, and its closest neighbours are the series that also deal with how beings appear and relate. Series S29, Metaverse DAO, is the first: if Avatar.Chat ever has a world, the boundary between the chat DAO that fills a space and the metaverse DAO that governs the space itself would need to be written down first. Series S2, Empathic.X, is the second: conversation through avatars is, at its best, a way of understanding and being understood, and the two series would need to say how they relate rather than both claiming the same ground.

Other related series include S1 Cognition.X (the intelligence behind the conversation, if any), S16 EchoDawn DAO (whose name suggests voice or presence, though its focus is not stated in the files reviewed either), and S7 Divine DAO (if conversation ever touches the Fellowship's spiritual work — which would need a written agreement with the Fellowship, and the Fellowship's independence respected, before any such conversation could be hosted). None of those relationships is a partnership today.

In tier terms, this series would belong to the commercial engine tier — the W3 Wyoming series network — under the Foundation's mission gate at the apex. The Foundation does not own the series in the way a parent company owns a subsidiary, and this page does not claim that it does. Any relationship would be set out in a written mission agreement, with the Foundation board remaining independent, as a California nonprofit board must.

Under the intended structure described in the Foundation's planning documents, a W3 series would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. That is a plan, not a description of something operating today. No Steward Council for this series is named in the files reviewed, no operating agreement for this series is in the files reviewed, and no agent has authority to sign for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series or the Foundation.

The boundaries in that intended model are deliberately narrow. Agents would prepare work, draft documents, keep records, and coordinate — they would not enact doctrine or charters, hire or fire, offer securities, sign contracts, or move funds. Humans would execute every step that creates a legal or financial obligation. If this series ever moves from planned to active, those boundaries would need to be written down first, in the series' own governing documents, and approved by the Foundation board and by counsel — not assumed from this page.

Governance also means the Foundation's role stays a mission gate, not ownership in the ordinary commercial sense. The Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, and its board must remain independent. Control over a series, if any is ever exercised, would be through written mission agreements, not through treating a nonprofit as a subsidiary of a for-profit head or a series as a pocket of the Foundation. That distinction matters, and it is why this page keeps saying "intended" and "planned" instead of describing a live reporting line.

What would need to happen next

The list below is the same honest checklist that applies to every planned W3 series: it is the work that would have to be done, in writing, before this series could be described as anything more than planned. It is not a roadmap with dates, and it is not a promise that any of it will happen.

  1. Confirm the series exists in law. A Wyoming SOS search for W3 LLC, and a written per-series designation for this series, would have to be produced. Until then, the series name is a name on an internal list, not a legal entity.
  2. Write the focus down. A one-page mission statement for this series, approved in writing by the Foundation board, stating what it will and will not do — so that later work can be checked against it instead of drifting.
  3. Name the humans. A Steward Council and a named human steward, with their roles, limits, and decision process written down. No series should operate on unnamed stewards.
  4. Settle the metaverse question. Either anchor a fabric with coordinates, a fabric JSON file, and hosted assets, and attach it to RP1 — or record in writing that this series has no metaverse presence and does not need one. Either answer is acceptable; an unexamined assumption is not.
  5. Separate money from the start. If the series ever holds funds, it would need its own bank account and ledger, its own EIN where required, and a written agreement with the Foundation. No pooling, no shared pot, no informal transfers.
  6. Publish the record. Designation, mission, stewards, and any metaverse anchor should be published or filed where a reviewer can check them, so that the next version of this page can replace "planned" with a dated, sourced fact.

None of those steps has a date on this page, because no date has been set in the files reviewed. A step with no date is a step still to be scheduled, not a step that is late — and this page will not invent a timeline to make the plan look further along than it is.
