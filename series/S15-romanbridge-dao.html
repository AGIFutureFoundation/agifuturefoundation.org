What this series is

RomanBridge.DAO is W3 Series S15 in the Foundation's intended W3 network. Its name is the only verified detail about it beyond its number and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. This page therefore describes it only as a planned DAO whose focus, as the name implies, would be bridging: connection between people, places, systems, or communities that are currently apart. That is a description of a domain, stated as a planned focus — not a claim that any bridge, network, member, agreement, or transaction exists today under this name.

The name invites a broad reading, and the honest response is to keep that breadth visible rather than to narrow it by invention. A bridge can connect many kinds of distance: between two communities, between a physical place and a virtual one, between two systems that cannot yet talk to each other, or between people who need each other and have no route to meet. A series called RomanBridge.DAO could in principle work on any of those, but no choice among them is recorded, no community, place, system, or counterpart is named in any verified source, and this page does not pick one. The planned focus is therefore stated at the level the name supports: bridging and connection, governed as a DAO, if the series is ever formed.

The DAO form itself deserves a plain explanation, because the letters in the name are doing real work and should not be treated as decoration. A DAO — a decentralised autonomous organisation — in the Foundation's intended model means agent-managed day-to-day work under human decision authority: agents would operate the routine work of the bridge within limits set in advance by humans, while a human Steward Council decides, signs, and moves funds. The DAO label does not remove human responsibility, does not create a legal entity by itself, and does not authorise any transaction. For RomanBridge.DAO, the DAO form is planned, not operating: there is no member list, no token, no treasury, no vote, and no proposal record for this series today, and none is claimed. Any future DAO mechanism for this series would need its own legal form, its own rules, and counsel's review — the name alone creates none of those things.

The questions a future steward would need to answer are therefore about the bridge itself, before any mechanism is chosen. What exactly would RomanBridge.DAO connect, and for whom? Who is on each side of the bridge, and what do they need from each other that they cannot get today? What would the bridge carry — information, introductions, coordination, resources — and what would it refuse to carry? Who would be accountable when the bridge fails, or when something crossing it causes harm? How would membership, if there were members, be decided, and what rights and duties would members have? None of those questions has a verified answer today. Listing them is a record of the work that remains before this planned series could become a real one, not a substitute for doing it.

What is planned

The plan, as far as it can honestly be stated today, is to keep this series as a named, planned focus until a human steward, with counsel, decides whether it should be formed at all — and if so, what exactly it would bridge, for whom, and under what rules.

  • Decide, in writing, whether RomanBridge.DAO should be formed, and if so, what it would connect, for whom, and what it would carry — and what it would refuse to carry.
  • Define the DAO's human governance first — who decides, who signs, who moves funds, and who is accountable — before any agent operation, membership, or treasury is considered.
  • Set the membership, proposal, and decision rules in writing, with counsel, including who can join, who can propose, who can vote, and how a decision can be reversed.
  • Choose a governance form — series designation, operating agreement, and Steward Council appointments — with counsel, if formation is approved.
  • Only then, decide whether a metaverse presence would serve the communities the bridge would connect, and build it to the four-part standard described in the metaverse section above.

Each step would need its own document, decision, and, where required, counsel's review. None is presented here as done, scheduled, or promised by a date, because no date is verified.

How it fits

Tier. This series is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation's planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series, including this one, as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series' designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain this page can cite, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series' future.

Related series. The nearest neighbours in the numbering are Series S14 (AskDoc.X) and Series S16 (EchoDawn DAO), and the wider group on this site runs from S09 through S16. Numbering proximity is the only relationship this page claims: the series in this group share a place in the same internal list and the same intended W3 structure, and no operational partnership, shared member, shared community, shared system, or shared funding between this series and any other series is claimed. Any future relationship with EchoDawn DAO (Series S16), the other DAO-named series in this group, would need its own written agreement and would be cited here only then; sharing the DAO form does not by itself create a partnership.

Governance for this series, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be DAO-managed: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed series is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for this series. There is no Steward Council roster, no operating agreement, and no published set of agent limits for this series to point to today. Naming the intended model here is a statement of intent about how the series would be run if it were formed — it is not evidence that the model is already running. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, so control would run only through those written agreements, designed with counsel.

Three practical consequences follow. First, no agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one. Second, any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement for this series would need its own legal documents, its own issuer, and counsel review — the series name alone authorises nothing. Third, the public record for this series should grow only as real documents are created: a designation, an operating agreement, a council appointment, a signed agreement. Until then, the honest public record is this page's status banner.
