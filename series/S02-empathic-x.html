What this series is

Empathic.X is Series 2 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. Its name points at empathy: the capacity to recognise, understand, and respond appropriately to what another person is feeling, and to build systems that support that capacity rather than simulate it crudely or exploit it. That is the domain the name implies, and it is stated here as a planned focus, because beyond the name and the series number there is almost nothing verified to say about this series. No product, no research programme, no partner, no user group, and no published description beyond the name itself has been verified in the files.

The distinction between supporting empathy and pretending to feel is the central question this series would have to answer, and it is worth stating before any building begins. A system can help a person notice another’s distress, can suggest a kinder phrasing, can slow a conversation down, or can flag that someone may need human care — all without claiming to feel anything itself. A system that claimed to empathise, that used emotional signals to manipulate, or that substituted a machine response for human care where a human was needed would be a different and more troubling thing. Which side of that line Empathic.X intends to stand on has not been written down anywhere verified, so this page does not assign it a position. It names the question because any honest account of an empathy series must.

The neighbouring domains help to sketch, without inventing, where such a series might matter. Learning (Cognition.X, Series 1) depends on trust and on a teacher noticing when a student is lost or distressed. Assessment and care (Diagnostic.X, Series 3) depend on listening well and on not reducing a person to a score. Community and meaning (Divine DAO, Series 7) depend on how people treat one another. Empathic.X, if it is built, would sit across those neighbours rather than inside any one of them. That is a statement about the shape of the domain, not a claim that any collaboration exists: no joint work with any neighbouring series is verified.

There is also a privacy question that belongs on this page from the start. Anything that works with emotional signals — words, tone, expression, or behaviour — works with some of the most personal information a person can produce. A series in this domain would need, before it collected anything, a written answer to what it collects, why, how long it keeps it, who can see it, and how a person opts out or deletes it. No such answer exists for Empathic.X today. The absence of a data policy is not a detail to be filled in later; for this domain, it is the first thing that would need to exist, and its absence is part of why this page describes the series as thin and planned rather than operating.

Metaverse connection

Anchored world: Empathic.X, 47.43990000744796, -179.34952851914045. This is one of the verified anchored worlds in the Foundation’s metaverse work: an Empathic.X fabric with those coordinates. Its fabric JSON is live on GitHub Pages under the WorldForge-Browser my-fabric directory (fabric JSON), but the referenced assets behind it — on the order of two hundred GLB model files plus the map.wasm module — are not hosted there, so the world does not render fully in Artemis yet. That is the verified state: a published fabric definition, an anchored location, and an incomplete render because the assets it points to are not in place.

What that means in practice is that the world exists as a plan and a partial build, not as a place a visitor can enter today. The next step, if it is to be visitable, is specific: host the referenced GLB assets and map.wasm at the paths the fabric expects, then verify the render in Artemis. Until that is done, any image or description of the world should be labelled as a concept or a partial build, not as a live world. And the world is not on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1, no fabric or service for this series has ever been attached there, and the RP1 Attach wizard needs a live metaverse-server endpoint before any attach can happen.

For an empathy-focused series, a metaverse world raises the same question the series itself raises, in a more concrete form: what would people do there that helps them understand one another better, and what would be off limits? A shared space could host guided conversation, perspective-taking exercises, or simply a calmer place to meet — or it could become a stage for performance and surveillance. No design for such a space is verified, so this page claims none. The anchored coordinates are a location for a future build, not evidence of one.

What exists today — and what is planned

Two things are verified. First, the name and number: Empathic.X, Series 2, in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. Second, the anchored metaverse fabric: an Empathic.X world at 47.43990000744796, -179.34952851914045, with its fabric JSON live on GitHub Pages and its assets not yet hosted, so it does not render fully in Artemis. What is planned

Everything else is planned, not existing: the formation of W3 LLC and the designation of this series; a human Steward Council and operating rules; any empathy-related product, research, or service; any data policy for emotional signals; any funding; and a fully rendering world on a live metaverse service. Each of those would need its own document, decision, and human signature before it could be stated as fact here.

The thinness of this page is deliberate. A series about empathy that filled its own page with invented users, invented outcomes, or invented testimonials would be failing its own subject before it began. The honest size of Empathic.X today is a name, a number, and a partial world — and the most useful next step would be a written statement of scope from a named human steward, including a data policy and a clear line between supporting human empathy and simulating it.

Governance — planned, not yet operating

The planned governance model for the W3 series, as described on the Foundation site, is DAO-managed day-to-day work with human decision-making at every point that matters: agents may draft proposals, prepare research, and operate routine systems, but humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. A Steward Council of humans is intended to hold that gate. No Steward Council membership, no signed votes, no seated manager, and no operating agreement for Empathic.X is verified in the files, so none is named here. Until those human roles are seated in writing, there is no one who can speak for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series — and this page does not do so.

What governance would have to answer first. Before Empathic.X could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. Listing the questions is not the same as answering them, and this page deliberately does not pretend otherwise.

What would have to happen before this series could operate

A named series is not a formed series. For Empathic.X to move from a line in an internal list to an operating venture, several distinct steps would each have to happen, in order, with documents to prove them. First, W3 LLC itself would need to be formed and evidenced by a filing that can be checked. Second, this series would need to be designated in writing as a series of that LLC, with its own name, purpose, and manager recorded. Third, the series would need its own operating rules, its own human stewards, and its own segregated accounting — so that its money, liabilities, and records are not mixed with any other series or with the Foundation. Fourth, any bank account, contract, hire, or public claim would need a named human signer acting under those written rules. Fifth, any fundraising would need its own counsel-approved documents, its own issuer, and its own securities compliance — it could not borrow the Foundation’s nonprofit status, and the Foundation, as filed, cannot offer equity or a SAFE. None of those steps is verified as done for Empathic.X. This section is included so that a reader can see the gap plainly, rather than mistaking a polished landing page for a finished company.

That gap is also why this page carries no timeline. There is no launch date, no funding date, and no hiring date stated here, because none has been set in a verified document. A date on a landing page would look like a commitment; without a filing, a budget, and a human team behind it, it would be a decoration, not a plan. When a real date exists, it should appear here with the document behind it cited.

Proposed projects — for DAO review

These are proposals for the series DAO team to review. None is approved, funded, staffed, or scheduled.

Beyond the name and the series number, almost nothing is verified about this series: no product, no research programme, no partner, no user group, and no published description beyond the name itself. The proposals below are therefore generic to the name and to the planned focus the name implies: empathy as the capacity to recognise, understand, and respond appropriately to what another person is feeling.

Support, not simulation, principle paper

A written statement of the central distinction this series must hold: systems should support empathy without claiming to feel anything themselves. The paper would set out what support means in practice and which claims the series will never make. It would be written before any building begins.

What success would look like: A principle paper exists that the stewards have approved, stating plainly that the series supports empathy and never claims to feel.

Reviewing team: the Empathic.X DAO

Status: PROPOSED — pending DAO team review

No funding, partner, or timeline yet.

Kinder phrasing helper proposal

A proposal for a tool that suggests a kinder phrasing in a conversation, helping a person notice how their words might land. The proposal would cover when suggestions appear, how a person can dismiss them, and how the tool avoids steering a conversation toward a manipulative outcome. No such tool is verified as built.

What success would look like: Reviewed examples show the helper suggesting kinder phrasing that the speaker accepts or declines freely, with no hidden steering.

Reviewing team: the Empathic.X DAO

Status: PROPOSED — pending DAO team review

No funding, partner, or timeline yet.

Distress flagging with a human handoff

A proposal for a tool that notices signs that someone may need human care and routes the situation to a human, rather than substituting a machine response where a human is needed. The proposal would name the human responder role and the handoff steps. It would never present the flag itself as care.

What success would look like: A handoff flow exists with a named human responder role, tested in rehearsal from flag to human contact.

Reviewing team: the Empathic.X DAO

Status: PROPOSED — pending DAO team review

No funding, partner, or timeline yet.

Conversation slow-down aid

A proposal for a tool that slows a heated conversation down, giving people room to think before they reply. The proposal would describe the pacing cues, how a person opts in, and how the aid steps aside when it is not wanted. No such tool is verified as built for this series.

What success would look like: A rehearsal script shows the aid slowing a heated exchange and both speakers reporting more room to think.

Reviewing team: the Empathic.X DAO

Status: PROPOSED — pending DAO team review

No funding, partner, or timeline yet.

Misuse guardrails draft

A written list of emotional-signal uses this series will not build: no manipulation through emotional signals, no substitution of a machine response for human care where a human is needed, and no claim to empathise. The list would be a guardrail document for stewards to enforce. No such document exists today.

What success would look like: A guardrails list exists that the stewards have approved, naming the emotional-signal uses the series refuses to build.

Reviewing team: the Empathic.X DAO

Status: PROPOSED — pending DAO team review

No funding, partner, or timeline yet.
