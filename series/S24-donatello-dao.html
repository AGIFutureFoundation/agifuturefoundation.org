What this series is

Donatello DAO is W3 Series S24, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. That is, honestly, almost everything the files reviewed say about it. The name "Donatello" gives no focus in the way that "Diagnostic.X" or "Get.Delivery DAO" give a focus, and no description, mission, project, person, place, product, or partner for this series appears in the files reviewed. This page therefore says so plainly, rather than filling the gap with invention — and it is, with S19 and S20, one of the thin pages in this group for exactly that reason.

It would be easy, and wrong, to guess. A reader might associate the name "Donatello" with a historical figure, an artist, a fictional character, or a person they know, and then write a page about whichever association came to mind first. This page does none of that. No association is stated in the list, no expansion or explanation of the name is given there, and no site copy, article, or planning document reviewed here explains what "Donatello" means for this series. To pick one association and present it as the series' focus would be to invent a fact and then build a page on it — and to risk, in the process, implying an endorsement by, or a connection to, a real person or estate that has never been established.

That last risk deserves emphasis, because names that sound like people's names carry a particular danger. If "Donatello" refers to a person — living or historical — then a page that assumed which person, and what they would endorse, would be putting words in someone's mouth. If it refers to something else entirely — a place, a project codename, an inside reference — then a page that assumed a person would be wrong in a different way, but wrong all the same. The only safe course is the one this page takes: record the name exactly as listed, record that no explanation is given, and invite whoever proposed it to supply the explanation in writing.

What can be said, without guessing, is what the "DAO" suffix and the series number imply about the shape of the thing, as opposed to its subject. Donatello DAO is intended to become a liability-isolated series under W3 LLC, DAO-managed day-to-day with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council, under the Foundation's mission gate. That shape is planned for the whole programme and is described in the "How it fits" section below. It says nothing about what Donatello DAO would do — only how it would be governed if it ever did anything.

The questions that would have to be answered before this page could grow are simple to list and impossible to answer from the files reviewed: What does "Donatello" refer to? What field, problem, or community would the series serve? Who proposed it, and why? What would its first piece of work be, and who would do it? Is there an existing project, place, or person behind the name, or is the name a placeholder for an idea not yet written down? Each of those questions has a named human who could answer it, and until one of them does, in writing, this page should stay as it is: a name with its status stated, and no more.

That restraint is not a failure of the page; it is the page doing its job. A series page that invented a focus for Donatello DAO would mislead everyone who read it — a potential collaborator would arrive expecting one thing and find another, a reviewer would cite a focus that never existed, and the real project, if one exists behind the name, would have to spend its first months correcting a public record it never wrote. A page that says "not stated" costs nothing and can be corrected in a single sentence the moment a real answer arrives.

Metaverse connection

This series has no anchored metaverse fabric yet. That sentence is the whole of what can honestly be said about its metaverse connection today: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are in the files reviewed for this series, and this page invents none of them.

Zero worlds are live on RP1. That is the position for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to this series, and it should not be read as a failure of this series in particular — it is simply where the programme stands. A series page that claimed a live world, a set of coordinates, or a fabric URL without a file to cite would be inventing a fact, and this page will not do that.

What would be needed for this series to gain a metaverse presence is concrete and checkable: a chosen location with published coordinates, a fabric JSON file describing the world, hosted assets (models, textures, and any interactive elements) that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach that can be opened and verified by a reviewer. Until all four exist and can be cited, the correct statement remains "no anchored metaverse fabric yet," and any future update to this section should name the coordinates, link the fabric file, and give the date the RP1 attach was verified — not merely assert that a world exists.

Whether this series even needs a metaverse presence is an open question, not a settled one. Some series are naturally spatial — a training ground, a clinic, a city, a delivery network — and some are naturally not. The decision should be made series by series, in writing, with a reason: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the series works in documents, code, and human conversation and does not need a world. Either answer is defensible. What is not defensible is assuming a world exists because the programme talks about worlds in general.

For this series in particular, there is not even a basis to speculate about what a metaverse presence might be for. A spatial world serves a purpose — a place to meet, to train, to show, to build — and no purpose has been stated for Donatello DAO, so no world can be described, even as a plan. The metaverse decision for this series comes after the focus decision, not before it: first say what the series is for, then ask whether a shared spatial place would help it do that, and only then consider coordinates, a fabric JSON, hosted assets, and an RP1 attach.

How it fits

Because no focus is stated, no related series can honestly be named for Donatello DAO. To list neighbours would be to guess at a relationship on the basis of a name that has not been explained, and this page will not do that. The series belongs, in tier terms, to the commercial engine tier — the W3 Wyoming series network — under the Foundation's mission gate at the apex, in the same planned shape as every other series in the list. That is a statement about its intended legal and governance shape, not about its subject.

The governance model that would apply, if the series ever becomes active, is the programme-wide intended model described below: DAO-managed day-to-day, with agents operating within human-set limits, and humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council. No council for this series is named, no DAO vote or treasury is claimed, and no agent has authority to act for it today.

The most useful "fit" statement for this series is therefore a procedural one: Donatello DAO fits the programme as a named, numbered, unfilled slot — a place held in the list for an idea whose description has not yet been written down. Its fit with any other series will be decided when its focus is stated, and the page should be updated then, with the relationships named and sourced, rather than now, by guesswork.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the intended structure described in the Foundation's planning documents, a W3 series would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. That is a plan, not a description of something operating today. No Steward Council for this series is named in the files reviewed, no operating agreement for this series is in the files reviewed, and no agent has authority to sign for, commit, or spend on behalf of this series or the Foundation.

The boundaries in that intended model are deliberately narrow. Agents would prepare work, draft documents, keep records, and coordinate — they would not enact doctrine or charters, hire or fire, offer securities, sign contracts, or move funds. Humans would execute every step that creates a legal or financial obligation. If this series ever moves from planned to active, those boundaries would need to be written down first, in the series' own governing documents, and approved by the Foundation board and by counsel — not assumed from this page.

Governance also means the Foundation's role stays a mission gate, not ownership in the ordinary commercial sense. The Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, and its board must remain independent. Control over a series, if any is ever exercised, would be through written mission agreements, not through treating a nonprofit as a subsidiary of a for-profit head or a series as a pocket of the Foundation. That distinction matters, and it is why this page keeps saying "intended" and "planned" instead of describing a live reporting line.

What would need to happen next

The list below is the same honest checklist that applies to every planned W3 series: it is the work that would have to be done, in writing, before this series could be described as anything more than planned. It is not a roadmap with dates, and it is not a promise that any of it will happen.

  1. Confirm the series exists in law. A Wyoming SOS search for W3 LLC, and a written per-series designation for this series, would have to be produced. Until then, the series name is a name on an internal list, not a legal entity.
  2. Write the focus down. A one-page mission statement for this series, approved in writing by the Foundation board, stating what it will and will not do — so that later work can be checked against it instead of drifting.
  3. Name the humans. A Steward Council and a named human steward, with their roles, limits, and decision process written down. No series should operate on unnamed stewards.
  4. Settle the metaverse question. Either anchor a fabric with coordinates, a fabric JSON file, and hosted assets, and attach it to RP1 — or record in writing that this series has no metaverse presence and does not need one. Either answer is acceptable; an unexamined assumption is not.
  5. Separate money from the start. If the series ever holds funds, it would need its own bank account and ledger, its own EIN where required, and a written agreement with the Foundation. No pooling, no shared pot, no informal transfers.
  6. Publish the record. Designation, mission, stewards, and any metaverse anchor should be published or filed where a reviewer can check them, so that the next version of this page can replace "planned" with a dated, sourced fact.

None of those steps has a date on this page, because no date has been set in the files reviewed. A step with no date is a step still to be scheduled, not a step that is late — and this page will not invent a timeline to make the plan look further along than it is.

The three advisors per world are Host, Steward Liaison, and Alignment; advisors are scripted guides, not AI, and get no prompt by contract. The prompts are deterministic stubs held to a ten-point eval (umbrella present, every human-reserved action listed and marked Held for the Steward Council, every forbidden action listed, source cited, zone named, no personal data, under 6,000 characters) — and no model has run any of them.
  • DAO management pattern in the same repo (agents/dao.mjs) — one DAO team per world, seated in the eight agent roles the charter assigns to agents (research synthesis, task routing, proposal generation, operational coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, grant drafting). Proposals move draft → under review → enacted or declined, with steward transitions requiring a 2-of-3 steward quorum; no treasury is held and treasury.transfer is refused to everyone.
