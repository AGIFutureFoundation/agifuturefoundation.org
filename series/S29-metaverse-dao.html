What this series is

Metaverse DAO is W3 Series 29. The April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Metaverse DAO — Series 29” and gives no further description, so the scope on this page is a planned focus drawn from what the name implies, labelled as planned throughout. The name implies a governance role for the metaverse itself: the series that would decide how the Foundation's worlds are run, who can build in them, what agents may do there, and how the worlds relate to the series that anchor them.

That governance role is the most important thing to understand about this series, because it is easy to misread. Metaverse DAO is not a world. It is not a place you visit, and it is not the owner of the six anchored worlds. The anchored worlds belong to their series — SmartCiti.X, Cognition.X, Locator.X, Biota.blockchain (unnumbered), Empathic.X, and Diagnostic.X — and this series governs none of them today. What is planned is a coordinating function: shared standards for worlds across the network, so that a visitor, a builder, or an agent encounters consistent rules about identity, safety, and conduct rather than a different regime in every world. Whether that coordination is needed, and what authority it would have over worlds that belong to other series, are open questions that would need written agreements, not assumptions.

The context for those standards is worth stating honestly. As stated on the Foundation site, WorldForge holds 80 worlds — that figure is site copy, not a verified count from this page's own research, and it is labelled as such here. Zero worlds are live on RP1: no world in the network has been verified as operating on RP1, and this page does not claim otherwise. The gap between a catalogue of worlds and worlds that are live, governed, and visited is exactly the gap a Metaverse DAO would exist to close — and it is a large gap. A DAO that writes standards for worlds nobody operates would be governing an empty room. The planned sequence is therefore worlds first in the sense that matters: defined fabrics, stated purposes, and real activity, with governance standards following the activity rather than preceding it in the abstract.

If the DAO ever operates, its agenda would be practical rather than grand. It would need to decide: how identity works across worlds, and what an agent must disclose about its operator and its human principal before it acts in-world; what conduct rules apply, who enforces them, and what appeal exists for someone who is sanctioned; how builders get space and what they owe in return; what data a world collects about visitors and where that data goes; and how a world is retired, including what happens to its contents and its records. It would also need to decide its own limits: which decisions belong to the DAO, which belong to the series that anchors each world, and which belong to the Foundation as mission gate. Those boundaries, written down and agreed, are the governance product. No such standards, no enforcement, and no membership exist today.

Validation for Metaverse DAO would be measured in adoption, not in documents. A set of standards that no world adopts is a draft, however well written. The planned path is to start with one world that has real activity and real stewards, agree a small set of rules with them, operate under those rules for a fixed period, and publish what worked and what did not — then extend to a second world only if the first asks for it. That is slower than announcing a network-wide constitution, and it is the only way the standards would be grounded in something real. Until there is a world with activity asking to be governed, Metaverse DAO remains a planned coordinating function awaiting something to coordinate, and this page keeps it described that way.

One line on an internal list: the April 2026 W3 series list names this series and its number. The Foundation itself is verified as filed (see the status note above). That is the complete inventory. This page does not present a plan as an achievement, and it does not fill the empty inventory with plausible-sounding detail.

What is planned

A DAO-managed series in the intended W3 network, operating day to day through agents within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds, and with the Foundation as mission gate under written agreements. Before any of that can happen, the series needs a written statement of purpose, a designated legal form, counsel's review, a named steward, and a first stage-gate decision. Each of those is a human decision with a dated record. None has been made yet.

The gap between the two cards above is the work. It is normal for a planned series to have that gap; what would not be normal is pretending it does not exist. Readers, reviewers, and potential stewards should judge this series by what is on record, and should ask for the dated record behind any future claim that the gap has closed.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the Foundation's intended venture-studio model, each W3 series is DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents operate the series within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. That division is deliberate and non-negotiable in the model. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute. Every material decision — forming the series, opening a bank account, signing a partnership, spending money, or taking on an obligation — requires a named human's decision and signature.

The Foundation sits above the W3 series as a mission gate, not as an owner in the ordinary commercial sense: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and the Foundation's board must remain independent. Control in the intended model runs through written mission agreements and standards the series agree to, with counsel to design the exact structure. The Foundation's public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — is the standard every series is meant to be measured against, and the Windfall Clause framework is planned to apply across the W3 series: a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold (to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP) would flow to humanity through global public goods. The threshold, measurement, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today, and no series page states them as settled terms.

Governance of a series, once designated, would also need a written operating agreement, a named Steward Council, a compliance calendar, and separate accounts and records per series. None of that exists for this series yet, because the series itself does not exist yet as a legal entity. The governance described on this page is the intended model, not a description of a operating body. Anyone reading this page who wants to help shape that governance should contact the Foundation (see below) rather than assuming any of it is already in force.

Open questions this series must answer before it can proceed

  1. Which worlds, if any, have asked to be governed by shared standards — and what would those standards cover first?
  2. Where does Metaverse DAO's authority end and each anchoring series' authority begin, in writing?
  3. What would count as adoption: how many worlds, with what activity, operating under the standards for how long?

These are not rhetorical questions. Each one needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series passes its first stage gate. If you can help answer one of them, the contact below is the route.
