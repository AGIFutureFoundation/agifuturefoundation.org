What this series is

ParkingSmart.X is W3 Series S12 in the Foundation's intended W3 network. Its name is the only verified detail about it beyond its number and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. This page therefore describes it only as a planned series whose focus, as the name implies, would be parking made smarter: parking data, guidance to available spaces, and the management of parking as a shared urban resource. That is a description of a domain, not a claim that any parking product, deployment, city agreement, or user exists today under this name.

The planned focus, stated at the level the name supports, is a practical one. Parking is a coordination problem: drivers need to know where space is, owners and cities need to know how space is used, and everyone involved benefits when searching, congestion, and wasted trips fall. A series called ParkingSmart.X could in principle work on guidance (telling a driver where space is likely to be), on management (helping an owner or a city run a facility or a zone), or on the data underneath both (counting, predicting, and reporting how parking is used). No choice among those is recorded, no city, facility, or deployment is named in any verified source, and this page does not invent one. The planned focus is therefore the domain itself: smarter parking, delivered by agents under human governance, if the series is ever formed.

The questions a future steward would need to answer are the ordinary, grounding questions of any urban service. Whose parking would the series serve — a single facility, a district, a city — and under whose authority? Parking space is owned, regulated, and priced by someone, and a guidance or management service cannot operate without that owner's or authority's written agreement. What data would it rely on, and how would it be collected: sensors, cameras, payment records, user reports, or some combination — and with what consent and privacy protection for the people whose movements that data can reveal? How would it handle the difference between a prediction (space is likely available) and a guarantee (space is reserved for you), and what would happen when the prediction is wrong? What would it charge, to whom, and under what terms — and what would it refuse to do, for example with enforcement or with pricing that excludes people who need access? None of those questions has a verified answer today, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Two further cautions belong on this page. First, parking is local: a system that works in one facility or city does not automatically work in another, and no claim is made here about any city, site, or deployment. Second, parking data is location data, and location data about individuals deserves a privacy design from the start — what is collected, how long it is kept, who can see it, and how a person opts out — decided by humans before any collection begins. A series that treated those questions as afterthoughts would be building on sand. Naming them here is a record of the work that remains before this planned series could become a real one.

First, an anchor: a named place with published coordinates that the series has actually chosen, not a coordinate invented to make a page look finished. Second, a fabric: a fabric JSON file describing that world, published where it can be checked. Third, hosted assets: the 3D models and supporting files the world needs, actually hosted and reachable, so the world renders instead of failing to load. That standard is stated here because the Foundation's metaverse work elsewhere shows how easy it is to overclaim. This page therefore treats a metaverse connection as a verifiable chain (coordinates, fabric, hosted assets, live render, RP1 attach), and it reports this series as having none of that chain yet. If the series later gains an anchor, this section should be updated with the coordinates, the fabric link, the asset hosting, and the date and result of the live render check — each as a dated, checkable fact. In the meantime, the planned metaverse role for a series like this one is best described as a question for its future stewards, not as a feature: would a virtual space help the people this series would serve, and if so, what would it need to contain to be useful rather than decorative? No such decision is recorded here, because no such decision has been made.

What is planned

The plan, as far as it can honestly be stated today, is to keep this series as a named, planned focus until a human steward, with counsel, decides whether it should be formed at all — and if so, which parking problem it would solve, for whom, and under whose authority.

  • Decide, in writing, whether ParkingSmart.X should be formed, and if so, whether it would offer guidance, management, data, or some combination — and which of those it would refuse.
  • Identify a first owner or authority whose written agreement would be needed, and do not collect data or operate a service before that agreement exists.
  • Commission a privacy design for any location or payment data the series would handle, before any data is collected.
  • Choose a governance form — series designation, operating agreement, and Steward Council appointments — with counsel, if formation is approved.
  • Only then, decide whether a metaverse presence would serve the series' users, and build it to the four-part standard described in the metaverse section above.

Each step would need its own document, decision, and, where required, counsel's review. None is presented here as done, scheduled, or promised by a date, because no date is verified.

How it fits

Tier. This series is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation's planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series, including this one, as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series' designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain this page can cite, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series' future.

Related series. The nearest neighbours in the numbering are Series S11 (Molecular.) and Series S13 (Gonstead.X), and the wider group on this site runs from S09 through S16. Numbering proximity is the only relationship this page claims: the series in this group share a place in the same internal list and the same intended W3 structure, and no operational partnership, shared customer, shared product, shared city, or shared funding between this series and any other series is claimed. In particular, no relationship with SmartCiti.X (Series S10) beyond shared membership in the intended network is claimed, even though both names touch cities and the built environment; any future cooperation would need its own written agreement and would be cited here only then.

Governance for this series, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be DAO-managed: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed series is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for this series. There is no Steward Council roster, no operating agreement, and no published set of agent limits for this series to point to today. Naming the intended model here is a statement of intent about how the series would be run if it were formed — it is not evidence that the model is already running. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, so control would run only through those written agreements, designed with counsel.

Three practical consequences follow. First, no agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one. Second, any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement for this series would need its own legal documents, its own issuer, and counsel review — the series name alone authorises nothing. Third, the public record for this series should grow only as real documents are created: a designation, an operating agreement, a council appointment, a signed agreement. Until then, the honest public record is this page's status banner.
