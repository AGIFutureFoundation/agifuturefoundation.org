What this series is

Solar DAO is W3 Series 32. The April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Solar DAO — Series 32” and gives no further description, so the scope on this page is a planned focus drawn from what the name implies, labelled as planned throughout. The name implies an energy lane: solar generation, storage, and the infrastructure that moves power from where it is made to where it is used. Energy is the most physical of the W3 lanes after robotics and aerospace, and the planned focus has to be grounded in that physicality: panels, inverters, batteries, grids, permits, and the people who install and maintain them.

The first distinction to make is between governing energy and generating it. A DAO in this lane could, in principle, do either or both — operate solar assets, coordinate a network of them, or govern how energy data and energy value are shared — and those are different businesses with different capital needs, different regulations, and different risks. No choice between them has been made, and no asset, site, installation, customer, or power-purchase agreement exists for this series. Any page that described Solar DAO as owning panels or selling power would be inventing a company. This page describes a planned focus and the decisions that would have to precede any asset: which lane, in which place, under which permits, with whose capital, and accountable to whom.

Energy also connects this series to the Foundation's wider purpose more directly than most. The Windfall Clause framework, as planned, directs value above a threshold toward global public goods, and energy infrastructure is named among the destinations in the pitch framework — alongside healthcare access, technological self-determination, and global public goods generally. That is a framework, not a funding commitment: the threshold, the share, and the recipient are all blank for counsel, and no money exists to distribute. But the connection is real as a direction of travel. A solar series that one day helped bring power to places that lack it would be the clearest expression of the Foundation's mission in physical form. The honest corollary is that such work would also be the slowest and most capital-intensive in the network, and it would need partners — utilities, installers, communities, and regulators — none of whom have been named or approached for this series.

The practical questions for Solar DAO are therefore grounded and unglamorous, and this page lists them because they are the questions a future reviewer should ask. Where would the first installation be, on whose land or roof, under which permit, and with whose consent? Who installs and maintains it, under what safety standard, and with what warranty? How is the power metered, who buys it, and at what price, under what agreement? What happens when equipment fails, when a battery degrades, or when a site is sold? And how is the community that hosts the installation involved in the decision, rather than merely informed of it afterwards? A DAO that cannot answer those questions is not an energy company; it is a name. None of these questions has an answer today, because no site, no partner, and no agreement exists.

Validation for Solar DAO would be measured in a single working installation, honestly reported, rather than in a portfolio plan. The planned sequence is: written scope first, choosing between governing, coordinating, and generating; then a regulatory and permitting map for one place, reviewed by counsel; then one small installation, with a named installer, a named site, a metered output, and a published account of cost, output, and anything that went wrong; and only then any discussion of a second site. Energy infrastructure rewards that patience and punishes its absence: a portfolio announced before the first panel is metered is a press release, not a power system. Until the first installation exists and is reported, Solar DAO remains a planned focus, and this page keeps it described that way.

Metaverse connection

Neither a fabric nor a purpose exists today.

If any of that changes, this page should be updated with the dated evidence — a fabric address, a world identifier, a transaction record — rather than with a new claim. This page does not present a plan as an achievement, and it does not fill the empty inventory with plausible-sounding detail.

What is planned

A DAO-managed series in the intended W3 network, operating day to day through agents within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds, and with the Foundation as mission gate under written agreements. Before any of that can happen, the series needs a written statement of purpose, a designated legal form, counsel's review, a named steward, and a first stage-gate decision. Each of those is a human decision with a dated record. None has been made yet.

The gap between the two cards above is the work. It is normal for a planned series to have that gap; what would not be normal is pretending it does not exist. Readers, reviewers, and potential stewards should judge this series by what is on record, and should ask for the dated record behind any future claim that the gap has closed.

Governance: DAO-managed day to day, humans decide

Under the Foundation's intended venture-studio model, each W3 series is DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents operate the series within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. That division is deliberate and non-negotiable in the model. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute. Every material decision — forming the series, opening a bank account, signing a partnership, spending money, or taking on an obligation — requires a named human's decision and signature.

The Foundation sits above the W3 series as a mission gate, not as an owner in the ordinary commercial sense: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and the Foundation's board must remain independent. Control in the intended model runs through written mission agreements and standards the series agree to, with counsel to design the exact structure. The Foundation's public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — is the standard every series is meant to be measured against, and the Windfall Clause framework is planned to apply across the W3 series: a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold (to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP) would flow to humanity through global public goods. The threshold, measurement, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today, and no series page states them as settled terms.

Governance of a series, once designated, would also need a written operating agreement, a named Steward Council, a compliance calendar, and separate accounts and records per series. None of that exists for this series yet, because the series itself does not exist yet as a legal entity. The governance described on this page is the intended model, not a description of a operating body. Anyone reading this page who wants to help shape that governance should contact the Foundation (see below) rather than assuming any of it is already in force.

Open questions this series must answer before it can proceed

  1. Which lane is in scope — generating, coordinating, or governing energy — and in which place, under which permits?
  2. Who installs, maintains, and warrants the first installation, and how is its output metered and reported?
  3. How is the host community involved in the decision, and what agreement governs the power, the price, and the land or roof?

These are not rhetorical questions. Each one needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series passes its first stage gate. If you can help answer one of them, the contact below is the route.
