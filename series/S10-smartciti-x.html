SmartCiti.X

SmartCiti.X is W3 Series S10, the planned Trade Craft Academy venture: a proposed new corporation for robotics training, union trades AR/VR, and robotics manufacturing in Louisiana, Nebraska, and Indiana. It is proposed, not formed, funded, or offered.

What this series is

SmartCiti.X is W3 Series S10 in the Foundation's intended W3 network, and it is the most developed plan in the S09–S16 group — which still means it is a plan, not a company. The plan is for a proposed new corporation (a Delaware C-corporation is recommended, with Wyoming as an alternative, and whether it would be a public benefit corporation or a standard corporation is a choice for counsel to make — one form only). It is not Series 10 as a formed legal entity. The issuer of any future raise would be the new corporation, not the filed nonprofit Foundation and not a W3 series.

As stated on the Foundation site, SmartCiti.X is the Trade Craft Academy: robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, and robotics manufacturing in Louisiana (first launch), Nebraska, and Indiana. The intended use of a future $10 million raise is manufacturing, training, and the first sites: $4 million for manufacturing, $3 million for the first Louisiana and Indiana sites, $2 million for AR/VR training, and $1 million for working capital and legal costs. That $3 million covers the first sites only, not an unlimited portfolio, and the real estate would be owned or leased by the corporation itself. No site, city, address, price, seller, or union is named on this page, because none is signed: no site has been purchased, leased, or placed under contract; no site, city, address, price, or seller is verified; and no union and no customer has signed. Any property mentioned in connection with SmartCiti.X is a watchlist candidate only unless a signed agreement says otherwise, and investors in any future corporation would own shares, not units or parcels of land.

The money figures for SmartCiti.X are suggested and illustrative only, never a price. The suggested pre-money valuation is $25 million, and the suggested raise is $10 million, tranched as $5 million in Tranche 1 plus $5 million in Tranche 2 on milestone close. Tranche 1 would be a SAFE or a priced round — one instrument only, with a suggested $25 million cap if a SAFE. Tranche 2 would close only on board certification of all its gates: a signed union agreement, an AR/VR pilot delivered, a first site closed or leased, a first manufacturing order or pilot customer, Tranche 1 reconciled, and a Windfall report drafted. The Tranche 2 price must be stated in the Tranche 1 documents. No money has been raised. No SAFE has been issued, no terms have been agreed, and no return is promised. Any future raise would need its own documents, its own issuer (the new corporation, once formed), counsel review, and the applicable securities filings.

The governance and windfall design planned for the new corporation follow the Foundation's wider architecture. The planned share classes are Class A with one vote and full economics (for the global public), Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only, control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, with its threshold, measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Sovereign participation would be screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules. None of this is in force today, because the corporation does not yet exist.

Two further facts keep this page honest. First, the deeper real-estate plan divides the work into phases: the first sites inside the $3 million envelope; a Louisiana launch and further purchases in Tranche 2; and Nebraska and Indiana expansion only on a Series A or later funding, not inside the $10 million. Housing, if ever offered, would be by residency agreement with a board-set fee, with zoning and safety cleared before occupancy. Second, no SAFE may be issued by the filed nonprofit Foundation: the for-profit and nonprofit tracks are alternatives counsel must choose between in writing before any solicitation, and this page follows the proposed-corporation track.

What is planned

The plan is to form the new corporation first, with counsel choosing one corporate form, and only then to pursue the tranches, the sites, and the training build-out in the order the gates require.

  • Form the proposed SmartCiti.X corporation (Delaware C-corporation recommended), with name and trademark clearance first, its own charter, bylaws, EIN, bank account, and cap table.
  • Choose one corporate form with counsel (public benefit corporation or standard corporation) and write the Class A / Class B / Class C share structure and the Windfall Clause — including its threshold, measure, and recipient — into the charter.
  • Close Tranche 1 ($5 million, one instrument: SAFE with a suggested $25 million cap, or a priced round) under counsel-reviewed documents and the applicable securities filings, with the Tranche 2 price stated in the Tranche 1 documents.
  • Meet every Tranche 2 gate and have the board certify all of them — signed union, AR/VR pilot delivered, first site closed or leased, first manufacturing order or pilot customer, Tranche 1 reconciled, Windfall report drafted — before any Tranche 2 close.
  • Host the Holodeck's approximately 200 GLB assets and map.wasm, verify a full render in Artemis, and pursue an RP1 attach world-by-world only when the render is verified.

Each step would need its own document, decision, and, where required, counsel's review. None is presented here as done, scheduled, or promised by a date, because no date is verified.

How it fits

Tier. This series is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation's planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series, including this one, as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series' designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain this page can cite, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series' future.

Related series. The nearest neighbours in the numbering are Series S09 (Astrological.X) and Series S11 (Molecular.), and the wider group on this site runs from S09 through S16. Numbering proximity is the only relationship this page claims with those series. SmartCiti.X also has a planned relationship with the wider intended network: it is described in the Foundation's planning as the first studio raise, and its planned governance includes the Foundation-only Class C control shares described above. No operational partnership, shared customer, shared product, or shared funding between SmartCiti.X and any other series is claimed, and no other series is presented as a subsidiary, a customer, or a supplier of SmartCiti.X. The BuilderSpace tour — a separate San Francisco startup-community hackathon tour to Louisiana and Nebraska — is a separate project with its own pages and is not presented here as a SmartCiti.X site, venue, sponsor, or commitment.

Governance for this series, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be DAO-managed: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed series is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for this series. There is no Steward Council roster, no operating agreement, and no published set of agent limits for this series to point to today. Naming the intended model here is a statement of intent about how the series would be run if it were formed — it is not evidence that the model is already running. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, so control would run only through those written agreements, designed with counsel.

Three practical consequences follow. First, no agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one. Second, any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement for this series would need its own legal documents, its own issuer, and counsel review — the series name alone authorises nothing. Third, the public record for this series should grow only as real documents are created: a designation, an operating agreement, a council appointment, a signed agreement. Until then, the honest public record is this page's status banner.
