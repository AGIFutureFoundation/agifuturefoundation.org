What BuilderSpace is

BuilderSpace starts from a simple observation: a lot of the energy that makes San Francisco’s startup community work — people building things in public, shipping quickly, hiring from the room, and introducing each other to the next opportunity — travels well, and it travels best when it goes somewhere it is wanted. The Midwest is seeing new data centers and new jobs, and with them new demand for people who can build software, ship products, and start companies. BuilderSpace is the tour that puts those two facts in the same room: SF founders, builders, hackers and startup teams on the road, and local Midwest builders, students and talent on the ground, doing the work together for a season.

The tour is organised around building and shipping. That is the whole programme in one sentence, and it is worth saying plainly because it is also what the tour is not: it is not a speaker series, not a pitch competition circuit, and not a recruiting fair with a hackathon attached. The events are working sessions. A build weekend is a stretch of days set aside to build something and show it. A builder night is an evening for builders to meet, compare notes, and work. A pitch night is an evening for presenting what was built. A skills day is a day for learning and certifying practical skills. Each format is described in general terms on this page and on the track pages, because no detailed agenda has been published for any event, and inventing one would mislead people about what they are signing up for.

The season runs from November 2026 to May 2027. It opens online, with five Thursday-evening onboarding nights in November and December 2026, and then moves onto the road from January to May 2027, with eleven tour events across the winter and spring. The onboarding nights are where the community forms before the tour begins; the tour events are where the building happens. The Grand Finale on Saturday, May 1, 2027 closes the season. It is the closing event of the tour, not a site launch, and it should not be described as one.

Who it is for

BuilderSpace is for two groups of people, and it only works if both show up. The first group is the travelling group: SF founders, builders, hackers and startup teams — people who already build in the San Francisco startup community and are willing to bring that energy on the road. The second group is the local group: Midwest builders, students and talent — people who live where the tour stops and who bring local knowledge, local networks, and their own projects to the table. Neither group is the audience for the other. The point of the tour is that both groups build together, in the same rooms, on the same weekends.

Within those two groups, the tour is open to a wide range of experience. A founder bringing a startup team, a hacker looking for a weekend project, a student learning to ship their first thing, and a local builder with a product already in the world all have a place here, because the formats are built around doing the work rather than around credentials. What matters is showing up ready to build, to help someone else build, or to learn how. There is no published application, no selection process described on Luma, and no attendee list to join — the sign-up route is the event link for each event, and the events are unlisted, so the link is the way in.

What the formats are, in general terms

No detailed agenda has been published for any BuilderSpace event, so the formats below are described only in general terms, from what the event names and formats themselves mean. Anything more specific would be invented, and invented agendas are how people arrive expecting something that is not happening.

A build weekend is a multi-day stretch — Friday to Sunday on this tour — set aside for building. Teams and individuals work on a project, check in with each other as they go, and show what they made at the end. The Bayou AI Build Weekend (Jan 9–11), the Cajun Code Sprint (Mar 5–7), and the Prairie AgTech Build Weekend (Mar 26–28) are the tour’s three build weekends. Each starts on a Friday afternoon or evening; end times are TBD, because Luma required an end time and the listings show the start time in that field.

A builder night is an evening gathering for builders: a chance to meet, talk about what you are working on, find collaborators, and get help on something stuck. The two Nights & Weekends builder nights — NOLA on Feb 17 and Prairie on Apr 8, both at 10:30pm Central — are the late-night version of the format, for people who build after hours. They are explained in more detail on the builder-night page. The online Build Night on Dec 3 is the onboarding track’s version of the same idea, aimed at shipping a first project.

A pitch night is an evening for presenting what was built: short presentations, in front of the room, with the work itself doing most of the talking. The Red River Pitch Night on Apr 15 is the tour’s pitch night. No judging panel, prize, or investment outcome is named on the listing, and none is claimed here.

A kickoff, a toolkit session, a coffee meetup, and a skills day round out the tour. The Season Opening Kickoff on Jan 8 opens the tour season. The Crescent City Founder Toolkit on Feb 11 is a founder-focused session, named for the practical tools founders need. Heartland Builders & Coffee on Feb 18 is a daytime meetup over coffee. BuildFest Skills & Certs Day on Apr 22 is a day for learning and certifying practical skills. In each case the name is the description: no agenda, speakers, or curriculum have been published, and none are described here.

The arc: November to May

The season has a shape, and it is worth seeing whole. November and December 2026 are the online season: five Thursday evenings to welcome the community, meet each other, ship a first project, learn about sponsors, training and incubators, and prepare for January. January 2027 opens the road season with a kickoff on Friday the 8th and the first build weekend, January 9–11. February brings a founder toolkit session, a late-night builder meetup, and a daytime coffee meetup. March brings two more build weekends. April brings a second late-night builder meetup, a pitch night, and a skills and certs day. May 1 closes the season with the Grand Finale, a Saturday afternoon event from 1:00 to 5:00pm Central. The Grand Finale closes the tour; it is not a site launch, and no site launch is part of the tour.

The season in one line: online in November and December, on the road from January to May, closing with the Grand Finale on May 1, 2027.
