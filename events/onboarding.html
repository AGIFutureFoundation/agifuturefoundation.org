Why onboarding comes first

A tour that starts on the road starts cold: strangers meeting for the first time in a room they have never been in, trying to build together with no shared context. Onboarding exists so that does not happen. The five online nights run in November and December 2026, before the tour season opens in January 2027, and their job is to form the community while everyone is still at home. By the time the Season Opening Kickoff arrives on January 8, the people in the room should already know each other’s names, know what each other is working on, and know how the tour works. That is the whole purpose of the track, and it is why all five nights are online: online is where the most people can show up, from San Francisco and from the Midwest alike, with no travel and no venue to arrange.

The order of the five nights is deliberate, and it is worth following in order if you can. The welcome night opens the season and explains the tour. The community night introduces the people — SF and Midwest builders meeting each other. The build night gets everyone working on something real, before the tour, so that the first build weekend in January is not anyone’s first time shipping. The info night covers the practical questions: sponsors, training, and incubators. And the pre-season prep night, a week before Christmas, is the last check-in before January: what to bring, what to expect, and how the first tour events will run. Each night stands on its own, so missing one does not lock you out of the rest — but the sequence is the intended path from newcomer to tour-ready.

Who should come

The onboarding track is for the same two groups the whole tour is for. The first is the travelling group: SF founders, builders, hackers and startup teams who are considering joining the tour on the road. The second is the local group: Midwest builders, students and talent who live where the tour will stop. Both groups belong in the onboarding nights, because the community the nights are forming is the community that will be building together from January on.

Within those groups, the nights are open to a wide range of experience, and no background is assumed. A founder with a team and a roadmap, a hacker with a weekend project, a student who has never shipped anything, and a local builder with a product already in the world are all in the same room, and the formats are built around doing the work rather than around credentials. If you are not sure whether the tour is for you, the onboarding nights are the low-commitment way to find out: they are online, they are ninety minutes, and they are the place to ask questions before January. There is no published application or selection process for the onboarding track, and no attendee list to join — the sign-up route is the Luma link for each night.

How the unlisted sign-up works

All five onboarding nights are Private/unlisted on Luma. That means they do not appear in Luma’s public listings, they are not searchable on the platform, and no invitations have been sent. Each event is reachable only through its own link, and the link is the sign-up route: open the link, sign up on Luma, and you are in. The links on this page are those links, and they are the only way in at this stage.

Because the events are unlisted, there is no public directory of BuilderSpace events to browse, and no registration counts or attendee numbers are published. This page does not estimate them. If you have a link, you can sign up for that night; if you want to bring someone else, share the link with them — that is how an unlisted event spreads. The same arrangement applies to the eleven tour events: unlisted, link-reachable, no invites sent. Questions about sign-up go to x@agifuturefoundation.org.

What to expect from an onboarding night

Each onboarding night runs ninety minutes, 6:00–7:30pm Central, which is 4:00–5:30pm Pacific — an after-work slot on the West Coast and an evening slot in the Midwest. All five are online, so the practicalities are the practicalities of any online evening event: a link, a device, and ninety minutes. There is no venue to travel to, no ticket to print, and no dress code to worry about. Bring whatever you are working on for the build night; for the other nights, bring yourself and your questions.

What you will not find at an onboarding night is a detailed agenda published in advance. The descriptions on this page are drawn from the event names only, because no agendas, speakers, or running orders have been published for any of the five nights. That is a deliberate choice on this page, not an oversight: an invented agenda would tell people to expect things that may not happen. When agendas, speakers, or materials are published, this page will be updated with them.

The onboarding track ends on December 17, 2026. The tour track opens three weeks later, on Friday, January 8, 2027, with the Season Opening Kickoff, followed by the first build weekend, January 9–11. The eleven tour events run through May 1, 2027, closing with the Grand Finale. All eleven are Private/unlisted on Luma, like the onboarding nights, and all eleven have locations TBD — no venue has been confirmed for any of them.
