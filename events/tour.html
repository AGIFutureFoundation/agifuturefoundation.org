TL;DR

The tour track is eleven events from January to May 2027, all in Central time, all with locations TBD. It is the road season of BuilderSpace: the San Francisco startup community travelling to the Midwest — areas with new data centers and jobs — to build, ship, hire, and connect with local Midwest builders, students and talent. The Jan–May arc

The tour season has a shape, and it is worth seeing whole before reading the individual events. January opens the season: a Friday-evening kickoff on January 8, then the first build weekend, Friday to Sunday, January 9–11. February slows the pace and widens the circle: a founder toolkit session on February 11, a late-night builder meetup on February 17, and a daytime coffee meetup on February 18. March is the heavy building month, with two multi-day build weekends — March 5–7 and March 26–28. April brings the season toward its close: a second late-night builder meetup on April 8, a pitch night on April 15, and a skills and certs day on April 22. May 1 closes it with the Grand Finale, a Saturday afternoon event from 1:00 to 5:00pm Central. That is the whole arc, and every date in it is as listed on Luma.

The arc is also a progression in what the tour asks of the people on it. January is about starting: showing up, meeting the community that formed online in November and December, and building something in the first weekend. February and March are about doing the work repeatedly, in different formats and different places, with different people in the room each time. April is about showing the work — a pitch night, a skills day — and May 1 is about closing the season together. None of that is an agenda for any single event; it is the shape of the season as a whole, read from the event names and dates alone.

What a tour stop involves, in general terms

No detailed agenda has been published for any tour event, so what a tour stop involves can only be described in general terms, from the event names and formats themselves. A tour stop is a working gathering, not a conference: people come to build, to meet other builders, and to show what they made. The formats repeat across the season because they are the working formats of a builder community, and each one is described below in general terms only.

A build weekend is a multi-day stretch — Friday to Sunday on this tour — set aside for building. Teams and individuals work on a project, check in with each other as they go, and show what they made at the end. The tour has three: the Bayou AI Build Weekend (Jan 9–11), the Cajun Code Sprint (Mar 5–7), and the Prairie AgTech Build Weekend (Mar 26–28). Each starts on a Friday afternoon or evening; end times are TBD, because Luma required an end time and the listings show the start time in that field.

A builder night is an evening gathering for builders: a chance to meet, talk about what you are working on, find collaborators, and get help on something stuck. The two Nights & Weekends builder nights — NOLA on Feb 17 and Prairie on Apr 8, both at 10:30pm Central — are the late-night version of the format, for people who build after hours. They are explained in more detail on the builder-night page.

A pitch night is an evening for presenting what was built: short presentations, in front of the room, with the work itself doing most of the talking. The Red River Pitch Night on Apr 15 is the tour’s pitch night. No judging panel, prize, or investment outcome is named on the listing, and none is claimed here.

A kickoff, a toolkit session, a coffee meetup, and a skills day round out the tour. The Season Opening Kickoff on Jan 8 opens the tour season. The Crescent City Founder Toolkit on Feb 11 is a founder-focused session, named for the practical tools founders need. Heartland Builders & Coffee on Feb 18 is a daytime meetup over coffee. BuildFest Skills & Certs Day on Apr 22 is a day for learning and certifying practical skills. In each case the name is the description: no agenda, speakers, or curriculum have been published, and none are described here.
