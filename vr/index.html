Inside the Holodeck — the environment behind the tour

The deepest environment this tour points into is the SmartCiti.X Holodeck (S10): 8 districts, 111 union halls, the Simulator Yard with 11 trade simulators at its centre, nine scripted advisors, and a low-poly building kit the halls are built from. The numbers below come from the Trade Craft Academy specification (ACP v3.2) and the Holodeck fabric (my-fabric/holodeck.json). SmartCiti.X itself is proposed, not formed, funded, or offered.

Meet the guides — nine scripted advisors

A hall is a building until somebody in it will answer a question. Nine advisors stand in the rooms and on the campus green, each one of the Academy's own rigged avatars — breathing, turning its head toward you — and each able to answer exactly 35 questions between them. An advisor is a scripted guide, not an instructor and not an AI: no model runs behind one, and nothing is generated at view time. An answer is either say (written and cited) or read (bound to a live record, such as the PPE conditions, a seat rubric, or a hall's rooms). Twenty-one of the 35 topics carry no words. Advisors are not scored, and no grader reads advisor state.

  • 🧭 Orientation guide — hall door — “What is this hall?”
  • 🦺 Safety steward — safety room — PPE, hazards, light, air, noise — “Can I refuse the work?”
  • 🧰 Crib keeper — tools room
  • 📐 Layout hand — layout room — control points
  • 🔍 Inspector — inspection room — “Can I talk my way to a pass?”
  • 📋 Foreman — coordination room — shift brief, walkaround, hand-offs
  • 🗂️ Records clerk — documentation room — progress, certification, school partners
  • 👷 Operator — at the running seat's own yard — task, controls, gauges, rubric, walkaround, reference procedure
  • 📡 Dispatcher — campus green — campus districts, other campuses, nearby anchors; 10- and 15-minute walk bands (800 m / 1,200 m)

How the halls are built — the building kit

The halls are assembled from an 18-piece low-poly building vocabulary — 43 primitives (36 boxes + 7 cylinders), 12–72 triangles per piece (median 34; the whole vocabulary 556 triangles). A hall envelope is 12.0 m wide, 6 + (depth % 3) × 0.7 m high, with its door toward local −z. Measured reference: one residential city block, 21,893 triangles / 93 meshes.

  • Entrance stoop
  • Entrance tread
  • Kerb run
  • Parapet coping run
  • Parapet corner block
  • Eaves gutter
  • Downpipe run
  • Downpipe shoe
  • Wall vent hood
  • Louvred vent panel
  • Door awning
  • Bollard
  • Wall-mounted hand rail

Interiors follow the same discipline: six authored custom spaces — a welding bay, a rigging yard, an electrical lab, a scaffold court, a flipped classroom, a crane simulator bay — with floor plans drawn at 40 px/m; 29 interior props over 7 families, 5,082 instances across 1,221 rooms; wall runs first, then authored islands with a 1.5 m aisle; and the 11 room strands mirror the 11 skill strands.

About this VR entry

IWSDK-patterned, not IWSDK-built

This scene follows IWSDK patterns — a session entry button, a local-floor reference space, waypoint gates, and tap-to-select — using a vendored Three.js build (assets/js/vendor/three.min.js, r152). It does not import @iwsdk/core and is not presented as built with IWSDK. A full IWSDK app does not fit this static site.

Three modes, one tour

Headset: WebXR immersive-vr where the browser reports a headset. Desktop: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, arrow keys and 1/2/3 step keys. Mobile: drag, tap-to-select, pinch to zoom, pixel ratio capped for mobile GPUs, buttons do everything the scene does.

Honest limits

This entry was verified on a local dev server in a browser without WebGL, so the 3D render itself still needs a real desktop browser and a real phone to confirm, and the headset path still needs a real Quest. The 2D fallback above is the complete tour when 3D cannot run.
