SmartCiti.X: the first planned studio raise

SmartCiti.X is planned as the first portfolio company of the agent-managed venture studio: robotics training, union-trades training with augmented and virtual reality, and robotics manufacturing, with first sites in Louisiana and Indiana. It is planned as a new, separate corporation — not as a series of an LLC. The corporation has not been formed, no money has been raised, and no site, union, or customer has signed. This page sets out the plan exactly as drafted, with every figure labeled as suggested and every gap stated plainly.

A new corporation — and what changed

SmartCiti.X is now planned as a new, separate corporation — its own legal entity, its own charter and bylaws, its own EIN, its own bank account, and its own cap table — not solely as W3 LLC Series 10. Earlier paperwork presented SmartCiti.X as Series 10, and that issuer is superseded. The corporation is the current planned issuer for the SmartCiti.X raise. Series 10 remains only as a name on the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and counsel must decide in writing what happens to it: leave it dormant or unused, wind it down if it was ever designated, transfer any Series 10 intellectual property or contracts to the new corporation by written assignment if any exist — none are evidenced in the files — or keep Series 10 as a separate operating series with a separate budget. The same $10,000,000 must never be raised through both the corporation and Series 10, and Series 10 and the corporation must never be presented as the same entity to a bank, an investor, or a regulator. Anyone who saw the earlier Series 10 plan must be told the issuer changed, in writing, in the formal documents.

The proposed legal name is SmartCiti.X, Inc., with the exact filed name to be cleared first: a name search and a trademark search come before any filing, because the brand name is not the same as a cleared corporate name, and the period in the name may need to be handled under state filing rules, as counsel confirms. The recommended state of incorporation in the plan is Delaware, as a C-corporation. The alternative is a Wyoming corporation, to match AGI Corp's intended Wyoming head. Counsel must choose one state in writing, weighing investor familiarity, franchise tax, foreign qualification in California, Louisiana, and Indiana, and SAFE compatibility. The corporation must not be incorporated in both states. Whether it is a standard corporation or a benefit corporation is a separate choice counsel must make in writing; it must not be called a benefit corporation unless it is actually filed as one.

The three products

1 · Robotics training

Hands-on industrial and service robotics training — safety, maintenance, and programming — for workers, students, and career changers. Curriculum plus simulator plus instructor-led delivery, with human instructors as the credentialing authority. Agents build content and simulators; they do not certify a tradesperson.

2 · Union-trades AR/VR training

Augmented and virtual reality training for union trades. The trade list is to be confirmed with union partners — no trade list is promised until a union agreement is signed. Modules are built to trade standards and safety standards, with human instructors and union journeymen as the credentialing authority.

3 · Robotics manufacturing

Setting up robotics manufacturing: assembly, integration, testing, and small-batch production first. Full manufacturing only if capital and orders support it — the board must confirm the scope, and a full factory must not be promised on a first raise.

Suggested figures — planning only

The suggested pre-money valuation is $25,000,000, for the SmartCiti.X corporation only — not AGI Corp, not W3, and not a family valuation. The suggested total raise is $10,000,000, in tranches built into the plan. Tranche 1 is a $5,000,000 first close, as a seed SAFE or priced round, with counsel to choose one instrument and one cap or price. The suggested SAFE cap, if a SAFE is used, is $25,000,000 post-money — a planning figure only. Tranche 1 would fund site search and diligence and first-site deposits and closing costs, the first phase of the augmented and virtual reality curriculum build, robotics training pilot setup, manufacturing pilot setup, legal, compliance, and accounting, and working capital. Its closing conditions are: the corporation formed, board authorization signed, intellectual property assigned, bank account and accounting live, formal documents approved by counsel, and accredited-investor verification under Regulation D, or the platform form for a Regulation CF tranche.

Tranche 2 is a $5,000,000 milestone close, not a date close. It releases only when all five milestone gates are certified by the board in writing. If a gate is missed, Tranche 2 does not release automatically — the board and the investors, as the signed documents provide, decide whether to waive, extend, re-price, or cancel Tranche 2. A date alone never releases Tranche 2. The price for Tranche 2 must be chosen by counsel and the board and stated in the Tranche 1 documents — fixed at the Tranche 1 price, capped at a stated figure, or re-priced at the milestone valuation — or Tranche 2 will be a dispute. A Series A priced round is a future, separate round, with its price set then and not today; no Series A price, timing, or investor may be promised in Tranche 1 documents.

Proposed use of the $10,000,000

The proposed use, which the board must set finally, is: $4,000,000 for robotics manufacturing setup — equipment, integration, testing, and small-batch production first; $3,000,000 for Louisiana and Indiana real-estate acquisition and fit-out, covering the first sites' acquisition costs and not an unlimited portfolio; $2,000,000 for union-trades augmented and virtual reality training build; and $1,000,000 for working capital, legal, compliance, accounting, and Windfall and benefit reporting. No use-of-proceeds line may be spent on another entity's costs without a written, disclosed inter-entity agreement and board approval.

The five milestone gates, in full

Tranche 2 releases only when the board certifies in writing that every one of these gates has been met. The list below is the plan, not a signed gate; the board must set the exact list before any solicitation.

  1. Signed union partnership agreement. The union name is blank — no union is named as a partner until an agreement is signed. Human instructors and journeymen are the credentialing authority, and agents do not certify a tradesperson.
  2. Augmented and virtual reality curriculum pilot delivered. A pilot cohort completed, with the curriculum and simulator delivered — not merely built in a demo.
  3. First site closed or leased. A signed purchase or lease, not a site tour or a letter of intent alone — unless the board expressly accepts a letter of intent plus a deposit as a partial gate and states so in writing.
  4. First manufacturing order or pilot customer signed. A paying pilot. A non-binding interest letter is not a pilot customer.
  5. Financial reporting. Tranche 1 use of proceeds reconciled to the bank and accounting, with no commingling across entities, and a Windfall and benefit report drafted — even if the Windfall threshold is not yet triggered, because reporting discipline starts at Tranche 1.

Real estate — built into the plan, with no site named

The geographies for the plan are Louisiana, the tour and BuilderSpace geography, and Indiana, the SmartCiti Holodeck anchor geography. The anchor coordinates, 39.846, -86.622, mark a world location, not a site purchase. No site, city, address, price, seller, broker, or landlord is named anywhere in the plan, because none is signed.

The site program runs in phases. Phase R0, criteria and search, runs under Tranche 1 before any deposit: board-approved criteria covering workforce access, manufacturing suitability, training suitability, cost discipline inside the $3,000,000 first-sites envelope, and legal diligence. Phase R1, diligence, runs under Tranche 1 before closing: title, survey, zoning, environmental Phase I and Phase II if recommended, independent appraisal, building inspection, insurance quote, and a lease-versus-purchase analysis per site. No site closes without R1 complete; a skipped item must be waived in writing by the board, with the risk stated. Phase R2, acquisition structure, is a board, counsel, and lender decision per site: purchase outright, financed purchase, or lease with an option to purchase. The first raise funds site acquisition costs for the first Louisiana and Indiana sites only. Phase R3, fit-out and opening, follows closing or lease, with permits and inspections before opening and safety and insurance in place before any trainee or manufacturing work begins. Phase R4, the second site, runs under Tranche 2 only after the Tranche 2 gates, repeating R0 through R3 in full.

Real estate is owned, or leased, by SmartCiti.X Corporation itself, in its own name — not by Mike personally, not by AGI Corp, not by W3, and not by a nonprofit. Investors own shares in the corporation, not a parcel, not rent, and not a share of a site sale. The risks to be disclosed include: no site under contract; appraisal may come in below price; zoning may be denied or delayed; environmental issues may require remediation; fit-out costs may exceed budget; and a lender may decline financing. No site, rent, purchase price, or site pipeline may be presented as an asset in a valuation.

Share structure, Windfall Clause, and Open Global Investment

The share classes for the corporation follow the pitch standard: Class A common stock with one vote per share, broad public access, and full economic participation — the class SAFE holders convert into and the class priced investors buy, unless counsel creates a preferred class for a priced round, in which case the preferred terms must be drafted and the Class A, B, and C rights restated in the charter. Class B has ten votes per share and is restricted to aligned partners and sovereign entities meeting strict safety and geopolitical frameworks, with both economic participation and voting, subject to screening. Class C has one thousand votes per share, is held exclusively by the Foundation as the ethical backstop, and is control only, with no economic participation. Founders must not be issued Class C; founders' economics are common or Class A stock plus any founder preferred stock counsel drafts, with vesting over four years and a one-year cliff.

The Windfall Clause would be incorporated into the charter and bylaws and into every SAFE and preferred agreement for the corporation. Its threshold is a percentage of Global GDP, with the measure, the profit measure, and the scope blank for counsel — the scope is this corporation's profits, unless counsel expressly expands it to affiliates. Its Windfall Share above the threshold is blank for counsel, as are its administrator and segregated Windfall account, with the money used only for the benefit buckets: Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for projects advancing the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. The clause may be amended only by a supermajority plus a Class C vote, and whether it survives a sale, merger, or change of control must be confirmed in writing by counsel. The clause reduces the profits available to shareholders above the threshold, by design; it is not a promise of any economic outcome. The pitch figures sometimes quoted alongside it — Gini figures of about 0.96 and about 0.89, and the phrase "a massive portion" — are pitch illustrations, not verified data.

Fundraising route — as the plan states it

The instrument is a YC-style post-money SAFE, adapted to this corporation, for a Regulation D raise — or priced seed preferred stock, with counsel to choose one per tranche. As the plan states it, Regulation D for the full $10,000,000, for accredited investors, by SAFE or priced stock, is the cleanest route for the full amount. The alternative stated in the plan is a $5,000,000 Regulation D first close plus a separate Regulation CF tranche of up to $5,000,000 on a platform, with a Form C for the Regulation CF tranche as a separate set of documents. Regulation CF alone cannot raise $10,000,000, because its cap is $5,000,000. A Regulation A+ round is a later route, not part of the $10,000,000 plan. That is what the plan states; it is not advice from this page, and counsel must choose one exemption per tranche. The formal documents must disclose the corporation's formation status, the Series 10 to corporation issuer change, the Windfall blanks and the Class A, B, and C rights and sovereign screening, the real-estate status with no site under contract, the union and pilot status with no union or customer signed, corporate tax treatment with no K-1 form for shareholders, and founder vesting and the fully diluted cap table.

What is still missing

Stated plainly: the corporation is not formed; no state, no benefit-corporation choice, no Tranche 1 instrument, no Tranche 2 price mechanism, and no Series 10 disposition has been chosen in writing; no board has been appointed; no intellectual property assignment has been signed; no bank account has been opened; no site is under contract; no union agreement is signed; no pilot customer is signed; and no revenue has been evidenced. The $25,000,000 and $10,000,000 figures are suggested planning figures only. The governance of the plan, the share structure, and the Windfall Clause are described on the governance pages, and the sequence of steps before any raise is on the process page.
