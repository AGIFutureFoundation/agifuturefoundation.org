What this section is — and what it is not

The AGI Future Foundation is a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation. Its filed purpose is safe, equitable, and socially beneficial artificial intelligence, and research and coordination toward that end. Around that mission, a venture studio model has been designed on paper: a studio function that sources, validates, builds, launches, and operates new projects, each held in its own legal series or corporation, with human stewards deciding and agents doing the day-to-day work. The corporate paperwork for that model exists as drafts. The filings, the bank accounts, the signed agreements, and the formal documents that a real raise would need do not all exist yet. This section describes the design honestly, with the filed facts separated from the plans.

What this section is

Background information about a planned venture studio, a planned first raise, planned series raises, and the steps that must come first. Every figure on these pages is a suggested planning figure unless the page says it is a verified filing fact. Every plan is labeled as a plan.

What this section is not

It is not an offer to sell securities. It is not a price, a term sheet, or a promise of any economic outcome. No shares, SAFEs, notes, tokens, or interests of any kind are being sold through this site, and no money is being accepted here.

Who this section is for

People who want to understand the model before any formal process opens — prospective accredited investors, partners, researchers, and donors who want to know what is real today and what is still only planned. Donors belong on a different track: the Foundation, as filed, can receive donations and grants only, and cannot sell equity of any kind.

The studio at a glance

The studio is a function of AGI Corp, the intended Wyoming head corporation — not a separate legal entity, unless counsel advises forming one. AGI Corp itself has an EIN only; no Wyoming articles of incorporation were in the files reviewed, so its incorporation is unverified and must not be assumed. The studio's portfolio is the W3 LLC Wyoming series network plus the Delaware cohort series (Cohort 1, Series 1–3, known as C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3). Each project lives in its own series or corporation, with its own books, its own bank account, and its own Steward Council of three consenting humans. The Foundation acts as mission steward: every studio project passes a Foundation mission-fit gate, and the Foundation can withdraw mission approval for a project. The AGI Fellowship is a separate California church and a partner — never a portfolio company, never a series, and never part of any raise.

The studio works in two modes, used deliberately. In agent-managed mode, agents run the day-to-day work of a series — drafting, research, building, coordination, monitoring, and reporting — within written limits set by humans. In agent-assisted mode, a human founder, steward, or manager does the work with agents as co-workers. In both modes, an agent never signs a legal document, moves funds, hires or fires, offers securities, or enacts doctrine or charter changes on its own. Every such act requires a human with signed authority, recorded in minutes. Agents hold no equity, no tokens, and no profit interests. A project moves through six stage gates — Idea, Validate, Build, Launch, Operate and Scale, Exit or Wind-down — and a gate is passed only when a human approves it and the evidence is filed. An agent's "ready" flag is not a gate pass. Full detail is on the studio page.

The suggested planning figures for the studio itself are a $20,000,000 suggested pre-money valuation for AGI Corp as studio, and a $2,000,000 suggested studio seed. Both figures are suggested planning figures only — not prices, not appraisals, not terms. The studio seed would fund studio operations such as sourcing, gate reviews, legal and compliance work, the chief financial officer search, and series designations. It would not be double-counted as SmartCiti.X project money. Counsel must choose one structure for the same dollars, not both.

The first planned raise: SmartCiti.X

SmartCiti.X is planned as the first studio portfolio raise, and it is planned as a new, separate corporation — SmartCiti.X, Inc. — with its own charter, bylaws, EIN, bank account, and cap table. That is a change from earlier paperwork, which presented SmartCiti.X as W3 LLC Series 10. The corporation is the current planned issuer; Series 10 remains only as a name on the April 2026 internal series list, and the same $10,000,000 must never be raised through both the corporation and Series 10. The corporation has not been formed. No state of incorporation has been finally chosen: Delaware C-corporation is recommended in the plan, Wyoming is the alternative, and counsel must choose one in writing. Whether it is a standard corporation or a benefit corporation is also a choice counsel must make in writing.

Suggested figures — planning only

$25,000,000 suggested pre-money for the SmartCiti.X corporation only. $10,000,000 suggested total raise, tranched: $5,000,000 first close, then $5,000,000 milestone close. The second tranche releases only when the board certifies in writing that every milestone gate has been met. A date alone never releases it, and the second-tranche price must be stated in the first-tranche documents.

What the money would build

Three products: hands-on robotics training; union-trades training delivered with augmented and virtual reality; and robotics manufacturing, starting with assembly, integration, testing, and small-batch production. Full manufacturing only if capital and orders support it — a full factory is not promised on a first raise.

Proposed use of the $10,000,000

$4,000,000 for robotics manufacturing setup; $3,000,000 for first Louisiana and Indiana site acquisition and fit-out; $2,000,000 for union-trades augmented and virtual reality training; $1,000,000 for working capital, legal, compliance, accounting, and Windfall and benefit reporting. These are proposed figures the board must set finally.

No site, city, address, price, seller, broker, or landlord is named anywhere in the plan, because none is signed. No union is named as a partner, because no union agreement is signed. No pilot customer is named, because none is signed. There is no revenue evidenced for SmartCiti.X. The planned route for the full $10,000,000, as stated in the corporate plan, is Regulation D, for accredited investors — described as the cleanest route — with an alternative of a $5,000,000 Regulation D first close plus a separate Regulation CF tranche of up to $5,000,000. That is a statement of what the plan says, not advice from this page. Full detail, including the five milestone gates in full, is on the SmartCiti.X page.

Later series raises

After the first raise, other series would raise one at a time, each for its own project, each with its own documents, cap table, and bank account. The suggested range for each other W3 series is $100,000 to $500,000 for a first series, and the same $100,000 to $500,000 range is suggested for each Delaware cohort series, C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3. These are suggested ranges only. Funds are never pooled across series by default: money raised by one series stays in that series unless a signed agreement and counsel's advice expressly permit a specific transfer, minuted, priced, and reported. The issuer for a series raise is that series' legal name — or AGI Corp, if AGI Corp is the issuer for that round — and counsel chooses one issuer per round. Equity and SAFE raises are for accredited investors, under Regulation D, with a Form D filing. Customers come first: a paying customer validates a series better than any deck, and a genuine customer contract is not a securities sale. Full detail is on the series raises page.

These figures must never be added together into a single family total. Each raise has a different issuer, different documents, different eligibility, and different risks. In particular, the Foundation's suggested $50,000,000 figure and SmartCiti.X's suggested $25,000,000 figure must never be added to claim a $75,000,000 family valuation — that would double-count control, management, and series value, and it would fail diligence.

The process, in order

The recommended sequence, from the corporate raise advice, is: clean up first, then donations, then the AGI Corp SAFE, then the first series raise. Clean-up means confirming Secretary of State and Attorney General status for the Foundation and the Fellowship, searching for AGI Corp, W3, and Delaware filings, resolving the Russell Vacanti consent, seating third directors, and opening separate bank accounts per entity. Donation rounds for the Foundation and the Fellowship can open before any equity round, with counsel-approved language and separate accounts — and they dilute no one, because a nonprofit has no equity to dilute. The AGI Corp incorporation and SAFE authorization would come next, if counsel advises AGI Corp as the first issuer. Only then would a first series be designated and its raise opened — one series, one set of documents, one cap table — to prove the series model with one clean closing before a second series round opens. The Open Global Investment program and the Windfall Clause come only after counsel drafts signed terms for them, never as a promise inside an earlier SAFE or equity document. Full detail is on the process page.

The fork that decides everything

There are two different things that the words "AGI Future Foundation PBC" can mean, and they must not be confused. As filed and verified, the Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, Entity B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24. A nonprofit with that filing cannot sell equity, SAFEs, profit shares, or tokens, and cannot distribute profits. The Foundation site, by contrast, has used "PBC" language describing a for-profit benefit corporation with dual-class equity and profit participation. Those are not the same entity type. The suggested $50,000,000 Foundation valuation and $5,000,000 first raise apply only to a for-profit benefit corporation track — a new for-profit corporation, or a conversion counsel advises, or a raise run through AGI Corp instead. On the nonprofit track as filed, there is no valuation at all, and the raise is a donation and grant target of $250,000 to $750,000 in the first twelve months, by program. Counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Until then, do not run both tracks for the same money, to the same investors, on different terms. The share structure designed for the for-profit track — Class A with one vote and full economic participation, Class B with ten votes restricted to aligned partners and screened sovereign entities, and Class C with one thousand votes held exclusively by the Foundation for control only, with no economic participation — is described on the share structure page and the governance page.
