The process, in order — and the gates at every step

Nothing described on this site can be funded until a sequence of prior steps is complete. The sequence is deliberate: clean up the filings first, then open donation rounds, then authorize the studio's first SAFE, then designate and raise for one series — and only one series — at a time. Counsel gates every step. This page walks the sequence in order, names the documents and decisions each step requires, explains the fork in the road that decides the Foundation's track, and explains what an expression of interest is and is not.

The sequence at a glance

The sequence comes from the corporate raise advice, and it is recommended for all three entities together — Foundation, Fellowship, and the W3 series. The order protects the nonprofits from being used to sell securities, protects the series from being funded before they exist as legal entities, and protects anyone considering a future step from being asked to decide on the basis of an unfinished file.

Step 1 — Clean up first

Clean-up is measured in weeks, if the filings are straightforward, and it comes before any solicitation of any kind. For the Foundation, clean-up means confirming Secretary of State good standing and a current Statement of Information — the initial statement was due around 2026-07-23 and its status was unverified — and confirming Attorney General Registry registration, the correct tax return position with an accountant, signed bylaws and board consent with Mike Rice, Russell Vacanti, whose consent is pending, and a third director, a donor receipt template with no deductibility claim until an IRS determination letter exists, a grant agreement template, a separate bank account for Foundation donations only, and fundraising materials reviewed by counsel. For the Fellowship, clean-up means confirming Secretary of State good standing and a current Statement of Information — due around 2026-08-11, status unverified — and confirming the Attorney General and IRS posture, signed bylaws and board consent, partnership agreements signed before any support money moves, and a separate bank account. For the corporate side, clean-up means a Secretary of State search for AGI Corp, W3, and the Delaware master, Russell Vacanti's consent, third directors, and bank accounts per entity. No account opening has been verified yet for any entity.

Step 2 — Donation rounds open

Foundation and Fellowship donation rounds can open before any equity round, with counsel-approved language and separate accounts, and they dilute no one, because a nonprofit has no equity to dilute. The Foundation, as filed, can raise by unrestricted donations, restricted donations and grants for a named program under a written grant agreement, government and institutional grants subject to each grantor's eligibility rules — many require 501(c)(3) status, which is not yet evidenced — and sponsorships for charitable programs only, without giving the sponsor control. The Foundation cannot sell shares, SAFEs, convertible notes, tokens, profit participation, Class A shares, or Open Global Investment interests; cannot promise tax deductibility until an IRS determination letter exists; cannot promise any economic outcome to a donor; cannot use donations to fund AGI Corp, a W3 series, or a SAFE round except as a written grant serving a charitable purpose, approved by disinterested directors, with reporting back; and cannot solicit in California before Attorney General Registry registration is confirmed. The suggested nonprofit donation target is $250,000 to $750,000 in the first twelve months, by program.

The Fellowship can raise by donations, tithes, and offerings for religious purposes, grants from foundations that fund religious organizations — a narrower set than for the Foundation, checked per grantor — program fees for religious programs only if counsel confirms they do not create unrelated business income or private benefit issues, and support from partner corporations only under written partnership and support agreements, with an independence clause, minuted as support rather than ownership or control. The Fellowship cannot sell equity, SAFEs, tokens, profit shares, or membership interests; cannot promise tax deductibility until counsel confirms the church's IRS posture in writing; cannot allow a donor, partner corporation, or series to buy control over doctrine, minister appointment, membership, or church assets; cannot use Fellowship donations to fund AGI Corp, a W3 series, or a SAFE round except on counsel's written advice that a specific transfer serves a religious purpose at fair value — the default is no; and cannot engage in political campaign activity or substantial lobbying. The suggested Fellowship donation target is $50,000 to $250,000 in the first twelve months, by religious program. The Fellowship has no valuation, no equity, and no cap.

Step 3 — AGI Corp incorporation and SAFE authorization

The studio's first equity or SAFE vehicle, if counsel advises AGI Corp rather than a series as the first issuer, is the AGI Corp SAFE. That step requires Wyoming incorporation and good standing, an EIN matching the Secretary of State name, a bank account, and board authorization with the cap, discount, and round size filled in — not left as "standard terms". The SAFE form in the paperwork is a post-money YC-style SAFE, with a board authorization and term sheet draft, and a setup checklist. Every term is a blank for the board and counsel to set. The suggested planning figures for the studio are a $20,000,000 suggested pre-money valuation and a $2,000,000 suggested studio seed, and the for-profit PBC track, as an alternative issuer, carries a suggested Tranche 1 SAFE range of $500,000 to $1,500,000, a suggested post-money cap of $8,000,000 to $15,000,000, and a discount of twenty percent or none, with one choice made. AGI Corp and the for-profit PBC are alternative issuers, not both at once for the same round. The SAFE gates are strict by design: the issuer must be a formed for-profit; the board must authorize the round; counsel must review the documents; the investors must be accredited; the exemption must be Regulation D; and a Form D must be filed within fifteen days of the first sale. A studio project that is still at the "Proposed" stage cannot take investment, and agents do not solicit investment.

Step 4 — First series designation and first series raise

Only after clean-up, donations, and the AGI Corp decision does a first series get designated and its raise opened: one series, one set of formal documents, one cap table. The purpose of doing one series first is to prove the series model with one clean closing before a second series round opens. For SmartCiti.X, the issuer is the new SmartCiti.X corporation rather than Series 10, as described on the SmartCiti.X page, and the corporation must be formed, with its board, bank account, intellectual property assignment, accounting, and cap table, before its first close. For any other first series, designation requires the master LLC filed and its operating agreement signed, the series designated in the appendix with purpose, manager, Steward Council of three consenting humans, and segregated books and account, a cap table and intellectual property chain, a budget and use of proceeds, counsel-prepared disclosure and purchase documents, board and Council authorization with all numbers filled in, a Form D and state notice calendar, and a bank account in that series' legal name. The first series raise is for accredited investors, under Regulation D, with a Form D within fifteen days of the first sale, and a paying pilot customer is sought before or alongside the raise.

Step 5 — Subsequent series raises

Subsequent series raises follow one at a time, each with its own formal documents, cap table, and account. Three series rounds must never be opened on one deck. Each later series carries the suggested range of $100,000 to $500,000 for a first round, as described on the series raises page, and each is priced on its own diligence at the time. Funds are never pooled across series by default, and the issuer for each round is that series' legal name, or AGI Corp if AGI Corp is the issuer for that round, with counsel choosing one issuer per round.

Step 6 — Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause come only after counsel drafts signed terms for them, and only as a separate, expressly disclosed program — never as a promise inside an earlier SAFE or equity document. The Windfall Clause, as drafted to the pitch standard, is a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating the 33 W3 series, with a threshold expressed as a percentage of Global GDP, a Windfall Share above the threshold, benefit buckets for Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, and amendment only by a supermajority plus a Class C vote. Every operative term is blank for counsel. The Open Global Investment program likewise has no terms today, and no pitch figure — including the Gini illustrations of about 0.96 and about 0.89 — may be quoted as verified data or as a term.

The PBC fork — one track, chosen in writing

The fork decides everything, and it must be decided before any solicitation. "AGI Future Foundation PBC" is used in two different senses. As filed and verified, it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, Entity B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, with 501(c)(3) language in its purpose, an irrevocable dedication, and no private inurement. A nonprofit with that filing cannot sell equity, SAFEs, profit shares, or tokens, and cannot distribute profits. As labeled on the website, "PBC" has been used to describe a for-profit benefit corporation with dual-class equity, Class A profit participation, and Open Global Investment. Those are not the same entity type. The advice follows the filed status, because a court, the Attorney General, the IRS, and an investor's counsel will follow the filing, not the website label. Until counsel advises otherwise in writing, the Foundation must be treated as a nonprofit, and must not be pitched as an equity investment.

The three options counsel must choose among, in writing, are: form a new for-profit California benefit corporation, with a distinct Secretary of State filing, and run the raise through that new entity while the nonprofit is kept separate; convert or restructure the existing entity on counsel's advice, understanding that a nonprofit-to-for-profit conversion is a major Attorney General and IRS transaction, not a name change; or run the raise through AGI Corp in Wyoming instead, and keep the Foundation as a nonprofit mission steward. Until one of those is chosen in writing by counsel, the PBC raise structure must not be attached to the nonprofit Foundation, and both tracks must not be run for the same money, to the same people, on different terms. The for-profit track, if chosen, carries the suggested $50,000,000 pre-money valuation and $5,000,000 first raise, the three-class share structure, the Fiduciary Shield as expanded business-judgment protection — never described as immunity — an annual benefit report, and the Windfall Clause. The nonprofit track carries no valuation at all and a donation and grant target of $250,000 to $750,000.

Counsel gates at every step

Every step in the sequence has a counsel gate, and the gates are not formalities. Before any solicitation, counsel confirms the issuer and the track in writing. Before any SAFE or equity is issued, counsel reviews the formal documents, the disclosure documents carry risk factors including benefit-purpose risk, dual-class control risk, Windfall cost risk, and multi-entity risk, and the board authorizes the round with one cap, one discount, and one round size. Before any public statement about a raise, counsel approves the language. No general solicitation is used unless Regulation D Rule 506(c) and verification are expressly chosen. A Form D is filed within fifteen days of the first sale, with a state notice calendar. Related-party transactions — including any transaction with the nonprofit Foundation, the Fellowship, AGI Corp, W3, or any series — are disclosed, with recusal and disinterested approval.

What an interested person does now

There is no live raise, no formal documents, and no process to join today. The only step available now is to express interest by email to x@agifuturefoundation.org. An expression of interest is not a commitment, not a subscription, and not a purchase. It does not hold a place, does not set a price, does not create an obligation on either side, and does not entitle the sender to any document, term, or economic outcome. What it does is start a conversation: the Foundation can note the interest, and when — and only when — counsel has chosen an issuer and a track, the formal documents exist, and a round is authorized, the Foundation can contact the people who expressed interest through the proper channel, with accredited-investor checks first. Anyone who writes should say who they are, what they are interested in — the studio, SmartCiti.X, or a series — and how to reach them. No money should be sent, and no money will be accepted, in response to this page. Donors who want to support the mission should write separately about a donation or grant, and should expect counsel-approved donation language and a separate account, with no deductibility claim until an IRS determination letter exists.
