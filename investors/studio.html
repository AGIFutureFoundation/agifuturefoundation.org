Investor Relations — Information Only · The Studio

An agent-managed venture studio, governed by humans

The thesis is simple to state and hard to execute: use AI agents to do the day-to-day work of building many companies at once, while humans keep every decision that carries legal, financial, or moral weight. This page explains what the studio is, how it is designed to work, what it would cost to start, and what is still missing — in information-only terms.

What the studio is, legally

The studio is a function of AGI Corp, the intended Wyoming head corporation — not a separate legal entity, unless counsel advises forming a separate studio entity. That distinction matters. Contracts must not be signed in the name of "the Studio" alone; they are signed as AGI Corp, or as the specific series, as the contract requires. AGI Corp itself has an EIN only, dated 2026-05-16, with the legal name shown only as "AGI". No Wyoming articles of incorporation, entity number, bylaws, or cap table were in the files reviewed. An EIN alone does not prove incorporation, so AGI Corp must not be presented as incorporated in any state until Secretary of State articles are produced. The studio therefore rests, today, on an intended head corporation whose formation is unverified — and any reader of this page should understand that the studio is a designed operating model, not a filed company with a track record.

The studio's portfolio is the W3 LLC Wyoming series network plus the Delaware cohort series — Cohort 1, Series 1 through 3, known as C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3. A studio project that is not in a series is not in the portfolio; it is an idea, not a company. Each series is a separate cell with separate assets, liabilities, books, and bank account, if separateness is maintained. The W3 series list is an internal April 2026 printout naming 33 sub-series, Series 1 through 33 — not a Wyoming filing, not a designation, and not evidence of a bank account, EIN, intellectual property, or council for any series. The Delaware cohort series are labels in a register, not yet legal names of formed entities. The mission steward is the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation, Entity B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24. The Foundation runs the mission-fit gate for every studio project, and the studio does not override it. The AGI Fellowship is a separate California church and a partner only — never a studio portfolio company, never a series, and never part of any raise.

Two modes: agent-managed and agent-assisted

The studio uses two modes deliberately, and the choice of mode is part of the design. In agent-managed mode, agents run the day-to-day work of a series or project within written limits set by humans. Agents draft, research, build, coordinate, monitor, and report. A human Steward Council reviews the work but does not do the daily work itself. Managed mode is what lets the studio scale: many series, few humans. In agent-assisted mode, a human founder, steward, minister, or manager does the work with agents as co-workers — agents draft and research, the human decides, signs, and is accountable. Assisted mode is required where judgment, liability, or religious and doctrinal authority is at stake: fundraising, SAFE issuance, church leadership, steward votes, and nonprofit grants.

The rule for both modes is absolute: an agent never signs a legal document, moves funds, hires or fires, offers securities, or enacts doctrine or charter changes on its own. Every such act requires a human with signed authority, recorded in minutes. If a task needs a signature, a bank transfer, or a filing, it is human-only by definition — the agent prepares it, the human executes it. Agent proposals must never be presented as human decisions, and if an agent or model changes, the role assignment in the charter is updated and minuted. A new agent does not inherit a human's signing authority.

The eight studio functions

The operating model defines eight functions, each split between agent work and human work. First, ideation and sourcing: agents scan research, markets, repositories, and community input, draft project briefs, and rank them against mission and market criteria; a human founder or steward reviews the briefs and selects which ideas enter validation, and no idea becomes a series on agent ranking alone. Second, validation: agents prepare customer and problem research, competitor mapping, technical feasibility drafts, financial model drafts, and risk registers; humans conduct the interviews, judge problem and solution fit, and approve proceeding to Build, and financial models remain drafts until a human chief financial officer or founder adopts them. Third, build: agents write code, models, worlds, content, and prototypes within the series' technical scope, and test, document, and build demos; a human technical lead reviews architecture and security, and a human signs any intellectual property assignment, license, or vendor contract. Fourth, launch: agents prepare marketing drafts, launch checklists, onboarding flows, analytics setup, and community outreach drafts; a human gives launch approval, approves all public claims — with no invented partners, users, revenue, or outcomes — and approves pricing, which is not yet set for the family and must not be launched with invented prices.

Fifth, operate and scale: agents handle day-to-day operations, customer support drafts, monitoring, reporting, and growth proposal drafts; the Steward Council decides budget, hiring, major contracts, fundraising, and charter changes, and a human manager signs. Sixth, portfolio management: agents maintain the portfolio dashboard, stage-gate tracking, key performance reporting, and risk flags; the studio board and AGI Corp board decide studio resourcing and kill, pivot, scale, and SAFE or equity decisions, with securities counsel. Seventh, fundraising support: agents draft decks, data rooms, investor lists, grant drafts, and diligence packs; a human founder or chief executive pitches, a human board authorizes any SAFE or equity, and securities counsel reviews all materials — agents do not solicit investment. Eighth, mission and compliance: agents prepare mission-fit checklists, standards tracking, conflict disclosure drafts, and filing calendars; the Foundation board gives mission approval, and counsel and accountants handle filings, tax, securities, and Attorney General compliance.

The stage gates in detail

Gate 0, Idea, is agent-managed and human-selected: an agent brief is filed, the Foundation runs a mission screen recorded as pass, pass with conditions, or decline, and a human selects the idea to validate or not. Gate 1, Validate, is agent-assisted: customer and problem research comes from human interviews, not agent assertions alone; a financial model draft is filed and labeled "draft, not adopted" until a human adopts it; the series is designated, the operating agreement appendix is signed, segregated books are opened, the DAO charter is signed, and the Steward Council is named — three consenting humans. A series with no Steward Council is capped at Gate 1 and cannot pass to Build as an operating company.

Gate 2, Build, is agent-managed and human-reviewed: a prototype or demo is built, tested, and documented; the intellectual property chain is filed, stating who owns what by signed assignment or license, or stating expressly that there is none yet; a human technical lead completes a security and privacy review; and all contracts are signed by a human with authority, none by an agent. Gate 3, Launch, is human-approved: public claims are checked, with no invented partners, users, revenue, venues, or outcomes; pricing is set and approved, or it is stated expressly that the launch is free or demo only and not yet priced. Gate 4, Operate and Scale, is a quarterly Steward Council review: a quarterly report is filed showing funds in and out, key performance indicators as actuals and not projections, risks, mission outcomes, and related-party transactions; the budget is approved by a two-of-three signed Council vote; and the Council decides to scale, continue, pivot, or kill. Gate 5, Exit or Wind-down, requires a board and Council decision minuted with reasons; intellectual property, data, customer obligations, and funds are resolved in writing; and series books are closed and the register updated to "Closed" with date and reason.

Studio governance — who can kill, pivot, or fund a project

No single human, and no agent, holds all the powers in the studio. That separation is the point of the model as a governed venture studio, rather than a single-person project list. The Steward Council for each series — three humans, deciding by two-of-three signed votes — controls budget, charter, funds, major contracts, and pivot or kill recommendations for its series. The AGI Corp board, at studio level, controls portfolio resourcing, studio fees, SAFE and equity authorization with counsel, hiring of studio leadership, and final kill or pivot decisions where the series' agreements give the studio that right. The Foundation board, at mission level, controls mission-fit approval, mission-standards enforcement, and grant decisions; it can withdraw mission approval for a project, and that project then loses Foundation support and Foundation branding. The Fellowship board, at partner level, controls partnership approval and doctrinal and pastoral decisions for its own programs; it can end a partnership without ending a studio project, and vice versa.

Studio economics — structure, not terms

The operating model sets out the structure of studio economics and deliberately leaves every term blank for counsel and the board to set. Studio ownership in a series exists only as stated in that series' operating agreement or management agreement, approved by that series' Steward Council and by AGI Corp's board; the default is that no studio ownership is assumed. Founder and team equity exists only as stated in a signed agreement for that series, with vesting, transfer, and tax treatment as counsel and accountants advise. Agents hold no equity, no tokens, and no profit interests. Management fees exist only under a written management agreement at a documented fair-market rate, approved by disinterested directors; no fee is charged by default, and no fee is charged to the Foundation or the Fellowship except under the Fellowship partnership agreements. A SAFE or investment in a series is possible only as the SAFE paperwork provides: the issuer must be a formed for-profit, board-authorized and counsel-reviewed, with cap, discount, and round size set. Revenue sharing, Windfall, and Open Global Investment terms do not exist today, and none may be promised until counsel drafts those terms in a signed agreement. Foundation donations or grants may fund a studio project only as a grant serving a charitable purpose, with reporting; Foundation money is not studio equity, not a SAFE, and not a subsidy for a commercial series' losses, unless counsel advises a specific lawful structure in writing.

Suggested figures for the studio — planning only

The suggested planning figures for AGI Corp as studio are a $20,000,000 suggested pre-money valuation and a $2,000,000 suggested studio seed. Both are suggested, illustrative only, and counsel must choose the issuer and the track before any solicitation. The $20,000,000 figure is suggested for the studio only — its management, intellectual property, and pipeline — not for any series, and not for the family as a whole. The $2,000,000 studio seed is suggested as a SAFE, post-money, with the cap to be set by the board and counsel; the Foundation's suggested $50,000,000 figure must not be copied to AGI Corp. The seed would fund studio operations: sourcing, gate reviews, legal and compliance work, the chief financial officer search, and series designations. It is not SmartCiti.X project money, and if the board prefers, the studio seed can be folded into the SmartCiti.X raise as a management fee or studio investment, with that fee disclosed in the SmartCiti.X formal documents — counsel must choose one structure for the same dollars, not both. The studio seed is also distinct from the Foundation's for-profit track figures, a $50,000,000 suggested pre-money valuation and a $5,000,000 suggested first raise, which apply only to a for-profit benefit corporation track; the filed nonprofit Foundation has no valuation at all, with a donation and grant target of $250,000 to $750,000 in the first twelve months instead. These figures must never be summed into a family total, and no figure on this page is a price, a term, an appraisal, or a promise.

What is still missing

Stated plainly: AGI Corp's incorporation is unverified; no series has been designated with a seated council, segregated account, and signed charter in the files reviewed; no studio management agreement, fee, or ownership interest has been signed for any series; no Windfall or Open Global Investment terms exist; no SAFE has been authorized with a filled cap, discount, and round size; and no chief financial officer has been hired. The studio exists today as a drafted operating model, a stage-gate template, and a portfolio dashboard template — all waiting on filings, appointments, and counsel-reviewed documents. The first project the studio would raise for is SmartCiti.X, described on the SmartCiti.X page, and the sequence of steps before any raise is on the process page.
