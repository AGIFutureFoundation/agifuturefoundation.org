About this section

1. Is this an offering?

Any future raise would need counsel's choice of issuer and track, accredited-investor checks, and formal documents, and none of those exist yet for any raise described here. The banner at the top of every page in this section says the same thing, because it is the most important sentence on the site for anyone reading these pages.

2. Can I take part today?

No. There is no live raise, no formal documents, and no process to join. No one can buy, join, fund, or hold a place in anything described on these pages today. The only step available now is to express interest by email to x@agifuturefoundation.org, and an expression of interest is not a commitment, not a purchase, and not a promise of any economic outcome on either side. It does not hold a place, does not set a price, and does not entitle the sender to any document or term.

3. Is anything on this site legal, tax, or financial advice?

No. Nothing on these pages is legal, tax, or financial advice. The pages summarize corporate paperwork that is itself marked as draft, general information for planning, and not legal, tax, or securities advice. Anyone considering a future step should take their own counsel's advice, and should expect the formal documents for any future round to carry the actual terms, risks, and disclosures.

5. Is the Foundation tax-exempt? Are donations tax-deductible?

That must not be claimed. No IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter was in the files reviewed for the Foundation, and the EIN notice lists Form 1120 as due. The correct statement is "501(c)(3) determination pending", never "tax-deductible" and never "tax-exempt". The same caution applies to the Fellowship: no determination letter was in the files, its EIN notice lists Form 720 as due, and churches have a special IRS posture that must not be assumed. A donor receipt template must be reviewed by counsel and an accountant, with no deductibility claim until the basis is confirmed in writing.

6. What is the PBC fork?

The words "AGI Future Foundation PBC" are used in two different senses, and the fork is the choice between them. As filed, the Foundation is a nonprofit that cannot sell equity, SAFEs, profit shares, or tokens, and cannot distribute profits. As labeled on the website, "PBC" has been used to describe a for-profit benefit corporation with dual-class equity, Class A profit participation, and Open Global Investment. Those are not the same entity type. Counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation: form a new for-profit California benefit corporation with a distinct filing and keep the nonprofit separate; convert or restructure the existing entity on counsel's advice, understanding that a nonprofit-to-for-profit conversion is a major Attorney General and IRS transaction and not a name change; or run the raise through AGI Corp in Wyoming instead and keep the Foundation as a nonprofit mission steward. Until one track is chosen in writing, the PBC raise structure must not be attached to the nonprofit Foundation, and both tracks must not be run for the same money, to the same people, on different terms.

11. What is the venture studio, and who runs it?

The studio is a function of AGI Corp, the intended Wyoming head corporation — not a separate legal entity, unless counsel advises forming one. It sources, validates, builds, launches, and operates projects, each held in its own series or corporation, with human stewards deciding and agents doing the day-to-day work. AGI Corp itself has an EIN only; its incorporation is unverified. The studio works in two modes: agent-managed, where agents run day-to-day work within written human-set limits, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers. In both modes, an agent never signs, moves funds, hires or fires, offers securities, or enacts doctrine or charter changes. Agents hold no equity. Each series has a Steward Council of three consenting humans deciding by two-of-three signed votes, and each project moves through six stage gates — Idea, Validate, Build, Launch, Operate and Scale, Exit or Wind-down — passed only when a human approves and the evidence is filed. The Foundation is the mission steward; the Fellowship is a partner, never a portfolio company. Full detail is on the studio page.

13. What are the share classes, the Windfall Clause, and Open Global Investment?

On the for-profit track, the share structure is three classes, built to the pitch standard: Class A, one vote per share, broad public access, full economic participation; Class B, ten votes per share, restricted to aligned partners and sovereign entities meeting strict safety and geopolitical frameworks, with economic participation and voting, subject to sanctions, export-control, and national-security screening; and Class C, one thousand votes per share, held exclusively by the Foundation, control only, with no economic participation. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions — it is not immunity from shareholder suits. The Windfall Clause is a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, obligating the 33 W3 series, with a threshold expressed as a percentage of Global GDP and a Windfall Share above the threshold going to humanity through benefit buckets for Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination. Every operative term is blank for counsel, and Open Global Investment has no terms today either. The pitch Gini figures of about 0.96 and about 0.89 are illustrations, not verified data.
