Governance of the AGI Future Foundation

Three mechanisms, three tiers, three share classes, and one human gate. This page states the whole architecture in one place — and labels, at every step, what is filed fact, what is an intended plan, and what counsel still has to decide.

Honest status — read this first.

What the Foundation is

The Foundation’s filed purpose covers safe, equitable, and socially beneficial artificial intelligence, and research and coordination toward that end. Its principal and mailing address on file is 995 Market St, AGI Lab –10, San Francisco, CA 94103, and its agent is Mike Rice. As a nonprofit public benefit corporation, its assets are irrevocably dedicated to its charitable purpose: it cannot distribute profits, and it cannot be owned the way a for-profit subsidiary is owned. That last point matters for everything on this page. The Foundation’s board owes its fiduciary duties to the charitable mission and the public, not to any commercial parent’s shareholders.

The intended design in the family’s planning documents borrows stewardship principles associated with Stanford PACS and effective philanthropy — mission lock, independent fiduciary governance, pooled or segregated funds per project, and documented donor intent. That is a set of principles, not an affiliation: no Stanford entity endorses, partners with, or governs this family. In the intended design, the Foundation acts as mission steward: it sets the mission statement and standards, reviews projects for mission fit, and manages companies to those standards under written agreements — not by owning them.

One structural tension is still open and must be designed by counsel. The intended design places AGI Corp, a Wyoming corporation, as the commercial head of the family, with the Foundation beneath it as steward. A California nonprofit cannot be controlled by a for-profit head the way a subsidiary is controlled. The Foundation must keep an independent board, independent decisions, and the right to refuse a project that fails its mission standards — even if AGI Corp wants it. Until counsel designs that control mechanism, the honest description is that the Foundation is a steward, not a captive, and that control runs only through written mission agreements between equals.

The architecture in one page

Governance here is built from three mechanisms that interlock, three tiers that separate mission from commerce, three share classes that separate economics from control, and a human council that holds the final decision. Each piece is described briefly below; each has its own page in this section.

The three mechanisms

Fiduciary Shield

The legal protection for safety-first decisions. On the for-profit benefit corporation track, a California benefit corporation’s board must consider its public benefit purpose alongside shareholder interests, and its decisions are judged under an expanded business-judgment analysis. That is protection, not immunity: the board can still be sued, the company can still be acquired, and its benefit purpose can still be amended by the vote the law requires. The shield only works if it is evidenced — benefit purpose in the Articles, minutes showing the benefit was considered, and an annual benefit report.

Open Global Investment

Broad participation in economic upside, kept separate from governance control. The idea is that rivals and partners who share in the upside have less reason to race unsafely or to expropriate, and more reason to want the Foundation’s safe success. Economic participation is broad; governance is not. No terms for this program exist today — instrument, eligibility, price, governance rights, and transfer rules are all blanks for counsel.

Windfall Clause

A legal pre-commitment, adopted before profits are generated, to share profits above a set threshold with humanity rather than distributing them as ordinary shareholder profit. The threshold in the pitch standard is a percentage of global GDP, not a fixed dollar amount, and the obligation in the pitch runs to the 33 W3 series, not only to the Foundation. Below the threshold, standard returns and research funding apply. Above it, profits flow to benefit buckets for global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination, in support of the UN Sustainable Development Goals. The threshold percentage, the share of excess profits, the profit measure, and the administrator are all blank for counsel.

The three tiers

Tier 1 · Governance and mission — AGI Future Foundation: the filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation described above. It holds the mission, the standards, and the ethical backstop. It does not hold commercial operations, and it does not spend donations on a SAFE round or a for-profit series except as a written grant serving a charitable purpose, approved by disinterested directors, with reporting back.

Tier 2 · Commercial engine — W3 LLC, Wyoming series company (INTENDED, not verified as filed): the intended master company for the family’s operating series. No Wyoming filing for W3 LLC was in the files reviewed. If formed as intended, each series would be a separate cell with its own assets, books, and liabilities, and no commingling of funds across series.

Tier 3 · The series — 33 named sub-series: the operating companies and worlds, including SmartCiti.X, Robotics DAO, and InfoGurus. The count of 33 comes from an internal April 2026 printout listing named sub-series, Series 1 through 33. That printout is not a Wyoming Secretary of State filing, not a series designation, and not evidence of a bank account, EIN, intellectual-property assignment, or Steward Council for any series. The live site’s other counts do not match it, and this page uses 33 because that is what the internal list supports. New projects, under the intended design, go into a Delaware cohort series — Cohort 1, Series 1 through 3, labelled C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3 — likewise intended and not verified as filed. A series label is a reference, not a legal name: the legal name of a series is the master company’s name plus its series designation in the operating agreement.

The share classes, in brief

The three-class structure belongs to the for-profit benefit corporation track only. It does not apply to the filed nonprofit Foundation, which has no shares to sell. Class A, one vote per share: the broad-access class, fully participating in economic upside up to the Windfall threshold. Class B, ten votes per share: restricted to aligned partners and sovereign entities that meet strict safety and geopolitical frameworks, with economic participation as well as voting power; any sovereign participation needs sanctions screening and export-control and national-security review where applicable. Class C, one thousand votes per share: held exclusively by the Foundation, control only, with no economic participation. Even in small numbers, Class C dominates ordinary votes — that is how safety decisions are centralized in the Foundation while economics are widely distributed. That control must be disclosed to Class A and Class B holders as a governance fact, not hidden behind the word “decoupled.” No share counts, prices, or valuations are stated here: the planning files’ suggested valuations and raise sizes are illustrative planning figures only, not prices, not appraisals, and not terms, and they are never added together into a single family total.

The Steward Council: humans decide

Each series is intended to be managed under a DAO structure, in which agents operate day to day — drafting proposals, coordinating work, and keeping records. A DAO vote does not sign a contract, move funds, or issue securities. Only the series’ legal manager and its Steward Council, with signed authority, do that. The design in the planning documents gives each series a Steward Council of three human seats, deciding by two-of-three signed votes, as the only authority to move funds or enact a charter change. Until stewards are named, consenting, and holding keys, a series has no effective council. Agents draft proposals; humans decide. The same rule applies at the top: agents prepare, humans execute, and no agent signs, moves funds, hires or fires, offers securities, or enacts doctrine or a charter on the Foundation’s behalf.

How the tiers fit together

The arrows run from mission to commerce to operations: the Foundation sets mission and standards, W3 LLC is the intended commercial engine, and each series operates within its own cell. The Steward Council sits across the series as the human gate. AGI Corp, the intended Wyoming commercial head, is not drawn as a tier above the Foundation, because a nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head; its role is for counsel to design in writing.

Governance, page by page

Fiduciary Shield

How a benefit corporation’s board can lawfully put safety and public benefit ahead of short-term profit, what expanded business-judgment protection actually covers, and what it does not cover. It corrects the live site’s overstatement: the board is protected, not immune.

Windfall Clause

The pre-commitment to share profits above a threshold measured as a percentage of global GDP, the benefit buckets those profits would fund, and the amendment protection meant to keep the promise from being undone when profits arrive. Threshold, share, and administrator are still blank.

Share Structure

Class A at one vote, Class B at ten votes for restricted screened partners and sovereigns, and Class C at one thousand votes held only by the Foundation for control with no economics. This structure is for the for-profit benefit corporation track only, not the filed nonprofit.

Steward Council

The human backstop for every series: three seats, two-of-three signed votes, and the only authority to move funds or enact a charter change. Agents draft and coordinate; the Council decides, and a series with no seated Council has no effective gate.

Open Global Investment

Broad economic participation separated from governance control, intended to give rivals and partners a vested interest in safe success rather than a reason to race. No participation terms exist today, and none are offered here.

What is not true yet

  • Nothing has been issued. No shares, no SAFE, no convertible note, no token, and no profit-participation interest has been issued by the Foundation, by AGI Corp, or by any series. No offering is open, and nothing on this page is an offer to sell securities.
  • No SAFE or equity is offered by the filed nonprofit. The Foundation as filed cannot sell equity or SAFEs, and the Fellowship church cannot either. Any SAFE or equity structure on this site belongs to a for-profit track that requires a new for-profit benefit corporation, a conversion counsel advises on, or AGI Corp as issuer — and counsel must choose one issuer in writing first.
  • Formation is unverified beyond the two nonprofits. Only the Foundation and the Fellowship have verified Secretary of State filings in the files reviewed. AGI Corp has an EIN notice only, which does not prove incorporation in any state. W3 LLC, the Delaware cohort master, any for-profit benefit corporation, and any SmartCiti.X corporation are intended, not verified as filed.
  • The Windfall Clause and Open Global Investment have no terms. The threshold percentage, the share of excess profits, the profit measure, the GDP measure and vintage, the measurement scope, the administrator, the Class B eligibility framework, and the amendment vote are all blank. A clause with blank terms is a concept, not a calculable promise.
  • No site, union, customer, or revenue is claimed here. No site has been purchased, leased, or placed under contract; no union or pilot customer has signed; and no revenue is evidenced in the files reviewed. Suggested valuations, raise sizes, tranche splits, and use-of-proceeds figures in the planning files are illustrative planning figures only — not prices, not appraisals, not guarantees, and never to be summed into a family total.
  • People and roles are not inflated. Co-founder consent for the named nonprofit co-founder is still pending, and no one is listed here as a director, officer, or chief financial officer on the basis of having reviewed documents or offered to help. Reviewing and advising do not make someone an officer.

Until the open decisions are made — issuer and track, formation, series designations, seated Steward Councils, segregated accounts, and counsel-drafted offering documents with a Form D calendar — the governance described here is a design to be built, not a system already in force.
