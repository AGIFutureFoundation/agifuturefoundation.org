Delaware Cohort Series

The Delaware cohort series are an intended home for new cohorts and new projects in the Foundation’s structure planning — Cohort 1, Series 1 to 3, written C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3, with the naming itself still to confirm. No Delaware entity or series is verified as formed. This page exists so that a plan mentioned across the site has one place where its status is stated in full: intended, not formed, not funded, and not offered.

What this is

The Delaware cohort series are the second series route in the Foundation’s intended structure. The first route is the Wyoming one: W3 LLC, a filed Wyoming Series LLC (Filing ID 2024-001429416, filed 2024-03-21, Active, all standings Good), inside which an April 2026 internal list names 33 sub-series. The Delaware route was planned alongside it for a specific job: new cohorts — groups of new projects taken on together, in sequence — would be placed on new Delaware series, starting with Cohort 1, Series 1, 2, and 3, rather than added to the Wyoming list. Each cohort series would hold its cohort’s projects separately, under the same governance model as the rest of the network: agents operate day to day within human-set limits, a human Steward Council decides, and the Foundation acts as mission gate under written mission agreements.

That is the plan, stated at the strength the planning papers give it — which includes an explicit reservation. In the structure planning, the Delaware labels are written “C1S1, C1S2, C1S3 (to confirm)”: Cohort 1, Series 1 to 3. The “to confirm” is part of the record. It means the naming convention had not been settled when the plan was written, and no later document reviewed for this site settles it. This page uses the labels because the rest of the site uses them, and it carries the reservation every time the labels do real work, so that a reader never mistakes a planning label for a designated series.

What is verified — and what is not

For this route, the verified column is deliberately short, and its shortness is the finding. VERIFIED: the Foundation’s own filing, stated above; the W3 LLC parent filing in Wyoming, stated above; and the existence of the plan itself, in the Foundation’s structure planning, which names the Delaware cohort series as the intended home for new cohorts. Not verified, in any file reviewed for this site: a Delaware parent entity of any kind; a certificate of formation or conversion filed in Delaware; a series designation for C1S1, C1S2, or C1S3; a Delaware registered agent; an EIN for any Delaware entity or series; a bank account; an operating agreement; a cohort roster; a launched cohort; a portfolio project placed in a Delaware series.

The contrast with the Wyoming route is instructive, and this page draws it directly. For W3 LLC there is a filing to cite: a number, a date, a status, a registered agent. For the Delaware cohorts there is no filing to cite at all — only an intention, recorded in planning papers, with its own naming reservation attached. A page that described the two routes in the same tone would mislead in both directions: it would understate the Wyoming parent, which is filed and active, and overstate the Delaware route, which is a plan. The series pages for the Wyoming list therefore carry a “named in the internal list; parent filed” status, while this page carries the plain status the banner gives it: intended, not formed.

Three things this is not

It is not a W3 LLC series. The Wyoming list runs Series 1–33, and C1S1 is not a thirty-fourth entry in it. The cohort labels belong to a separate, unformed Delaware route. Numbering that looks similar — S1 in one list, C1S1 in the other — is a naming resemblance, not a legal relationship, and no page on this site treats a cohort label as a Wyoming series number.

It is not the proposed SmartCiti.X corporation. The SmartCiti.X planning (Series 10 in the Wyoming list) recommends a separate new corporation for that venture, with a Delaware C-corporation named in the planning as the recommended form and Wyoming as an alternative. That is a recommendation about one proposed corporation’s form, made in that venture’s planning papers. It is not a Delaware series formation, it does not create or evidence the cohort series, and the cohort series do not borrow it. Two different Delaware mentions in two different plans are two different proposals, and neither has been formed.

It is not a cohort that has run. No cohort has been verified as launched under the venture-studio model, no project has been verified as placed in a cohort series, and the studio itself — the operating model these cohorts were designed for — has formed nothing, funded nothing, and issued nothing under its model yet. Writing about cohorts in the future tense is accurate; any other tense would need a document that does not exist in the files.

The cohort idea, as planned

The planning behind the cohort route is a sequencing idea, and it can be described without overstating it. Rather than adding every new project to one long list, new projects would arrive in cohorts — small, dated groups that move through the venture studio’s stage gates together: Gate 0 Idea, Gate 1 Validate, Gate 2 Build, Gate 3 Launch, Gate 4 Operate and Scale, Gate 5 Exit or Wind-down. A cohort structure makes review easier to run and easier to audit: the projects in a cohort share a start point, a review rhythm, and a record, while each project’s own facts — its team, its spend, its evidence — stay its own. The portfolio dashboard in the studio planning is specified on the same discipline: actuals only, nothing projected presented as achieved.

Within the Wyoming list, the series that maps to this work is S21 Incubator DAO: the Foundation site’s magazine feature on the cohort machine (feature 19) covers Incubator DAO and the Delaware cohort series together, because the incubator function and the cohort route were designed as one mechanism — an incubator that admits projects in cohorts, and a series structure that would hold each cohort separately. Both halves of that mechanism are at the same stage today: described in planning, governed on paper, and unformed in fact. The stage gates, the dashboard specification, and the cohort labels are planning documents. They are useful precisely because they were written before anything was formed: a future reviewer can hold the first real cohort, if one is ever formed, against the gates it was supposed to pass.

Why Delaware at all is a question the planning raises rather than answers, and this page leaves it where the planning leaves it. Series structures exist under more than one state’s law, the Wyoming route was already filed, and the choice of a second state for cohorts — its costs, its filing mechanics, its tax and reporting obligations, and how two series systems would be administered side by side — is a counsel question. This page records that the plan names Delaware; it does not argue for Delaware, and nothing here is a recommendation about where any entity should be formed.

How a cohort series would be governed

Governance for a cohort series, if one were formed, would follow the model used across the corporate holdings in the agentic network. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable. The Foundation’s role would be the mission gate, exercised through written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and control by agreement is the only route the planning contemplates.

That model is itself planned, not operating for this route: there is no cohort series to govern, no council roster for one, and no signed mission agreement covering one. No agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the Foundation or by any intended entity; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one, and any future fundraising, partnership, or cohort admission would need its own documents, its own issuer where relevant, and counsel review.

What exists today vs what is planned

What exists today

  • Structure planning that names Delaware series C1S1, C1S2, C1S3 as the intended home for new cohorts — with the naming marked “to confirm” in the planning itself.
  • The venture-studio operating model and its six stage gates, as planning documents (Idea through Exit/Wind-down), with a portfolio dashboard specified on actuals only.
  • S21 Incubator DAO, named in the April 2026 internal W3 list, as the Wyoming series that maps to the cohort work.
  • The two filed anchors around the plan: the Foundation (Entity No. B20260196664, filed 2026-04-24) and the W3 LLC parent in Wyoming (Filing ID 2024-001429416, filed 2024-03-21, Active).

What is planned

  • A Delaware parent and series designations for C1S1, C1S2, and C1S3 — none filed or verified.
  • A settled naming convention for cohort series — still to confirm.
  • A first cohort admitted through the stage gates — none launched.
  • Per-series bank accounts, EINs, and Steward Councils for cohort series — none verified.
  • Written mission agreements covering the cohort route — intended, designed with counsel.

What does not exist today: a Delaware filing of any kind for this route, a designated series, a registered agent, a bank account, a cohort, or a project placed in a cohort series. The labels exist in planning. Nothing else about this route is claimed.

What would have to happen next

Before any Delaware cohort series could be described as formed, the steps would have to happen in order, and each would have to be documented: counsel’s written advice on the vehicle and the state, resolving why a Delaware route should exist alongside the filed Wyoming one; the naming convention settled in writing, so that C1S1 and its successors mean one thing everywhere they are used; a parent entity formed in Delaware, with its filing number and date cited here; the first series designated under it, with the designation document cited; the series’ own financial and governance identity established, with a bank account, any required EIN, and a named Steward Council; and only then a first cohort admitted, project by project, through Gate 0 with its own evidence. Each completed step would change this page, because each would be a document this page could cite.

Until those steps are taken, the accurate public record for the Delaware cohort series is this page: an intention in the structure planning, a set of labels with their reservation attached, a governance model described as planned, and no formation of any kind.
