W3 LLC

W3 LLC is a Wyoming series limited liability company — the parent entity is filed and active, and that is a verified fact with a filing number and a date. The 33 named sub-series inside it are a different matter: they are named in an internal list dated April 2026, and per-series legal designations are not on file. This page keeps those two facts separate, because the whole record of W3 LLC is the distance between them.

What this is

W3 LLC is, in the Foundation’s intended structure, the series network: one filed Wyoming series LLC whose individual series would each hold a separate project, so that projects can be built, governed, and — if they fail — wound down separately from one another. The Foundation’s planning describes the Foundation running the W3 LLC series to mission standards, each series DAO-managed in the specific sense used across this site: agents operate day to day within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council decides. All new projects were intended to go into series. That is the intended role. What is verified today is the parent filing and the internal list of names, and this page leads with the verified part.

The filing facts that anchor every page in this section are the same. AGI Future Foundation is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, Entity No. B20260196664, with Articles filed on 2026-04-24 and Secretary of State approval on 2026-04-30, at 995 Market St AGI Lab -10, San Francisco, CA 94103. Its Employer Identification Number was assigned on 2026-05-07. No IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files reviewed for this site, so no page here claims that the Foundation is tax-exempt, and no page claims that any donation to the Foundation is tax-deductible. Nothing on this site is legal, tax, or financial advice.

A structural point belongs in the same passage as the plan itself. The Foundation, as filed, is a California nonprofit. A nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by another entity in the way a subsidiary is owned, and its board must remain independent. The intended relationship between the Foundation and the W3 series is therefore stewardship by written mission agreement, designed with counsel — not ownership. Until such an agreement is signed and cited, the stewardship described here is an intended model, not a completed legal arrangement.

The filing, stated exactly

Because the Wyoming record is the anchor document for this page, it is stated exactly, with nothing added. VERIFIED (Wyoming Secretary of State record, checked 2026-11-10): the entity is named “W3 LLC”; it is a Wyoming Series LLC; its Filing ID is 2024-001429416; it was filed on 2024-03-21; its status is Active; and all of its standings are Good. The registered agent of record is Registered Agents Inc, of Sheridan, Wyoming.

What that record proves is the parent: a series LLC exists in Wyoming, in good standing, able under Wyoming’s series framework to establish individual series. What it does not contain is any individual series. It names no series, designates no series, and records no series-level bank account, tax number, asset, or council. A reader who treats the parent filing as proof that “Series 10” or any other numbered series is a designated legal series has made the central error this page is written to prevent: the banner says per-series designations are not on file, this section says it again, and the section below on what the filing does not prove says it a third time, with the full list of what is missing.

What the parent filing does not prove

The gap list is stated in full so that no reader has to infer it. For each individual series, the following are not on file in the records reviewed for this site: a legal designation of the series under the parent; a separate bank account; a separate EIN; any intellectual property held in the series’ own name; a Steward Council roster for the series; an operating agreement specific to the series; a customer, a contract, or revenue. A series name in an internal list, a page on this site, a repository, or a world in a registry is none of those things. Each would be a separate, verifiable fact, with its own document, and none is claimed here unless the page cites the document, the date, and the issuing authority.

This discipline cuts both ways, and the second direction matters just as much. The absence of a per-series designation is not evidence that the parent is inactive or behind: the Wyoming record shows the opposite, an Active parent with all standings Good. Earlier drafts in the network described W3 LLC as simply “intended” or “not verified as filed”; the 2026-11-10 Secretary of State check superseded that description for the parent, and this page follows the verified record. Where an older page on this site still describes the parent itself as unfiled, the older page is out of date on that point and this page is the corrected statement.

REPORTED from the public repository AGIFutureFoundation/agi-corp-worlds, reviewed 2026-11-07: the registry file worlds/series.json lists the W3 series as “names and numbers only, as listed by the founder in chat on 2026-09-23”, and it holds 35 entries, Series 1–35 — the same 33 names as the April 2026 internal list, plus two more: 34 ALS DAO and 35 Parkinsons DAO. Those two do not appear in the April 2026 internal list, and no series number is assigned to Biota in either record, which is a second gap in the same place.

The tension is stated, not smoothed over. The April 2026 internal list names 33. The repository registry names 35. The repository’s own README uses a third formulation, “34 chartered worlds”, which counts worlds rather than series and never reconciles the difference in its own text. This site quotes the April 2026 internal list — 33 named sub-series — as its settled count, because that list is the document the series pages were built from, and it flags the registry’s extra two names wherever the registry is described. Neither ALS DAO nor Parkinsons DAO has a series page on this site, because neither has a number in the settled list; if a future internal list assigns them numbers, pages can follow from that document and not before.

How a series would be governed

Governance for each series, as planned, follows the model used across the corporate holdings in the agentic network. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold decision authority for the series, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable. The Foundation’s intended role is the mission gate: a series that drifts from the mission standards would be a series the Foundation’s stewardship is meant to catch, under written mission agreements, with the Foundation’s own board remaining independent.

That model is itself planned, not operating per series today: no per-series Steward Council roster, operating agreement, or published set of agent limits exists to point to for any individual series. No agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by W3 LLC, by any series, or by the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit any of them, and any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement would need its own documents and counsel review.

  • A registered agent of record: Registered Agents Inc, Sheridan, Wyoming.

  • A public registry repository (agi-corp-worlds) listing the series names — with two extra names, as described above (REPORTED from the repository).
  • The Foundation’s filing record (Entity No. B20260196664, filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07).

What is planned

  • Per-series legal designations under the parent — not on file for any series.
  • Per-series bank accounts, EINs, and Steward Councils — none verified for any series.
  • Written mission agreements between the Foundation and the series network — intended, designed with counsel.
  • New projects placed into series as the portfolio route — intended; nothing has been formed, funded, or issued under the venture-studio model yet.
  • Delaware cohort series for new cohorts — a separate intended route, covered on the Delaware cohort series page.

What does not exist today, for any individual series: a legal designation on file, a bank account, an EIN, a council roster, a customer, or revenue. The parent is filed; the series are named. Both halves of that sentence are the record.

What would have to happen next

Before any individual series could be described as designated, five things would have to happen, and each would have to be documented: the series designated in the parent’s own records in the manner Wyoming’s series framework and counsel require, with the designation document cited; the series’ own operating terms written down, including who its Steward Council is; its own financial identity established, with a bank account and, where required, its own EIN; any intellectual property it is said to hold assigned to it in writing; and only then any operating claim — a customer, a contract, a deployment — stated with its own evidence. Each completed step would change this page, because each would be a document this page could cite.

Until those steps are taken for a given series, the accurate public record for that series is its own series page: a name, a number in an internal list, a planned focus described as planned, and a parent filing that is verified at the parent level only.
