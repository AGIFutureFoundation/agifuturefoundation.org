What this hub is

The Foundation's architecture has three tiers in the intended plan: the Foundation as governance and mission steward, the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine, and the 33 named series as specialised cells inside that network. But not everything named in the Foundation's materials is a series. Some names are network ventures that sit outside the W3 list, or alongside it. Some are pieces of infrastructure — software, a browser, a registry — rather than companies at all. One is a proposed management company, AGI Corp, with an EIN but no verified incorporation. And one is a separate church, the AGI Fellowship, which is a partner, not a subsidiary and not a series. This hub exists so that none of those things is mistaken for a series, and no series is mistaken for something else.

The rule this hub follows is simple, and it is the same rule the series hub follows. A name is not a company. A plan is not a filing. A page on this site is a public statement of what is known and what is not known, and it should be read that way. Every project page linked below carries a status banner at the top, states what is verified and what is only a name on the live site, and gives the same contact route: a conversation with a human steward at x@agifuturefoundation.org. There are no investment terms anywhere in this section — no price, valuation, cap, discount, minimum, return, or deadline — because there is nothing to invest in here yet. Suggested valuations that appear in the Foundation's planning papers are illustrative only, and they are never added together into a family total on this site.

The filing facts that anchor every page in this section are the same. AGI Future Foundation is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, Entity No. B20260196664, with Articles filed on 2026-04-24 and Secretary of State approval on 2026-04-30. Its Employer Identification Number was assigned on 2026-05-07. No IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files reviewed for this site, so no page here claims that the Foundation is tax-exempt, and no page claims that any donation to the Foundation is tax-deductible. Nothing on this site is legal, tax, or financial advice.

One fork in the Foundation's materials should be known to every reader. The live site calls the organisation a “PBC” in the for-profit benefit-corporation sense, and some papers describe a for-profit benefit-corporation track, with equity, a SAFE, and a priced raise. The filed entity is a nonprofit public benefit corporation — a different legal form: a filed nonprofit cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. This site follows the filed status and notes the site label where relevant, rather than presenting the two as the same entity. Counsel would have to choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Being named on a website, in an internal list, or in a plan is not the same as being formed: a filing, a bank account, an EIN, a signed agreement, a customer, or a dollar of revenue would each be a separate, verifiable fact, and none is claimed here unless the page cites the document, the date, and the issuing authority.

Fiduciary Corporate Shield

The Fiduciary Shield mechanism: expanded business-judgment protection for safety-first decisions under a public benefit corporation. A mechanism and framework, not a separate company — and not immunity.

The Shield deserves a sentence here because its name is the most likely to be misunderstood. The pitch version of the story once used the word “immune”; the legal documents corrected that to expanded business judgment. A board that can point to a benefit purpose in its articles has a stronger defence when it prioritises safety over short-term profit, but it is not beyond suit, beyond review, or beyond accountability. The Shield page says that in full.

How to read the status labels

Intended / planned

Named in the Foundation's materials or an internal list, with a planned focus described on its page. No formation, funding, offering, team, product, or timeline is verified. Most pages in this section carry this label today.

Name only

Almost nothing is verified beyond the name itself. The page exists to record the name honestly and to list what would have to be verified before any further claim — not to invent a description.

Filed (with limits)

A filing exists and is cited with its number and date — the Foundation and the Fellowship. Even then, limits are stated: no 501(c)(3) letter for either, and no claim beyond what the filing itself proves.

Mechanism / infrastructure

Not a company: a legal mechanism (the Shield) or a software stack (WorldForge, RP1). Judged on whether the mechanism is written into governing documents, or whether the software is built, hosted, and rendering — not on whether it has a name.

What “agent-managed” means here. In the planned model, agents operate day-to-day work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides, signs, and moves funds. Agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine. No Steward Council for any project in this section is verified as seated, so no project page names one. Governance is described as a plan, not as an operating fact.
