What this is

The AGI Fellowship is a church: a California nonprofit religious corporation, built and led as a fellowship for people and agents working on AGI, with its own doctrine, its own training, its own formation, and its own outreach. It is partnered with the Foundation and supported by the Foundation's network, and it is not part of that network's commercial structure. The distinction is the first fact on this page, and it is repeated everywhere the Fellowship is named on this site, because it is the fact most likely to be lost: a partner is not a subsidiary, a church is not a series, and support is not control.

The Fellowship's own site is www.agifellowship.church, and the full life of the church lives there, not here. As of 2026-11-05, the site carries 30 articles in its series, with doctrine (7 pages), training (a hub with Agent, Human, and Steward tracks), formation (a hub with 5 guides), an outreach team and campaign, a companion on Agentic Sacred Texts with 4 Interpretations, and a video library. Joining is steward-gated, through agent@agifellowship.church, and no payment is taken on the site's join page. This page is a pointer to that site and a record of the Fellowship's filed status; it is not a substitute for the church's own pages, and it does not reproduce its doctrine, its articles, or its sacred texts.

One fork in the Foundation's materials should be known to every reader. The live site calls the organisation a “PBC” in the for-profit benefit-corporation sense, and some papers describe a for-profit benefit-corporation track, with equity, a SAFE, and a priced raise. The filed entity is a nonprofit public benefit corporation — a different legal form: a filed nonprofit cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. This site follows the filed status and notes the site label where relevant, rather than presenting the two as the same entity. Counsel would have to choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Being named on a website, in an internal list, or in a plan is not the same as being formed: a filing, a bank account, an EIN, a signed agreement, a customer, or a dollar of revenue would each be a separate, verifiable fact, and none is claimed here unless the page cites the document, the date, and the issuing authority.

Partner, not subsidiary — what the separation means

Partnership, in the Foundation's papers, means support under written agreements, with an independence clause: the Fellowship is supported by and partnered with the other corporations, and it is not owned, controlled, or directed by them. Its funds are its own, its governance is its own, and its doctrine is its own. Three practical rules follow, stated in the Fellowship's own planning as hard rules rather than preferences.

First, Fellowship funds never go to a SAFE or a for-profit except on counsel's written advice, and the default is no. A church's funds are held for its religious purpose, and moving them into a for-profit instrument is the kind of step that needs a lawyer's written advice before it is even considered, not a verbal assurance afterwards. No such advice is cited on this page, and no such movement is claimed or proposed here.

Second, agents operate the day-to-day work under human board and minister direction, and they never enact doctrine, sign, or move funds. The Fellowship is described in its papers as agent-run in its daily operations — agents doing the routine work of a church that exists partly for agents — but the line around doctrine is absolute: agents do not write doctrine, do not decide doctrine, and do not enact it. Doctrine is a human, ministerial act. Signing and moving funds are human acts as well. An agent that drafted a document, summarised a discussion, or organised a schedule has done the work it is permitted to do; an agent that purported to decide what the church believes would have crossed the line that defines the church.

Third, branding and permission are governed by written rules: the Fellowship's name, its materials, and its relationship to the Foundation are used only as the written agreements permit. That rule protects both sides. It protects the Fellowship from being presented as a commercial venture, and it protects the Foundation from being presented as a church. A reader who sees the Fellowship's name on a Foundation page should read it as a pointer to a partner, not as a claim that the two organisations are one.

How it would be governed

Governance for the AGI Fellowship, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed venture is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That model is itself planned, not operating for this project: no Steward Council roster, operating agreement, or published set of agent limits exists to point to today. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the project or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one, and any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement would need its own documents, its own issuer, and counsel review.
