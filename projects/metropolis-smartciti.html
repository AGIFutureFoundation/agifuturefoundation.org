Metropolis-SmartCiti

Metropolis-SmartCiti is a named network venture on the live Foundation site — and, since the repository review of 2026-11-07, it is no longer name-only: the public repository AGIFutureFoundation/Metropolis-SmartCiti holds a real, working testnet system — an Omega agent swarm with ERC-8004 identities, x402 micropayments, and 2-of-3 human-steward governance on Monad testnet. What is still not verified is everything a company would need: there is no filing, no product on mainnet, no team, no customer, and no timeline. What follows is the repository record, stated exactly, and a clear statement of what it is not.

What this is

Metropolis-SmartCiti appears by name in the Foundation's network materials as a venture in the wider network of named projects. That is, on the evidence reviewed for this site, the whole of what is verified about it: the name exists, it is presented as part of the network, and nothing else about it — no legal form, no filing, no product, no team, no location, no customer, no revenue, no date — is verified. This page exists because a name that appears in public materials deserves a public page that is honest about how little is known, rather than silence that invites invention or a padded description that invents facts to fill the space.

The planned focus, stated as a plan and nothing more, is the one the name itself points at: a city-scale, “smart city” venture — the metropolis as a place where the Foundation's city-scale ideas would be tried. That is a direction, not a description. It does not say which city, what would be built there, who would build it, who would pay for it, or when any of it would happen, because none of those things is verified. A reader who wants the developed city-scale plan in the Foundation's materials should read the S10 SmartCiti.X series page instead — and should understand that reading it does not tell them anything about Metropolis-SmartCiti beyond the shared words in the name.

Nothing on this site is legal, tax, or financial advice.

One fork in the Foundation's materials should be known to every reader. The live site calls the organisation a “PBC” in the for-profit benefit-corporation sense, and some papers describe a for-profit benefit-corporation track, with equity, a SAFE, and a priced raise. The filed entity is a nonprofit public benefit corporation — a different legal form: a filed nonprofit cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. This site follows the filed status and notes the site label where relevant, rather than presenting the two as the same entity. Counsel would have to choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Being named on a website, in an internal list, or in a plan is not the same as being formed: a filing, a bank account, an EIN, a signed agreement, a customer, or a dollar of revenue would each be a separate, verifiable fact, and none is claimed here unless the page cites the document, the date, and the issuing authority.

Related to S10 SmartCiti.X by name only — do not merge them

The most important fact on this page is a negative one: Metropolis-SmartCiti is not S10 SmartCiti.X, and the two must not be merged. They share words — “SmartCiti” — and they share a city-scale direction. They do not share a verified identity. S10 SmartCiti.X is W3 Series 10 in the April 2026 internal list, with a developed plan for a proposed new corporation: the Trade Craft Academy, robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, robotics manufacturing, a suggested $25 million pre-money valuation and a suggested $10 million tranched raise, and a Louisiana, Nebraska, and Indiana geography. None of that plan is verified as belonging to Metropolis-SmartCiti. No document reviewed for this site states that Metropolis-SmartCiti is Series 10, is the same venture as SmartCiti.X under another name, is a subsidiary of it, is a customer of it, or shares its funding, team, or sites.

Merging them would do real damage. It would attach a $25 million suggested valuation and a $10 million suggested raise to a venture that has no verified plan at all, and it would attach a name-only venture's blank record to a series page that has been carefully built to state exactly what is and is not verified. It would also mislead anyone trying to diligence either venture: a person who had read the merged page would believe Metropolis-SmartCiti had a Louisiana launch and a union-trades programme, and would believe SmartCiti.X had an additional identity it has never been shown to have. Both beliefs would be false, and both would have been created by this site. So this site keeps them apart, links them only by name, and repeats the separation here so that no reader can miss it.

The same rule applies in the other direction. Nothing verified about Metropolis-SmartCiti — which is to say, almost nothing — should be read back into S10. The series page stands on its own evidence: the April 2026 internal list, the plan documents for a proposed new corporation, and the anchored SmartCiti Holodeck at 39.846, -86.622. Metropolis-SmartCiti has no anchor, no series number, and no plan document cited on this page, and it inherits none of S10's by association.

What a planned focus can and cannot mean

A planned focus is a direction of travel, not a destination, and it is worth being precise about what the city-scale direction does and does not imply. It implies that, if this venture were ever developed, the field it would work in is cities: how city-scale systems — learning, training, movement, energy, housing, work — could be organised with the Foundation's agent-managed model. It does not imply a city, because no city is named. It does not imply a site, because no site is named, priced, or under contract. It does not imply a building, a campus, or a facility of any kind. And it does not imply that the venture would be the first, the largest, or the only city-scale venture in the network: S10 SmartCiti.X already occupies the developed city-scale plan, and the BuilderSpace tour — a separate San Francisco startup-community hackathon tour to Louisiana and Nebraska — is a separate project with its own pages, not a Metropolis-SmartCiti site, venue, sponsor, or commitment.

There is a temptation, when a name is evocative, to write as if the evocation were evidence — to describe the metropolis that the name conjures, and to present the description as the venture. This page refuses that temptation. The metropolis in the name is a word, not a place, and until a document names a place, a plan, and a person responsible, there is no metropolis to describe. What this page can do, honestly, is hold the space: record the name, record the direction, record the separation from S10, and record the questions. That is a smaller page than a confident description would be. It is also a true one.

What the repository actually contains

The repository review of 2026-11-07 changed this page from name-only to repo-grounded, and the record below is drawn only from what that review read in AGIFutureFoundation/Metropolis-SmartCiti. The repository describes itself, in its README, as a “Monad Metropolis hackathon submission, Track 04: Trust, Identity & AI Infrastructure”, and as follows: “An AI agent swarm trains people and robots in the trades. The swarm comes from the SmartCiti.X Trade Craft Academy and the AGI Corp worlds.” That sentence is the concrete content behind the name: Metropolis-SmartCiti is the on-chain agent layer for the Trade Craft Academy curriculum — the S10 SmartCiti.X training system — not a city, a site, or a company. The “metropolis” in the name is still not a place; it is now a described technical layer, and this section records that description rather than the place the name conjures.

How it works, as stated in the repository. Every agent has a wallet and an ERC-8004 identity. Agents buy work from each other with x402 micropayments. Every payment is hash-chained. Buyers leave ERC-8004 reputation. Anything that matters — registering agents, minting, pausing, certifying a person — needs 2 of 3 human stewards signing with P-256 passkeys. That 2-of-3 human-steward pattern is the same governance shape as the Steward Council planned across the network, demonstrated here on a testnet rather than operating for any company.

What is live, and where. Live on Monad testnet (chain 10143), 41/41 transactions confirmed. Contracts, as stated in the repository (truncated addresses as published there): StewardCouncil 0x7582DdE4…, OmegaToken (tOMEGA, EIP-3009) 0xbc5F1B47…, OmegaAgentHub 0x32d1a038…, TradeCraftCredential (non-transferable) 0x8c556593…, ERC-8004 Identity Registry 0x8004A818…, Reputation Registry 0x8004B663…. Recorded activity: 12 agents, 11 x402 payments, 10 ERC-8004 reputation entries, and 4 Trade Craft credentials issued — Tower Crane Lift, Rigging Signal Call, and Weld Bead Run (the fourth credential is named in the repository's count, not by module name here). Spending caps per agent: 5 tOMEGA per payment, 50 tOMEGA per day. Tests: forge test 15/15, node agents/chains/test.mjs 13/13. Multi-chain: the same key on Monad plus ASI:Chain DevNet plus Solana devnet. What would have to happen next

Before Metropolis-SmartCiti could be described as anything more than a name, five things would have to happen, and each would have to be documented. First, a written statement of what the venture is for — its field, its intended first work, and how it differs from S10 SmartCiti.X — would have to be published, so the name stops being the whole description. Second, a legal form would have to be chosen and, if the venture is to be a company or a series, filed: the filing, the number, and the date would be cited here. Third, a person would have to be named as responsible — a founder, a director, or a steward — with their role stated in writing. Fourth, a first concrete step would have to be taken and recorded: a site, a partner, a pilot, or a product, with the agreement or document cited. Fifth, the relationship with S10 would have to be stated in writing — whether the two ventures are separate, related, or merged — by whoever is responsible for both, so that no reader has to infer it from a shared word.

Until those steps are taken, the honest public record for Metropolis-SmartCiti is this page: a name, a direction, a separation from S10, and a list of questions.

How it would be governed

Governance for Metropolis-SmartCiti, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed venture is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That model is itself planned, not operating for this project: no Steward Council roster, operating agreement, or published set of agent limits exists to point to today. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the project or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one, and any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement would need its own documents, its own issuer, and counsel review.
