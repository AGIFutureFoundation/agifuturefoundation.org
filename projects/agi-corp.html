What this is

AGI Corp is, in the Foundation's intended structure (structure v2), the company at the top: a Wyoming corporation that would head the network, run the venture studio, and manage the companies to the Foundation's mission statement and standards. The Foundation would manage companies to mission; AGI Corp would be the commercial head that builds and runs them; the W3 LLC series network and the Delaware cohort series would be the portfolio it builds in. That is the plan. What is verified is much smaller, and this page leads with the verified part, because the gap between the plan and the verification is the whole story of AGI Corp.

What is verified is a single document: an IRS notice, form CP575A, dated 2026-05-16, assigning an Employer Identification Number to a taxpayer named “AGI”, at 111 Maiden Ln Ste 540, San Francisco, CA 94108, with Form 1120 listed as the return to file. That is what an EIN notice proves: that the IRS assigned a number to a named taxpayer at an address, for tax purposes. It does not prove that a corporation was formed, in Wyoming or anywhere else. It does not prove that articles of incorporation were filed, that a Secretary of State accepted them, that directors were appointed, that shares were issued, that a bank account was opened, or that any business was conducted. Every one of those facts would need its own document, and none of them was in the files reviewed for this site.

The filing facts that anchor every page in this section are the same. AGI Future Foundation is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, Entity No. B20260196664, with Articles filed on 2026-04-24 and Secretary of State approval on 2026-04-30. Its Employer Identification Number was assigned on 2026-05-07. No IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files reviewed for this site, so no page here claims that the Foundation is tax-exempt, and no page claims that any donation to the Foundation is tax-deductible. Nothing on this site is legal, tax, or financial advice.

One fork in the Foundation's materials should be known to every reader. The live site calls the organisation a “PBC” in the for-profit benefit-corporation sense, and some papers describe a for-profit benefit-corporation track, with equity, a SAFE, and a priced raise. The filed entity is a nonprofit public benefit corporation — a different legal form: a filed nonprofit cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. This site follows the filed status and notes the site label where relevant, rather than presenting the two as the same entity. Counsel would have to choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Being named on a website, in an internal list, or in a plan is not the same as being formed: a filing, a bank account, an EIN, a signed agreement, a customer, or a dollar of revenue would each be a separate, verifiable fact, and none is claimed here unless the page cites the document, the date, and the issuing authority.

The EIN, stated exactly

Because the EIN notice is the only verified document for AGI Corp, it deserves to be quoted exactly, with nothing added. The notice is IRS form CP575A, dated 2026-05-16. The name shown is “AGI” — not “AGI Corp”, and the difference matters: a short name on a tax notice is not the same as a legal corporate name on articles of incorporation, and no document reviewed for this site states the two names as the same entity. The address shown is 111 Maiden Ln Ste 540, San Francisco, CA 94108 — the same address as the AGI Fellowship's filing, which is a fact worth noting and not worth over-reading: a shared mailing address does not prove a shared legal identity, board, or bank account. The form listed is Form 1120, consistent with a corporate taxpayer and not, by itself, proof of incorporation.

What the notice does not contain is as important as what it does. It contains no entity number, no state of incorporation, no filing date, no articles, and no registered agent. It does not name a director, an officer, a shareholder, or a bank, and it does not state that any shares were issued, any money was raised, or any business was conducted. A reader who treats the notice as proof of incorporation has made the single most common error in reading corporate records, and this page is written to make that error difficult: the banner says EIN only, this section says EIN only, and the “what exists” section below says EIN only once more.

The intended role: Wyoming head and venture studio

In structure v2, AGI Corp's intended role has two parts: structural (a Wyoming corporation at the head of the network, with the Foundation managing companies to mission and the W3 LLC series network and the Delaware cohort series, Cohort 1 Series 1–3 to confirm, as the portfolio) and operational (the venture studio, an AGI Corp function rather than a separate entity unless counsel advises otherwise, agent-managed and agent-assisted, with humans executing — signing, moving funds, hiring, and offering securities — and agents preparing, across six stage gates from Idea to Exit/Wind-down).

Both parts are intended, not verified as filed or operating. The Wyoming incorporation is not verified. The studio has formed nothing, funded nothing, and issued nothing under its model yet. No cohort has been verified as launched under the studio, no portfolio company has been verified as formed, and no economics have been agreed: the studio's economics structure exists in the planning papers, with no terms, and agents hold no equity. The suggested figures in the planning papers — a $20 million suggested pre-money for the AGI Corp studio and a $2 million suggested studio seed — are illustrative only. Nothing has been raised, nothing has been offered, and no SAFE has been issued.

There is also a structural tension in the plan that belongs in the same passage as the plan itself. The Foundation, as filed, is a California nonprofit. A nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent; control would run only through written mission agreements, designed with counsel. A plan that puts a for-profit corporation at the head of a network that includes a filed nonprofit therefore needs a legal design that keeps the nonprofit independent while letting the commercial head do its work — and that design does not yet exist in a verified document. Until counsel designs it, the “head” in structure v2 is a direction, not a legal fact.

The RP1 portal record — as reported from the portal

One further record exists for AGI Corp, and it is labelled carefully because of where it comes from. As reported from the RP1 portal, there is a company record numbered 130, with Company ID “agentbay”, legal name “AGI DAO Series 69”, and admin Mike Rice, Founder. That record is quoted here as reported from the portal, not as a filing, and the label matters: a portal record is a record in a software system, and it proves that someone entered those values into that system, not that a corporation was formed, not that a series was designated, and not that any legal entity bears the name “AGI DAO Series 69”. The number 69 is not a W3 series number — the W3 list runs Series 1–33 — and this page does not treat it as one. The portal record is also the only place where the name “AGI DAO Series 69” appears in the materials reviewed, and no filing, EIN notice, or operating agreement corroborates it.

The portal record is consistent with the wider verified fact about RP1: no fabric or service has ever been attached for AGI Corp, and zero worlds are live on RP1. A company record in a portal is not an attachment, a fabric is not a service, and a name in a database is not a live world.

No SAFE issued

Fundraising for AGI Corp exists in the Foundation's papers as drafts, and drafts only: a post-money SAFE draft, a board authorisation, and a term sheet — with all terms blank. No SAFE has been issued, no money has been raised, no investor has been named, and no Form D has been filed. A SAFE with blank terms is a template waiting for decisions that have not been made, and for counsel's review and the applicable securities filings. The drafts prove that someone prepared for a raise, and they prove nothing about a raise having happened.

The same discipline applies to the alternative issuer. The Foundation's papers also contain a for-profit benefit-corporation raise structure, with a suggested $500,000–$1.5 million first SAFE tranche, a suggested $8–$15 million cap, and a 20% discount or none. That structure is for a for-profit California benefit corporation only, and it is an alternative issuer to AGI Corp, not an additional raise alongside it: the same round cannot be issued by two issuers. Which issuer would be used, if any, is a choice counsel would have to make in writing first, and no choice has been verified. No SAFE has been issued under either structure.

What would have to happen next

Before AGI Corp could be described as a formed company, five things would have to happen, in order, and each would have to be documented: incorporation (articles filed with a named Secretary of State, with the entity number and date cited here); organisation (bylaws, a board, and officers appointed in writing); identity (an EIN in the corporation's legal name, a bank account, and a registered agent); a written counsel design resolving how a for-profit head relates to a filed nonprofit Foundation whose board must remain independent; and only then fundraising, with terms set, counsel review, and the applicable securities filings — and the choice of issuer made in writing first.

Until those steps are taken, the honest public record for AGI Corp is this page: an EIN notice, a portal record quoted as reported, a plan for a Wyoming head and a venture studio, and an incorporation that is unverified.

How it would be governed

Governance for AGI Corp, as planned, follows the model used across the intended W3 network. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans. Humans decide, humans sign, and humans move funds. Agents do not sign documents, do not move money, do not hire or fire, do not offer securities, and do not enact doctrine or charters. A human Steward Council would hold decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable. That separation is deliberate: the point of an agent-managed venture is not to remove human responsibility, but to make routine work faster while keeping consequential decisions in human hands.

That model is itself planned, not operating for this project: no Steward Council roster, operating agreement, or published set of agent limits exists to point to today. The Foundation's role in the intended structure is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one is a commitment by the project or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one, and any future fundraising, partnership, or customer agreement would need its own documents, its own issuer, and counsel review.
