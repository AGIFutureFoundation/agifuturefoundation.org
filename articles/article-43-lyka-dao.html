The work

The April 2026 internal W3 series list names “W3 LLC-Lyka DAO — Series 26” and gives no description at all: no domain, no product, no team, no partner, no technical scope, and no statement of what “Lyka” refers to. There is no verified material beyond the name and the number, and this article will not invent any. A reader who wants to know what Lyka DAO does is entitled to a plain answer, and the plain answer today is that it has not been published. That is not a failure of this article; it is the article reporting the state of the record accurately, so that nobody mistakes a name for a venture.

That thinness is itself information, and it is worth stating why rather than papering over it. A series name without a published focus cannot be evaluated: there is no mission to check against the Foundation’s public-benefit standard, no market to validate, no risk to assess, and no partner to contact. Until a human steward writes down what Lyka DAO is for — in a sentence that a stranger could test — the series cannot pass even the first stage gate of the Foundation’s intended studio model, which requires an idea to be stated before it can be validated. The name alone is not an idea. A thin honest page is more useful than a full invented one, because an invented page would mislead exactly the people — potential stewards, partners, and reviewers — whose trust the series would need most.

What can be said is what the structure around the name implies. “DAO” in the W3 naming pattern denotes a series intended to be DAO-managed day to day: agents operating within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds. That is the governance pattern for the series network as a whole, not a fact about Lyka DAO’s subject matter. It tells a reader how the series would be run if it were formed; it says nothing about what it would run. Similarly, its place as Series 26, between Mypet.AI (Series 25) and Genomic DAO (Series 27), is a numbering fact from the internal list, not a statement that the three series are related in subject. They are neighbours on a list, nothing more, and no shared work, shared governance, or shared resources exist between them.

It is also worth saying what Lyka DAO is not. It is not a formed company, not a funded venture, and not an investment of any kind; no terms, no token, no membership, and no offering exist. It is not a live DAO: there is no wallet, no contract, no chain, no token, no members, and no votes on record. It has no product, no users, no revenue, and no partners. If a focus is published in future, this article should be rewritten from that publication — not from speculation — and dated so a reader can see what changed and when. A page that grows one verified fact at a time is a page a reviewer can trust; a page that grows by speculation is not.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Lyka DAO would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Lyka DAO. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for Lyka DAO. No coordinates, no fabric JSON, no hosted 3D assets, and no RP1 attach are claimed for this series, because none is verified.

What would be needed is a short, concrete list. First, an anchor: a named place with published coordinates that the series has actually chosen. Second, a fabric: a fabric JSON file describing that world, published where it can be checked. Third, hosted assets: the 3D models and supporting files the world needs, actually hosted and reachable, so the world renders instead of failing to load. Fourth, an RP1 attach: a verified step that brings the world into RP1, confirmed by a live check rather than asserted on a page. Zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series in the network.

That standard is stated here because the Foundation’s metaverse work elsewhere shows how easy it is to overclaim: a fabric file can be live on a public host while the assets it points to are not. If the series later gains an anchor, this section should be updated with the coordinates, the fabric link, the asset hosting, and the date and result of the live render check.

What is planned

  • Write a one-page statement of purpose answering four questions: what problem, for whom, why a DAO-managed series, and what counts as success or failure in the first year.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Lyka DAO is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. The only honest “related series” for Lyka DAO are its neighbours on the internal list, Mypet.AI (Series 25) and Genomic DAO (Series 27), and the other DAO-named series such as Avatar.Chat DAO. Those are list neighbours, not subject-matter relationships, and no shared work, shared governance, or shared resources exist between them. Any future relationship would need a written agreement; none exists today.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.

Open questions

  1. What does “Lyka” refer to, and what problem is this series for, in one testable sentence?

  4. What would count as success or failure in the first year, and who would judge it?
  5. What would count as validation before any build, and what would cause the series to be wound down rather than continued?
  6. What would be lost if this series never formed — and who, specifically, is waiting for it?
