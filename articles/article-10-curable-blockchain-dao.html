The work

Curable.Blockchain DAO is W3 Series 17, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. The name itself is the most that the files reviewed say about it: two ideas joined together — “Curable,” pointing toward health conditions that can be treated or cured, and “Blockchain,” pointing toward a shared, tamper-evident record — governed as a DAO. Everything beyond that on this article is a description of a planned focus implied by the name, clearly marked as such, not a report of work already done.

The planned focus, as the name implies it, would be health work where a trustworthy shared record matters: a condition is diagnosed, a treatment is given, an outcome is observed, and each step is recorded in a way that the patient, the clinician, and any later reviewer can check without having to take any single party’s word for it. That is the kind of problem a blockchain record is often proposed for — not because a ledger treats anyone, but because treatment only helps the next patient if the record of what was tried and what happened survives intact, in order, and attributable to the people and institutions involved.

It helps to say plainly what “curable” does and does not promise here. It does not promise that any particular disease is curable, and it does not promise that this series has a treatment, a drug, a device, or a clinic. No condition, no therapy, no trial, no clinician, and no patient group is named in the files reviewed for this series. The word “curable” in the name is an orientation — toward conditions where treatment can change the outcome, rather than conditions where only monitoring or comfort is possible — and the series, if it is ever built, would need to name its first condition, in writing, before any of the rest could be judged.

The “Blockchain” half of the name is equally a direction, not a system. A blockchain record for health work would need to answer questions this article cannot answer yet: who writes to the record, who can read it, how a patient consents and withdraws consent, how a mistaken entry is corrected without rewriting history, and how the record complies with health privacy law in every jurisdiction where it operates. Those are design decisions for a future governing document, not details that can be filled in by enthusiasm. A ledger that nobody trusts, nobody can correct, or nobody is allowed to read is worse than no ledger at all.

The “DAO” half of the name states the intended governance shape: day-to-day operation by agents within human-set limits, with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council, as described in the stewardship section below. That model is planned for the whole W3 programme. It is not operating for this series today, and no DAO vote, proposal, treasury, or token for this series is claimed anywhere on this article. Readers should therefore treat this article as a placeholder with a direction, not a prospectus: it says what the name points at, what the name does not promise, and what would have to be decided before the series could do anything at all.

Metaverse connection

Curable.Blockchain DAO has no anchored metaverse fabric yet. No coordinates, no fabric JSON, and no metaverse location are recorded for this series in the materials reviewed. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one — it would send a reader, or a builder, to a place that does not exist. Zero worlds are live on RP1.

What would be needed for a metaverse presence is the same sequence that the anchored series went through, and it has not started for this series: a decision about what a world for this series would be for, a fabric definition published at a checkable address, the assets that fabric references hosted at the paths it expects, and a verified render in Artemis. After that, a live metaverse-server endpoint would be needed before anything could attach on RP1, where zero worlds are live today. None of those steps has been taken, and none is claimed here.

It is worth asking whether a health-record DAO needs a metaverse world at all, and the honest answer is that it probably does not need one first. A record-keeping series’ first home is a governing document, a consent design, and a working ledger — not a world. A world could one day host training or demonstration of how the record works, but only after the series could say what it records, who can read it, and how consent works. Building the world first would put the venue ahead of the governance, and this article does not recommend that order.

The DAO model and stewardship

The operating model planned for every W3 series is the same, and it is worth stating in full on each article page, because it is the part of the plan that decides who can actually do anything. In the intended model, Curable.Blockchain DAO would be DAO-managed day to day: agents operate the routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides everything that carries a legal, financial, or doctrinal consequence. Agents prepare; humans execute. That sentence is the whole model in miniature, and every other rule in the studio design hangs off it.

The studio model names eight agent roles for each series: research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting, with a coordinator covering any role left unfilled. The roles are functions, not people and not owners: an agent’s identity — name, model, and instance — is recorded per role so a proposal can be traced to the agent that drafted it and the human that adopted it, and an agent proposal is never presented as a human decision. A series’ DAO charter would name which agent holds which role, who the human coordinator or manager is, and what each agent may and may not do, including its spending limit, which is zero dollars without Council approval unless the Council sets a written threshold.

The human gate is where the model gets its teeth. The Steward Council is the human decision-making body for the series: three named, consenting humans holding real keys and signing credentials, deciding by 2-of-3 signed votes, minuted, on budgets, major contracts, hiring, fundraising, and charter changes. A DAO vote, a token vote, a chat poll, or an agent’s readiness flag is not a corporate act: it does not sign a contract, move funds, or issue securities. Only the council, with signed authority, does that. Agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charter changes.

Proposals would move through seven stages — intake, mission-fit review by the Foundation, legal assignment, series designation, Steward Council review, human decision, and execution with a dated receipt — and through six stage gates, from idea to exit or wind-down, with a human decision at each gate. Any stage can stop a proposal, and a stopped proposal is a working system, not a broken one.

None of this is operating for Curable.Blockchain DAO today. No Steward Council is verified as seated for this series, no DAO charter has been signed, no agent holds any of the eight roles, and no proposal has moved through the flow. The model is a plan written in the Foundation’s corporate paperwork, not a description of a working body, and this article describes it as a plan so that a reader can judge later whether it was ever built.

How it fits the mission

Curable.Blockchain DAO sits in the third tier of the Foundation’s intended three-tier architecture. The apex tier is governance: the AGI Future Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation, holding the mission standard — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate for the series below it. The middle tier is the commercial engine: W3 LLC, an intended Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series with isolated assets and liability. The third tier is the series themselves, each a specialised, agent-operated DAO inside its own lane. Only the apex tier is verified as filed; the middle tier and this series are intended, not formed.

Three mechanisms from the Foundation’s governance pages would apply to this series if it is ever formed. The Fiduciary Shield is the proposed protection for a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns: California benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule for benefit-considering decisions, under Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, acquired, or have its purpose amended by the required vote. Open Global Investment is the proposed model that decouples economics from control — broad economic participation through Class A shares with one vote each, a larger voice for screened partners through Class B with ten votes, and decisive control through Class C with one thousand votes, held only by the Foundation — but it belongs to the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation track, not to the filed nonprofit, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. The Windfall Clause is a planned legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; the threshold, share, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today.
