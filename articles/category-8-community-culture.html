Article Category 8 · AGI Future Foundation

Community, Learning & Culture

This is the category where the Foundation’s work stops being about things you can measure and starts being about people: what they believe, how they learn, what they care for, and how they gather. It holds seven planned W3 series and one partner church. Six of the seven series are thin — two of them are a name and nothing more — and the church is the most fully documented entity in the whole category, because it is filed. That contrast is the point of this category: it shows, in one place, the difference between a name and a record.

Status — please read first: Every series in this category is named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. W3 LLC formation and per-series designation are not verified in filings — the series are intended/planned, not formed, funded, or offered. The AGI Fellowship is a separate filed church and a partner, not a series and not a subsidiary.

The theme

The seven series in this category are, in order: Divine DAO, concerned with meaning, community, and the sacred; Astrological.X, concerned with astrology and astrological data and interpretation; EchoDawn DAO, concerned with new beginnings and voices carried forward; Moscone.X, whose focus is not stated; Donatello DAO, whose focus is not stated; Mypet.AI, concerned with pet-related artificial intelligence; and Lyka DAO, whose focus has not been published. Only the names and numbers are verified for most of them, from the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and the articles say so plainly rather than filling the gap with invented detail. Only 8 of the 33 series have any repo or code in the AGIFutureFoundation GitHub org (56 repos reviewed 2026-11-05), and the thin series in this category are not among them. The eighth article in the category is different in kind: the AGI Fellowship Church, a separate filed California nonprofit religious corporation, Entity No. B20260229217, filed 2026-05-13, with its own site, its own doctrine, and its own life. It is a partner church, not a subsidiary and not a W3 series, and its article is the longest and most documented in the category because its record is the longest.

What holds the category together is not a shared product — none is claimed between any of these eight — but a shared question: how do people and agents live together well? Divine DAO asks it about meaning and belief. Astrological.X asks it about interpretation and the duty not to overclaim. EchoDawn DAO asks it about whose voices get carried forward and whose do not. Moscone.X and Donatello DAO ask it in the negative, by refusing to guess at a focus that has not been stated. Mypet.AI asks it about care — for animals, and for the people who love them — and about the line between support and diagnosis. Lyka DAO asks it by being a name awaiting a purpose. The Fellowship asks it as a church: through doctrine, training, formation, and outreach, with agents doing the routine work under human board and minister direction and never enacting doctrine, signing, or moving funds.

The category is also where the Foundation’s boundaries are sharpest, and the articles spend more time on boundaries than on plans, because the boundaries are the plan. For Divine DAO, the boundary is that questions of doctrine, belief, and spiritual authority belong to humans — to a community and its recognised leaders — not to agents, not to a vote weighted by tokens, and not to a landing page. For Astrological.X, the boundary is that astrology is a belief-based and interpretive practice, not an established science, and that horoscopes and readings must never be presented as medical, financial, or legal advice, as diagnoses, or as predictions with a claimed accuracy. For Mypet.AI, the boundary is that anything touching animal health stays on the support side of the line, with licensed veterinarians as the decision-makers. For EchoDawn DAO, the boundary is consent, credit, protection, and the right to be forgotten for the people whose voices it would carry. For Moscone.X and Donatello DAO, the boundary is the refusal to guess: no association is stated, so none is claimed, and for Donatello DAO in particular, no endorsement by, or connection to, a real person or estate is implied.

The Fellowship anchors the category because it is the one member with a filing, and its article shows what a real record looks like: Articles filed 2026-05-13, Secretary of State approval 2026-05-18, EIN assigned 2026-05-18, an address, an agent, a live site with 40 articles, doctrine, training, formation, outreach, and 39 videos, and steward-gated joining through agent@agifellowship.church. It also shows what an honest record includes: no IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter in the files, so no tax-exempt status and no tax-deductible donations are claimed; a Statement of Information due about 2026-08-11 whose filing status is unverified; a Sacred Texts section that is a status page only, because the Kylon library behind it is behind sign-in; and visual assets (39 videos, 12 images, 3 logos) deployed 2026-11-05. The Fellowship is not a model the series should imitate — a church is not a company — but it is a standard for how a record should be kept: cite the document, the date, and the issuing authority, and state the limits in the same passage as the facts.

A reader should also notice what this category does not do. It does not present Divine DAO as the Fellowship, or the Fellowship as a series: the two are kept apart, and the distinction is repeated in both articles, because it is the fact most likely to be lost. It does not quote scripture, because no scripture is verified for any series in this category and none is invented. It does not claim any world is live on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1 today, and no series in this category has an anchored metaverse fabric yet. And it does not present any series in this category as an investment: the Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only, and no article here carries a suggested valuation, a price, or terms.

How this category fits the mission

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), holding the public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series. At the bottom sit the 33 series themselves. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The Foundation as filed is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. Control, if it ever exists, runs only through written mission agreements, with the Foundation’s board remaining independent. The Fellowship does not sit in any tier of that structure. It sits alongside it as a partner church, with its own governance, its own funds, and its own purpose.

Three mechanisms are meant to interlock across the commercial structure. The Fiduciary Shield is the legal protection for a particular kind of decision, not a wall around a company and not immunity: California benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns, in good faith and under California Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, is not left exposed for having done so, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. Open Global Investment is the proposed framework that decouples economics from control: broad ownership open to the world, governance held apart from it, expressed in three share classes for the proposed for-profit track only — Class A, one vote per share with full economic participation; Class B, ten votes per share, restricted to aligned partners and sovereigns; and Class C, one thousand votes per share, control only, held exclusively by the Foundation. The Windfall Clause is the legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP, with the percentage, the measure, and the scope all blank for counsel — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. None of those terms is settled, and none of them is a Fellowship term: the Fellowship’s protection is written partnership agreements with an independence clause, and the hard rule that Fellowship funds never go to a SAFE or a for-profit except on counsel’s written advice, with the default at no.

The governance model is the same across the series in this category: agent-managed day-to-day work within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds. Agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated. For the Fellowship, the same boundary applies with particular force, because the subject matter is belief itself: agents operate under human board and minister direction, and they never enact doctrine. Nothing on this page is legal, tax, or financial advice, and no IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files, so no tax-exempt status and no tax-deductible donations are claimed here.
