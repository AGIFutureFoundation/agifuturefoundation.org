Mypet.AI: Companion Intelligence, With the Vet Still in Charge

Pets are the most emotionally immediate subject in the W3 list — and one of the easiest to get wrong. Mypet.AI is the planned series for pet-related artificial intelligence: companion and care tools for pet owners, support for veterinary and shelter work, or pet-related content and community. Only its name and number are verified, and this article is careful about the lines a series in this lane must never cross.

Mypet.AI series page (S25)

Status — please read first: Named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. W3 LLC formation and per-series designation are not verified in filings — this series is intended/planned, not formed, funded, or offered.

A note on how thin this article is: very little is verified about this series beyond its name and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. This article says so plainly rather than filling the gap with invented detail. There is also no repo or code for this series in the AGIFutureFoundation GitHub org (56 repos reviewed 2026-11-05; only 8 of the 33 series have any repo coverage) — the article is about the named intention and the stewardship model. What follows is the domain the name implies, labelled as a planned focus throughout.

TL;DR

Mypet.AI (W3 Series 25) is a planned, pet-focused AI series. The April 2026 internal list names it and gives no further description, so everything here is a planned focus drawn from the name. The lane is wide — owner tools, veterinary support, shelter support — and none of those has been chosen. Three boundaries would have to hold from the start: anything touching animal health stays on the support side of the line, with licensed veterinarians as the decision-makers; owner data is handled consent-first, sharing off by default; and software is never presented as a substitute for human or veterinary care. The series is at stage zero: an idea, a name on a list.

The work

The planned focus implied by the name is pet-related artificial intelligence: AI built around companion animals and the people who care for them. That is a wide lane, and the honest thing to say is that its boundaries have not been set. A pet-focused AI series could, in principle, cover companion and care tools for pet owners; support for veterinary and shelter work; or pet-related content and community. Each of those is a different product, a different regulatory posture, and a different set of partners, and none of them has been chosen. The first real decision would be to write down, in one page, which lane it is in and which it is not, and to have a human Steward Council approve that scope.

What can be said with confidence is what the series would have to get right if it ever operates in this lane. Anything touching animal health would need to stay firmly on the support side of the line: general information and organisation, not diagnosis or treatment, with licensed veterinarians as the decision-makers and with clear escalation to a vet for anything medical. That line is not a courtesy; it is the difference between a tool and a liability. An owner told by software what is wrong with their animal has been given a diagnosis by a machine that cannot examine the patient. A series that crossed that line would be practising veterinary medicine without a licence, and no agent, no model, and no landing page can authorise that. The human in the loop here is not a reviewer; it is the professional whose judgement the whole service depends on.

Anything touching pet owners’ data — photos, locations, health notes — would need consent-first handling, with sharing off by default and a plain-language explanation of what is stored and why. Pet data is family data in practice: photos of a home, locations of walks, notes about a household’s routine. A series that collected it without asking, or shared it without saying so, would break trust with exactly the people it most needs. And anything presented as companionship, for the pet or the owner, would need to be honest about what it is: software, not a substitute for human or veterinary care. A companion interface that pretended to be a living thing, or that let a lonely owner believe it was, would be a deception, not a product.

The pet lane also illustrates why the Foundation’s stage-gate model matters: a series moves through stages — idea, validate, build, launch, operate and scale, exit or wind down — and only passes a gate when a human review says it has. Mypet.AI is at stage zero: an idea, a name on a list. Validation would mean talking to real pet owners, shelters, and veterinarians and learning whether there is a problem worth solving that this series is well placed to solve; it would not mean building first and finding users later. That discipline is the difference between a series and a slide. The validation conversations are also the cheapest way to find out whether the lane is even the right one: a shelter, a vet, and an owner would name three different problems, and only the people who live with the problem can say which one matters.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Mypet.AI would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Mypet.AI. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for Mypet.AI. No coordinates, no fabric JSON, no hosted 3D assets, and no RP1 attach are claimed for this series, because none is verified.

What would be needed is a short, concrete list. First, an anchor: a named place with published coordinates that the series has actually chosen. Second, a fabric: a fabric JSON file describing that world, published where it can be checked. Third, hosted assets: the 3D models and supporting files the world needs, actually hosted and reachable, so the world renders instead of failing to load. Fourth, an RP1 attach: a verified step that brings the world into RP1, confirmed by a live check rather than asserted on a page. Zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series in the network.

That standard is stated here because the Foundation’s metaverse work elsewhere shows how easy it is to overclaim: a fabric file can be live on a public host while the assets it points to are not. If the series later gains an anchor, this section should be updated with the coordinates, the fabric link, the asset hosting, and the date and result of the live render check.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Mypet.AI is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. The most natural neighbours are the series that work on intelligence people interact with directly: Cognition.X (Series 1), Empathic.X (Series 2), and Avatar.Chat DAO (Series 23). Any overlap — for example, a companion interface that Mypet.AI and Avatar.Chat DAO might both touch — would need to be written down as a boundary agreement between the series, not left to drift. No such agreement exists today.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.

Open questions

These are genuine unanswered questions, not rhetorical ones. Each needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series can pass its first stage gate.

  1. Which lane is Mypet.AI in — owner tools, veterinary support, shelter support, or something else — and which lanes is it explicitly not in?
  2. Who is the human steward accountable for animal-welfare and data decisions, and what veterinary advice has been taken before any health-adjacent claim is made?
  3. What would validation look like: which pet owners, shelters, or veterinarians would need to say this is worth building before any build begins?
  4. How would the line between support and diagnosis be drawn, shown to every user, and enforced — and what escalation to a vet would be built in?
  5. What consent-first rules would govern photos, locations, and health notes, including sharing off by default and deletion?
  6. Where does Mypet.AI end and Avatar.Chat DAO begin — what boundary agreement would prevent two series building the same companion interface?
