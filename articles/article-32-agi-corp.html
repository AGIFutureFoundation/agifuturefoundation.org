AGI Corp

AGI Corp is the proposed management company and venture-studio head of the Foundation's intended structure, and the most important fact about it is a negative one. It has an EIN, assigned 2026-05-16. It has no verified incorporation: no articles of incorporation, no entity number, and no Secretary of State filing were in the files reviewed. An EIN alone does not prove incorporation, and this article does not present it as proof.

TL;DR

AGI Corp is, in the Foundation's intended structure, the company at the top: an intended Wyoming corporation that would head the network, run the venture studio, and manage the companies to the Foundation's mission statement and standards. What is verified is a single document: an IRS notice, form CP575A, dated 2026-05-16, assigning an Employer Identification Number to AGI, at 111 Maiden Ln Ste 540, San Francisco, CA 94108, with Form 1120 listed. No SAFE has been issued, and nothing has been formed, funded, or issued under the studio model.

The work

Because the EIN notice is the only verified document for AGI Corp, it deserves to be quoted exactly, with nothing added. The notice is IRS form CP575A, dated 2026-05-16. The name shown is “AGI”, not “AGI Corp”, and the difference matters: a short name on a tax notice is not the same as a legal corporate name on articles of incorporation, and no document reviewed for this site states the two names as the same entity. The address shown is 111 Maiden Ln Ste 540, San Francisco, CA 94108, the same address as the AGI Fellowship's filing, which is a fact worth noting and not worth over-reading: a shared mailing address does not prove a shared legal identity, board, or bank account. The form listed is Form 1120, consistent with a corporate taxpayer and not, by itself, proof of incorporation. What the notice does not contain matters as much as what it does: no entity number, no state of incorporation, no filing date, no articles, and no registered agent, and no director, officer, shareholder, or bank named, nor any statement that shares were issued, money raised, or business conducted.

In structure v2, AGI Corp's intended role has two parts: structural, a Wyoming corporation at the head of the network, with the Foundation managing companies to mission and the W3 LLC series network and the Delaware cohort series, Cohort 1 Series 1 to 3, to confirm, as the portfolio; and operational, the venture studio, an AGI Corp function rather than a separate entity unless counsel advises otherwise, agent-managed and agent-assisted, with humans executing, signing, moving funds, hiring, and offering securities, and agents preparing, across six stage gates from Idea to Exit or Wind-down. Both parts are intended, not verified as filed or operating. The Wyoming incorporation is not verified. The studio has formed nothing, funded nothing, and issued nothing under its model yet. No cohort has been verified as launched under the studio, no portfolio company has been verified as formed, and no economics have been agreed: the studio's economics structure exists in the planning papers, with no terms, and agents hold no equity.

The studio model itself deserves a careful description, because it is the most developed design in the Foundation's papers and the easiest to mistake for an operating business. The studio has eight functions: sourcing and intake, validation, building, launch, operations and scaling, portfolio management, fundraising support, and mission and compliance. Three deserve extra attention. In validation, the model requires human interviews, not agent assertions alone, and a financial model stays labelled draft, not adopted, until a human adopts it. In launch, the claims check is absolute: no invented partners, users, revenue, venues, or outcomes, with the claims list filed before launch approval is signed, and pricing set and approved, or the launch stated expressly as free or demo-only. In fundraising support, agents draft decks, data rooms, and diligence packs, and do not solicit investment. The eight agent roles are the staffing that performs these functions inside each series: the functions are the work, and the roles are who does it.

Fundraising for AGI Corp exists in the Foundation's papers as drafts, and drafts only: a post-money SAFE draft, a board authorisation, and a term sheet, with all terms blank. No SAFE has been issued, no money has been raised, no investor has been named, and no Form D has been filed. A SAFE with blank terms is a template waiting for decisions that have not been made, and for counsel's review and the applicable securities filings. The same discipline applies to the alternative issuer. The Foundation's papers also contain a for-profit benefit-corporation raise structure, with a suggested $500,000 to $1.5 million first SAFE tranche, a suggested $8 million to $15 million cap, and a 20 percent discount or none. That structure is for a for-profit California benefit corporation only, and it is an alternative issuer to AGI Corp, not an additional raise alongside it: the same round cannot be issued by two issuers. Which issuer would be used, if any, is a choice counsel would have to make in writing first, and no choice has been verified. No SAFE has been issued under either structure. One further record exists, as reported from the RP1 portal, not as a filing: company record 130, Company ID agentbay, legal name AGI DAO Series 69, admin Mike Rice, Founder. A portal record proves only that someone entered those values into that system, and 69 is not a W3 series number.

The DAO model and stewardship

The planned governance model for AGI Corp is the model described across the Foundation's governance and operations pages, and it is worth stating in full here because it is the part of the plan that most often gets misread. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans, in the eight roles of the Venture Studio Operating Model (research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting). Those roles describe functions, not people and not owners, and no agent holds any of them for AGI Corp today.

Above the agents sits the human gate. A human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds: a DAO vote, a token vote, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act, and only a named human acting within signed, written authority can sign a contract, move money, issue securities, or enact a charter. The Council would set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable.

The hard limits bind every agent role equally. Agents NEVER sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. For AGI Corp, that model is planned, not operating: no Steward Council is verified as seated, no operating agreement is in the files reviewed, and no published set of agent limits exists.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for AGI Corp: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are verified for it, and this article invents none of them. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one.

Zero worlds are live on RP1, for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to AGI Corp. A metaverse presence would need four checkable things: published coordinates, a fabric JSON file, hosted assets that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach a reviewer can open and verify. Until all four exist, the correct statement remains no anchored metaverse fabric yet. Whether this item even needs one is an open question: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the work happens in documents, code, and human conversation and needs no world.

How it fits the mission

AGI Corp fits the mission as the intended commercial head and the studio that would build the portfolio, with the Foundation as mission steward above it, or rather beside it, under written mission agreements. The three-tier structure is the Foundation as governance and mission tier, the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine, and the 33 named series as specialised cells, with AGI Corp intended to head the commercial side and run the studio that builds in it. The Fiduciary Shield would be the legal layer protecting the board that stewards the companies, Open Global Investment the framework that keeps governance and economics apart through Class A, B, and C shares, and the Windfall Clause the pre-commitment that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination. None of those mechanisms is in force for AGI Corp today, because the company itself is not verified as formed, and the fork between the filed nonprofit track and the for-profit benefit-corporation track must be chosen in writing by counsel before any of it can be relied on.

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), as governance and mission steward. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC, and below it the 33 named W3 sub-series in the April 2026 internal list. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The middle and bottom tiers are intended, not established, and nothing on this page should be read as saying the Foundation owns, controls, or has formed AGI Corp.

Three mechanisms hold the intended structure together, and each is a plan with work still to do. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions under California Corporations Code sections 14620 to 14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a board can still be sued and must still act in good faith. Open Global Investment is the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation framework only: Class A, one vote and full economics, open to the global public; Class B, ten votes, for restricted aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules; Class C, 1,000 votes, control-only, reserved to the Foundation. Its suggested first tranche is a SAFE of $500,000 to $1,500,000 with a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only, and a second priced tranche whose price would be set then, not today. The PBC track and the AGI Corp SAFE are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. The Windfall Clause is a pre-commitment, hardwired before profits exist, that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through four Benefit Buckets, Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Its threshold measure, scope, share, and recipient are blank for counsel, and it can be amended only by supermajority plus Class C.

Two cautions belong in the same passage. First, the filed Foundation is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent, with control only through written mission agreements designed with counsel. The for-profit track is separate and proposed, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Second, the Foundation's fundraising as filed is donation and grant only. No suggested figure here is an offering, a price, a term, or a promise of any return, and suggested figures are never added into a family total.
