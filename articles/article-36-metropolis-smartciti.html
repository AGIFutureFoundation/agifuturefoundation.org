Metropolis-SmartCiti: A Name, a Direction, and a Line Not to Cross

Metropolis-SmartCiti appears by name in the Foundation’s network materials as a venture. That is, on the evidence reviewed, the whole of what is verified about it: no legal form, no filing, no product, no team, no location, no customer, no revenue, no date. This article exists because a name that appears in public materials deserves a public page that is honest about how little is known.

Metropolis-SmartCiti project page

Status — please read first: Intended / planned unless a filing is cited. Nothing on this page is formed, funded, or offered.

TL;DR

Metropolis-SmartCiti is a named network venture, not a W3 series: it has no series number and is not in the April 2026 internal list of 33 named series. Its planned focus, stated as a plan and nothing more, is the city scale — the metropolis as a place where the Foundation’s city-scale ideas would be tried. The most important fact on this page is a negative one: Metropolis-SmartCiti is not S10 SmartCiti.X, and the two must not be merged. None of SmartCiti.X’s developed plan, suggested figures, geography, or Holodeck anchor belongs to Metropolis-SmartCiti, and nothing verified about Metropolis-SmartCiti reads back into S10.

The work

A planned focus is a direction of travel, not a destination, and it is worth being precise about what the city-scale direction does and does not imply. It implies that, if this venture were ever developed, the field it would work in is cities: how city-scale systems — learning, training, movement, energy, housing, work — could be organised with the Foundation’s agent-managed model. It does not imply a city, because no city is named. It does not imply a site, because no site is named, priced, or under contract. And it does not imply that the venture would be the first or the only city-scale venture in the network: S10 SmartCiti.X already occupies the developed city-scale plan, and the BuilderSpace tour is a separate project with its own pages, not a Metropolis-SmartCiti site, venue, sponsor, or commitment.

The separation from S10 SmartCiti.X deserves its own passage, because it is the fact most likely to be lost by a reader in a hurry. They share words — “SmartCiti” — and they share a city-scale direction. They do not share a verified identity. S10 SmartCiti.X is W3 Series 10 in the April 2026 internal list, with a developed plan for a proposed new corporation: the Trade Craft Academy, robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, robotics manufacturing, a suggested $25 million pre-money valuation and a suggested $10 million tranched raise, and a Louisiana, Nebraska, and Indiana geography. None of that plan is verified as belonging to Metropolis-SmartCiti. No document reviewed for this site states that Metropolis-SmartCiti is Series 10, is the same venture as SmartCiti.X under another name, is a subsidiary of it, is a customer of it, or shares its funding, team, or sites.

Merging them would do real damage, and it is worth saying exactly how. It would attach a $25 million suggested valuation and a $10 million suggested raise to a venture that has no verified plan at all, and it would attach a name-only venture’s blank record to a series page that has been carefully built to state exactly what is and is not verified. It would mislead anyone trying to diligence either venture: a reader of the merged page would believe Metropolis-SmartCiti had a Louisiana launch and a union-trades programme, and SmartCiti.X had an identity it has never been shown to have. So this site keeps them apart, links them only by name, and repeats the separation here so that no reader can miss it. The same rule applies in the other direction: nothing verified about Metropolis-SmartCiti — which is to say, almost nothing — should be read back into S10.

There is one concrete record attached to the name, and it belongs in this article even though it does not make the venture any less thin. The Monad deployment is testnet only — chain 10143, built for the Monad Metropolis Track 04 hackathon — and its record is: 6 contracts deployed, 41 of 41 transactions confirmed, 12 agents registered, 11 x402 payments completed, and 4 credentials issued. That is a real, checkable testnet record, and this article celebrates it as exactly that: a testnet record, not a live production network, not a product, and not a user base. Nothing about the testnet changes the venture’s legal status, its lack of a team, a site, or a customer, or the separation from S10 SmartCiti.X. It does show that someone built something under this name and ran it on a public testnet, and that is more than a name alone — it is a name with a verifiable experiment attached.

What would have to happen before Metropolis-SmartCiti could be described as anything more than a name is a short, honest list, and each item on it would have to be documented. First, a written statement of what the venture is for — its field, its intended first work, and how it differs from S10 SmartCiti.X — would have to be published, so the name stops being the whole description. Second, a legal form would have to be chosen and, if the venture is to be a company or a series, filed: the filing, the number, and the date would be cited here. Third, a person would have to be named as responsible — a founder, a director, or a steward — with their role stated in writing. Fourth, a first concrete step would have to be taken and recorded: a site, a partner, a pilot, or a product, with the agreement or document cited. Fifth, the relationship with S10 would have to be stated in writing — whether the two ventures are separate, related, or merged — by whoever is responsible for both, so that no reader has to infer it from a shared word.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Metropolis-SmartCiti would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Metropolis-SmartCiti. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Metropolis-SmartCiti is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. The only verified relationship claimed is the name relationship with S10 SmartCiti.X, stated above as a non-merger. No operational partnership, shared customer, shared product, or shared funding with any other venture or series is claimed. See Article 33 for the S10 plan it must not be confused with.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.
