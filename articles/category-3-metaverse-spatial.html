Article Category 3 · AGI Future Foundation

Metaverse & Spatial

The metaverse is where the Foundation’s plans become places: worlds with coordinates, fabrics with assets, a browser to enter them through, and a DAO to govern them. This category is the honest record of that presence — what is anchored, what is only named, and what is still zero.

All articlesAll 33 series

What this category is

This category gathers the five articles about space: the places, the presence, and the infrastructure. Three of them are W3 series — Locator.X (S08), Avatar.Chat DAO (S23), and Metaverse DAO (S29) — and two are projects — WorldForge, which builds worlds, and the RP1 ecosystem, the spatial-computing infrastructure those worlds would run on. The category exists because the five belong together: a world needs a builder, a builder needs infrastructure, infrastructure needs a browser and a registry, and all of it needs governance. Read separately, each article is a single claim about a single name; read together, they are the whole spatial programme, with its gaps visible.

The theme that runs through all five is the difference between a place and a claim about a place. A world can be an entry in a JSON catalogue, a fabric definition file published on GitHub Pages, a fully rendered environment in a browser like Artemis, or a live place attached to RP1 that people can enter. Those are four different stages, and the programme’s work sits at different stages in different places. Six fabrics are anchored and published, but their assets are not hosted, so they do not render fully. The WorldForge-Browser repository lists 544 worlds, every one with no RP1 attachment recorded, while the live site states 80 worlds as site copy with no verified counting method. Zero worlds are live on RP1. This hub does not resolve those numbers into one; it keeps them apart, with their sources, so a reviewer can see what each one counts.

The second theme is governance before scale. Metaverse DAO is the series planned to govern the metaverse, and it has no world of its own; Avatar.Chat DAO is the series planned to govern conversation through avatars, and it has no community, no members, and no fabric. That looks like a paradox until the sequence is stated: standards follow activity, and activity does not exist yet. A DAO that writes standards for worlds nobody operates would be governing an empty room. The planned path in every article in this category is the same — one world with real activity and real stewards first, a small set of rules agreed with them, a fixed period of operating under those rules, a published review of what worked — and only then any discussion of a second world. The category is, in that sense, a record of a programme waiting for its first live world, and honest about the wait.

The third theme is identity. In a spatial programme, who you appear as is part of the work: an avatar carries identity, expression, and presence into a space where bodies cannot go, and a world collects data about its visitors. The articles in this category return, again and again, to the same questions: how is identity verified, what happens when an avatar is used to impersonate someone, what is recorded, who can read the record, and can a participant delete what they said? None of those questions is answered in the files reviewed, and no article in this category answers them by invention. They are the questions a future governing document would have to answer before anyone could usefully enter a world — and the fact that they are unanswered is part of why every article here describes its subject as planned rather than operating.

How the category fits the mission

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), holding the public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series. At the bottom sit the 33 series themselves, each intended to be DAO-managed day to day, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The Foundation as filed is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. Control, if it ever exists, runs only through written mission agreements, with the Foundation’s board remaining independent.

Three mechanisms are meant to interlock across that structure. The Fiduciary Shield is an expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions, under California Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630 — not immunity, and not a wall around a company. Open Global Investment is the proposed framework that decouples economics from control, expressed in three share classes for the proposed for-profit track only: Class A, one vote per share with full economic participation, for the global public; Class B, ten votes per share, restricted to aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export controls, and CFIUS review; and Class C, one thousand votes per share, control only with no economic participation, held exclusively by the Foundation. The Windfall Clause is the legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — a percentage of global GDP, with the percentage, the measure, and the scope all still blank for counsel — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination. For this category, the mission test is concrete: a spatial programme that counts its worlds honestly, governs its spaces before it scales them, and protects the identity and records of the people inside them is serving the public benefit; one that inflates its catalogue, governs after its first crisis, or treats visitors’ data as an afterthought is not.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only. No article in this category is an offering, and none promises a price, terms, or a return. Suggested valuations appear only where a landing page already states them, and none is added here. The stewardship rule applies to every article in the category alike: agents operate day-to-day work within human-set limits, and humans decide, sign, and move funds. Agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated.

How to read this hub

Read the status banner first, on every article. It is the same sentence for the three series articles — named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list, with W3 LLC formation and per-series designation not verified in filings — and a parallel sentence for the two project articles. “Anchored” means a fabric definition is published with verified coordinates; it does not mean live, and it does not mean rendering. “Live on RP1” is a claim this hub never makes, because zero worlds are live on RP1. Where a number disagrees with another number — 33 series, 544 catalogue entries, 80 worlds as site copy, 41 and 40 repository counts, 34 worlds, 35 cells — the article keeps the disagreement visible and quotes each figure with its source, rather than picking the largest.

If you hold a document that changes something stated here — a filing, a designation, a signed agreement, a hosted asset set, a verified render, or a live RP1 attach with evidence — send it to x@agifuturefoundation.org and the relevant article can be updated to cite it, with its date and source. Until such a document is produced, the honest status on these pages stands. The next articles in the sequence are Article 13 (Genome.Blockchain), which precedes this category, and Article 19 (Get.Delivery DAO), which opens the next category, Robotics & Physical AI.
