Article Category 7 · AGI Future Foundation

Cities, Energy & Infrastructure

Cities are where the Foundation’s mission stops being abstract. A city is a place where people learn a trade, earn a wage, live near their work, and watch new technology either include them or pass them by. Energy is what a city runs on, and infrastructure is what a city is made of. This category gathers the four articles that work in that territory: the most developed plan in the W3 network, two thin planned series, and a named venture that must not be confused with the plan.

Status — please read first: Every series in this category is named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. W3 LLC formation and per-series designation are not verified in filings — the series are intended/planned, not formed, funded, or offered. Metropolis-SmartCiti is a named network venture, not a series, and is related to S10 SmartCiti.X by name only.

The theme

The four articles in this category span the whole range of what the Foundation’s site can honestly say about a series. At one end sits SmartCiti.X, the most developed plan in the network: a proposed new corporation for robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, and robotics manufacturing, with a first launch in Louisiana and later work in Nebraska and Indiana, suggested figures of $25 million pre-money and a $10 million tranched raise, and an anchored Holodeck that does not yet render fully because roughly 200 GLB assets and map.wasm are not hosted. Only 8 of the 33 series have any repo or code in the AGIFutureFoundation GitHub org (56 repos reviewed 2026-11-05); SmartCiti.X is one of them, and ParkingSmart.X and Solar DAO are not. At the other end sits Metropolis-SmartCiti, a named network venture with almost nothing verified beyond its name — no filing, no product, no team, no site, no customer — whose article exists to hold the space honestly and to keep the name from being merged with SmartCiti.X. Between them sit ParkingSmart.X and Solar DAO, two thin planned series whose articles say plainly how little is verified and focus on the questions their future stewards would have to answer.

What holds the category together is not a shared product or a shared customer — none is claimed between any of these four — but a shared subject: the physical world. Cities, parking, and power are all things you can touch, measure, and stand in front of, and that physicality disciplines the writing. A parking prediction is either right or wrong. A solar installation either produces metered power or it does not. A training programme either places graduates or it does not. A site is either under contract or it is not. The articles in this category lean on that discipline: where a fact is physical, the article asks for the document, the meter reading, or the signed agreement behind it, and where no such evidence exists, the article says so rather than filling the gap with adjectives.

The category also shows the Foundation’s stage-gate model at work in its most honest form. In the intended venture-studio model, a series moves through stages — idea, validate, build, launch, operate and scale, exit or wind down — and only passes a gate when a human review says it has. SmartCiti.X has the most developed plan and the most explicit gates: Tranche 2 closes only on board certification of a signed union, a delivered AR/VR pilot, a first site closed or leased, a first manufacturing order or pilot customer, Tranche 1 reconciled, and a Windfall report drafted. Solar DAO’s validation would be a single working installation, honestly reported, rather than a portfolio plan. ParkingSmart.X cannot even begin until an owner or authority signs a written agreement. Metropolis-SmartCiti cannot be described as anything more than a name until a written statement of purpose exists. The gates are different because the subjects are different, but the principle is the same: nothing moves forward on a claim alone.

A reader should also notice what this category does not do. It does not present the BuilderSpace tour — a separate San Francisco startup-community hackathon tour to Louisiana and Nebraska — as a SmartCiti.X site, venue, sponsor, or commitment. The tour is a separate project with its own pages. It does not sum suggested figures into a family total: the $25 million and $10 million figures for SmartCiti.X are illustrative only, they belong to a proposed new corporation, and no other series in this category carries a suggested valuation at all. And it does not claim any world is live on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series in the network, and the one anchored world in this category — the SmartCiti.X Holodeck at 39.846, -86.622 — is an anchored fabric with missing assets, not a working world.

The articles are written to be read in order, but each stands alone. Article 33 is the plan. Article 34 is the coordination problem. Article 35 is the energy lane. Article 36 is the warning about what happens when two similar names are allowed to merge. Together they are an honest account of the Foundation’s city-scale ambitions: one developed plan, two thin domains, and one name that must be kept separate from the plan it resembles.

How this category fits the mission

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), holding the public-benefit purpose — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series. At the bottom sit the 33 series themselves. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The Foundation as filed is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. Control, if it ever exists, runs only through written mission agreements, with the Foundation’s board remaining independent.

Three mechanisms are meant to interlock across that structure. The Fiduciary Shield is the legal protection for a particular kind of decision, not a wall around a company and not immunity: California benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns, in good faith and under California Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, is not left exposed for having done so, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. Open Global Investment is the proposed framework that decouples economics from control: broad ownership open to the world, governance held apart from it, expressed in three share classes for the proposed for-profit track only — Class A, one vote per share with full economic participation; Class B, ten votes per share, restricted to aligned partners and sovereigns; and Class C, one thousand votes per share, control only, held exclusively by the Foundation. The Windfall Clause is the legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP, with the percentage, the measure, and the scope all blank for counsel — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Energy infrastructure is named among those destinations, which gives Solar DAO the clearest mission link in this category — a link that is a direction of travel, not a funding commitment.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only. No article in this category is an offering, and none presents a series as an investment. Any future raise for a series would need its own issuer, its own documents, and counsel review. The governance model is the same across the category: agent-managed day-to-day work within human-set limits, with a human Steward Council deciding, signing, and moving funds. Agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated. Nothing on this page is legal, tax, or financial advice, and no IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files, so no tax-exempt status and no tax-deductible donations are claimed here.
