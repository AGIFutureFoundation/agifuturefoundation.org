The work

Cognition.X is Series 1 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and it is the series whose purpose is the easiest to state from the Foundation’s own published words: learning. As stated on the Foundation site, Cognition.X is connected to Flow Zone education — the idea that learning works best when a person is engaged at the right level of challenge, neither bored nor overwhelmed, and that an agent-assisted environment can help hold a learner in that zone. That sentence is site copy, not a filing and not a product description, but it is the one published anchor this series has, and this article treats it as such: a stated direction, not a delivered system.

The domain the name implies is cognition itself: how people learn, remember, reason, and apply what they know, and how agentic systems might support those processes rather than replace them. In a learning context that could mean tutoring that adapts to a student, practice that adjusts as mastery grows, explanations pitched at the learner’s actual level, or tools that help a teacher see where a class is stuck. None of those tools is verified as built for this series. The honest statement is narrower: the series is planned around learning and understanding, and the specific products, audiences, and methods are still to be defined in writing by the humans who would steward it.

What makes Cognition.X first in the list is worth noting without over-reading it. Series numbers in the April 2026 list are an ordering in an internal printout, not a ranking of priority, funding, or readiness, and no document explains why Cognition.X is Series 1 rather than Series 10 or Series 33. It is reasonable to observe that learning sits upstream of almost everything else the Foundation plans — diagnostics, robotics, city systems, and research all depend on people who understand them — but that is an observation about the domain, not a claim about this series’ status. Cognition.X has no verified team, budget, curriculum, or learner count, and this article does not invent one.

A learning series also carries a particular responsibility that should be named plainly. Education shapes what people believe and can do, so a series that builds learning tools would need to answer questions about accuracy, about whose knowledge it teaches, about how it handles a learner’s mistakes without shaming them, and about what data it keeps on a learner and who can see it. Those are governance questions, not features, and they belong to the human stewards, not to the agents that might one day operate the tools. No such governance document exists for Cognition.X today, which is itself a reason the series is described here as planned rather than operating.

The most useful next step for Cognition.X would not be a bigger claim but a smaller, checkable one: a written statement of scope from a named human steward, saying who the first learners would be and what the first learning tool would do. Until that document exists, the honest size of this series is a name, a direction, and a partial campus — and the rest of this article is built to keep those three things exactly that size.

Metaverse connection

Anchored world: Cognition.X Campus, 45.483127839031816, 18.572453605834877. This is one of the verified anchored worlds in the Foundation’s metaverse work: a Cognition.X Campus fabric with those coordinates. Its fabric JSON is live on GitHub Pages under the WorldForge-Browser my-fabric directory, but the referenced assets behind it — on the order of two hundred GLB model files plus the map.wasm module — are not hosted there, so the campus does not render fully in Artemis yet. That is the verified state: a published fabric definition, an anchored location, and an incomplete render because the assets it points to are not in place.

What that means in practice is that the campus exists as a plan and a partial build, not as a place a visitor can walk through today. The next step, if the campus is to be visitable, is unglamorous and specific: host the referenced GLB assets and map.wasm at the paths the fabric expects, then verify the render in Artemis. Until that is done, any image or description of the campus should be labelled as a concept or a partial build, not as a live world. And the campus is not on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1, no fabric or service for this series has ever been attached there, and the RP1 Attach wizard needs a live metaverse-server endpoint before any attach can happen.

The connection between the campus and the series’ learning purpose is easy to imagine and important not to overstate. A campus in a metaverse could host classes, practice spaces, and demonstrations that a flat web page cannot — that is the reason a learning series would want a world at all. But imagining a use is not the same as having built it, and this article keeps the two apart: the learning purpose is stated site copy, the campus is an anchored but incompletely rendered fabric, and the combination of the two into a working Flow Zone classroom has not been verified.

Second, the published direction: Flow Zone education, as stated on the Foundation site. Third, the anchored metaverse fabric: the Cognition.X Campus at 45.483127839031816, 18.572453605834877, with its fabric JSON live on GitHub Pages and its assets not yet hosted, so it does not render fully in Artemis. That is the complete verified inventory for this series.

What is planned

Everything else is planned, not existing: the formation of W3 LLC and the designation of this series; a human Steward Council and operating rules; learning products or curricula; any learners, teachers, or partner institutions; any funding; and a fully rendering campus on a live metaverse service. Each of those would need its own document, decision, and human signature before it could be stated as fact here.

The DAO model and stewardship

The operating model planned for every W3 series is the same, and it is worth stating in full on each article page, because it is the part of the plan that decides who can actually do anything. In the intended model, Cognition.X would be DAO-managed day to day: agents operate the routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides everything that carries a legal, financial, or doctrinal consequence. Agents prepare; humans execute. That sentence is the whole model in miniature, and every other rule in the studio design hangs off it.

The studio model names eight agent roles for each series: research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting, with a coordinator covering any role left unfilled. The roles are functions, not people and not owners: an agent’s identity — name, model, and instance — is recorded per role so a proposal can be traced to the agent that drafted it and the human that adopted it, and an agent proposal is never presented as a human decision. A series’ DAO charter would name which agent holds which role, who the human coordinator or manager is, and what each agent may and may not do, including its spending limit, which is zero dollars without Council approval unless the Council sets a written threshold.

The human gate is where the model gets its teeth. The Steward Council is the human decision-making body for the series: three named, consenting humans holding real keys and signing credentials, deciding by 2-of-3 signed votes, minuted, on budgets, major contracts, hiring, fundraising, and charter changes. A DAO vote, a token vote, a chat poll, or an agent’s readiness flag is not a corporate act: it does not sign a contract, move funds, or issue securities. Only the council, with signed authority, does that. Agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charter changes.

Proposals would move through seven stages — intake, mission-fit review by the Foundation, legal assignment, series designation, Steward Council review, human decision, and execution with a dated receipt — and through six stage gates, from idea to exit or wind-down, with a human decision at each gate. Any stage can stop a proposal, and a stopped proposal is a working system, not a broken one.

None of this is operating for Cognition.X today. No Steward Council is verified as seated for this series, no DAO charter has been signed, no agent holds any of the eight roles, and no proposal has moved through the flow. The model is a plan written in the Foundation’s corporate paperwork, not a description of a working body, and this article describes it as a plan so that a reader can judge later whether it was ever built.

Code and repositories

The strongest repository coverage of any series in the organisation sits with Cognition.X. The Cognition.X repository holds the Flow Zone dataset and six offline-first apps; the repository says its continuous-integration setup rebuilds the dataset and all six apps on every push, which is the repository's claim, not a verified run. The companion cognitionx-site repository is the landing page. The block counts disagree between repositories: Cognition.X lists 17,450 blocks in 47 packs, while agi-corp-worlds lists 6,750 learning blocks in 25 packs. Neither number is quoted here as settled.

How it fits the mission

Cognition.X sits in the third tier of the Foundation’s intended three-tier architecture. The apex tier is governance: the AGI Future Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation, holding the mission standard — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate for the series below it. The middle tier is the commercial engine: W3 LLC, an intended Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series with isolated assets and liability. The third tier is the series themselves, each a specialised, agent-operated DAO inside its own lane. Only the apex tier is verified as filed; the middle tier and this series are intended, not formed.

Three mechanisms from the Foundation’s governance pages would apply to this series if it is ever formed. The Fiduciary Shield is the proposed protection for a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns: California benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule for benefit-considering decisions, under Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, acquired, or have its purpose amended by the required vote. Open Global Investment is the proposed model that decouples economics from control — broad economic participation through Class A shares with one vote each, a larger voice for screened partners through Class B with ten votes, and decisive control through Class C with one thousand votes, held only by the Foundation — but it belongs to the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation track, not to the filed nonprofit, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. The Windfall Clause is a planned legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; the threshold, share, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today.

Open questions

  1. Who are the first learners Cognition.X would serve, and what would the first learning tool actually do for them?
  2. What does “Flow Zone” mean as a testable standard — how would anyone know whether a learner is in the zone, and what happens when the system gets it wrong?
  3. Whose knowledge does the series teach, and who decides what counts as accurate when sources disagree?
  4. What learner data would the series keep, for how long, and who could see it — and how would a learner opt out or delete it?
  5. How would the series handle a learner’s mistakes without shaming them, and who is accountable when a tool teaches something false?
  6. What would have to be true — which filing, which council, which document — before Cognition.X could claim its first real learner?
