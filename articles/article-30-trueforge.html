trueforge

trueforge is a named network venture on the live Foundation site. Almost nothing is verified beyond the name, and this article says so plainly: there is no filing, no product, no team, no site, no customer, and no timeline verified for it. What follows is a planned focus only, a careful reading of a name that points at making, and a clear statement of what it is not.

TL;DR

trueforge appears by name in the Foundation's network materials, and that is, on the evidence reviewed, the whole of what is verified about it. Its name points, if it points anywhere, at making or forging, building something, and building it truly, and that reading is name-implied only, not a verified description of a product. No thing made, no maker, no customer, and no method is verified. It is intended and planned, not formed, funded, or offered, and it has no anchored metaverse fabric yet.

The work

A suggestive name is not a description, and this article is where that rule does its hardest work. The name points, if it points anywhere, at making, at forging something, and at doing so truly, or for what is true. That reading is name-implied only: it is a guess about intent drawn from a word, not a verified description of a product, and this article does not treat it as one. What a name sounds like is not evidence of what a venture does, and no page on this site treats it as evidence. The reason for the rule is practical, not pedantic: if this site described trueforge by elaborating on its name, a reader would leave believing the venture does the thing the name suggests, and would carry that belief into a conversation with a lawyer, a bank, an investor, or a potential partner. The belief would have been created by this site, out of nothing. A name-only article must therefore do something unusual for a website: it must describe the absence, and let the absence stand.

That does not mean the name is meaningless. A name is a choice, and the choice tells you what the people who wrote it were thinking about: the field they had in mind, the problem they wanted to be associated with, the company they wanted this venture to keep. What it does not tell you is whether the venture was ever built, whether anyone worked on it, whether it has a product, or whether it will ever exist in any form beyond the name. Names are cheap; companies are not. The gap between the two is the distance this article is measuring, and the measurement, at the time of writing, is the whole of the venture: a name, and nothing yet behind it. There is also a second, subtler risk: writing an article so empty that a reader concludes the venture is a fiction, a mistake, or a deception. It is none of those things, on the evidence available. It is a name in planning materials for a network that is itself largely planned rather than formed.

Making, like watching or guarding, is not one thing, and unpacking it shows how much work the name conceals. A maker of software works in code, review, and release; a maker of hardware works in materials, tooling, and testing; a maker of content works in research, drafting, and publication; a maker of physical places works in land, permits, and construction. Each of those has different costs, different risks, different customers, and different legal duties, and no document reviewed for this site says which one trueforge is, or whether it is any of them. The word “true” adds a further question rather than an answer: true to what, a specification, a standard, a verified claim, or a purpose? If the name means anything by that word, only its proposer can say, in writing, and this article does not guess. What can be said is that a venture whose name promises truth in making would carry a heavy burden of proof, and that burden is exactly what this article refuses to lift by invention.

One neighbouring name deserves a direct answer, because it invites confusion. WorldForge is the world-building side of the network, and its name also points at making. A shared idea, making things, is not a relationship, and none is claimed here. trueforge is not presented as part of WorldForge, as a series, or as a subsidiary of anything, because no document reviewed for this site establishes such a connection. The two names sit near each other in the materials, and nearness is not partnership. If a relationship is later documented, the record should be updated to cite the document, the date, and the parties, not merely to assert that a relationship exists. The same discipline applies to the other name-only ventures: sharing a status, or a suggestive word, is not sharing a venture.

The DAO model and stewardship

The planned governance model for trueforge is the model described across the Foundation's governance and operations pages, and it is worth stating in full here because it is the part of the plan that most often gets misread. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans, in the eight roles of the Venture Studio Operating Model (research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting). Those roles describe functions, not people and not owners, and no agent holds any of them for trueforge today.

Above the agents sits the human gate. A human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds: a DAO vote, a token vote, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act, and only a named human acting within signed, written authority can sign a contract, move money, issue securities, or enact a charter. The Council would set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable.

The hard limits bind every agent role equally. Agents NEVER sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. For trueforge, that model is planned, not operating: no Steward Council is verified as seated, no operating agreement is in the files reviewed, and no published set of agent limits exists.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for trueforge: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are verified for it, and this article invents none of them. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one.

Zero worlds are live on RP1, for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to trueforge. A metaverse presence would need four checkable things: published coordinates, a fabric JSON file, hosted assets that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach a reviewer can open and verify. Until all four exist, the correct statement remains no anchored metaverse fabric yet. Whether this item even needs one is an open question: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the work happens in documents, code, and human conversation and needs no world.

How it fits the mission

trueforge fits the intended structure, if it is ever developed, in the commercial tier alongside the series, but that placement is itself a plan, not a current legal fact, because the venture is not formed and the W3 LLC formation is not verified in filings. It has no series number, it is not in the April 2026 internal list of 33 named series, and it is not presented as a cell inside the W3 network. Its fit with the mission is conditional in the strongest sense: a venture that genuinely made things, and made them truly, with verifiable claims, named makers, and accountable humans, could serve the Foundation's purpose of safe, equitable, and socially beneficial artificial intelligence. But making without a named thing made, a named maker, or a named customer makes nothing yet, and the mission gate exists to make sure a maker's name becomes written, accountable work before it is claimed as a result.

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), as governance and mission steward. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC, and below it the 33 named W3 sub-series in the April 2026 internal list. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The middle and bottom tiers are intended, not established, and nothing on this page should be read as saying the Foundation owns, controls, or has formed trueforge.

Three mechanisms hold the intended structure together, and each is a plan with work still to do. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions under California Corporations Code sections 14620 to 14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a board can still be sued and must still act in good faith. Open Global Investment is the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation framework only: Class A, one vote and full economics, open to the global public; Class B, ten votes, for restricted aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules; Class C, 1,000 votes, control-only, reserved to the Foundation. Its suggested first tranche is a SAFE of $500,000 to $1,500,000 with a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only, and a second priced tranche whose price would be set then, not today. The PBC track and the AGI Corp SAFE are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. The Windfall Clause is a pre-commitment, hardwired before profits exist, that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through four Benefit Buckets, Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Its threshold measure, scope, share, and recipient are blank for counsel, and it can be amended only by supermajority plus Class C.

Two cautions belong in the same passage. First, the filed Foundation is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent, with control only through written mission agreements designed with counsel. The for-profit track is separate and proposed, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Second, the Foundation's fundraising as filed is donation and grant only. No suggested figure here is an offering, a price, a term, or a promise of any return, and suggested figures are never added into a family total.
