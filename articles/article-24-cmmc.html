The work

The temptation with a four-letter name is to expand it. Readers familiar with a particular field may have a ready expansion in mind, and it would be easy to write an article about that expansion as if it were the series’ focus. This article does not do that. The April 2026 list gives the letters and nothing else, and no site copy, article, or planning document reviewed here expands them or explains what they stand for in this programme. An expansion supplied by a reader’s background knowledge is not a fact about this series, however familiar it feels, and presenting it as one would be invention. The only honest expansion on this page is none at all: CMMC, as listed, with its meaning to be supplied by whoever proposed it, in writing, with a source this article can cite.

That restraint matters more for an acronym than for an ordinary name, because acronyms collide. The same four letters can stand for different things in different fields, and an article that chose one expansion would mislead every reader who knew another, and would mislead most of all the person who actually proposed the series, if they meant something else entirely. Consider the damage in concrete terms: a reviewer, a collaborator, or a lawyer who reads an invented expansion here would carry it into diligence, into a conversation with a bank, or into a partnership discussion, and the correction, when it finally arrived, would have to undo a belief this site created. A page that says “not stated” can be corrected in a single sentence the moment the real answer arrives. A page that guesses wrong creates a false record that the real project has to fight.

What can be said without guessing is, again, about shape rather than subject. CMMC is a named series in the W3 list, intended to become a liability-isolated series under W3 LLC, DAO-managed day-to-day with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council, under the Foundation’s mission gate. That is the programme-wide intended model. It describes how the series would be governed if it became active; it says nothing about what it would do. The governance model is worth spelling out even for a series with no stated subject, because the model is the same whether the subject turns out to be compliance, manufacturing, health, or something else entirely: agents operate routine work within human-set limits, humans decide, sign, and move funds, and agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine.

The questions that would have to be answered before this article could grow are the same kind as for any name-only series, with one added first step: what do the letters stand for, in this programme? What field or problem would the series serve? Who proposed it, and why? What would its first piece of work be, and who would do it? Until those are answered in writing by a named human, this article should stay as it is, a name, a number, a status, and an open question, rather than growing into a description of a subject nobody has confirmed. That shortness is the article working correctly, not a draft waiting to be padded.

There is one more discipline this article observes that is worth naming, because it generalises. Every series in this group is thin, but CMMC is the thinnest, and the temptation to fill thinness with plausible detail grows as the verified record shrinks. The rule applied here is the opposite: the thinner the record, the stricter the restraint. Where the landing page says “name-implied only”, this article says the same, and where the landing page says nothing, this article says nothing, and spends its words instead on why the silence matters, what would have to change it, and how a reader should treat the name until it does. That is a legitimate use of two thousand words: not to describe a venture that does not exist, but to protect the record of one that might.

The DAO model and stewardship

The planned governance model for CMMC is the model described across the Foundation's governance and operations pages, and it is worth stating in full here because it is the part of the plan that most often gets misread. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans, in the eight roles of the Venture Studio Operating Model (research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting). Those roles describe functions, not people and not owners, and no agent holds any of them for CMMC today.

Above the agents sits the human gate. A human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds: a DAO vote, a token vote, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act, and only a named human acting within signed, written authority can sign a contract, move money, issue securities, or enact a charter. The Council would set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable.

The hard limits bind every agent role equally. Agents NEVER sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. For CMMC, that model is planned, not operating: no Steward Council is verified as seated, no operating agreement is in the files reviewed, and no published set of agent limits exists.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for CMMC: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are verified for it, and this article invents none of them. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one.

Zero worlds are live on RP1, for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to CMMC. A metaverse presence would need four checkable things: published coordinates, a fabric JSON file, hosted assets that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach a reviewer can open and verify. Until all four exist, the correct statement remains no anchored metaverse fabric yet. Whether this item even needs one is an open question: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the work happens in documents, code, and human conversation and needs no world.

  • A name, the four letters “CMMC”, and a series number (S20) in the April 2026 internal W3 series list.
  • The Foundation's filed nonprofit status and its intended W3 governance model, described in this article.
  • No expansion of the letters, no stated focus, no description, no mission, no project, and no person named for this series in the files reviewed.
  • No formed company, no designation, no bank account, no EIN, and no metaverse fabric for this series.

What is planned

  • A written expansion of the letters, and a one-page mission statement, from whoever proposed the series.
  • A named field, problem, or community the series would serve, and a first piece of work.
  • A Steward Council and named human stewards, only once there is something to steward.
  • A metaverse decision, only after the focus is stated: a fabric with coordinates and a verifiable RP1 attach, or a written record that no world is needed.
  • Separate money from the start, if the series ever holds funds: its own bank account and ledger, its own EIN where required, and a written agreement with the Foundation.

No timeline is given for any planned item, because no timeline is verified. Each planned item would need its own document and decision before it could be stated here as fact.

How it fits the mission

CMMC fits the mission, for now, only as a named, numbered, unfilled slot in the intended structure: a place held in the list for an idea whose description has not yet been written down. In tier terms it belongs to the commercial engine tier, the W3 Wyoming series network, under the Foundation's mission gate at the apex, in the same planned shape as every other series in the list. That is a statement about its intended legal and governance shape, not about its subject, and its fit with any other series will be decided when its letters are expanded and its focus is stated. Until then, the most honest fit statement is a procedural one: the series fits the programme as a placeholder, and the programme's discipline is to leave placeholders empty until a human fills them in writing.

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), as governance and mission steward. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC, and below it the 33 named W3 sub-series in the April 2026 internal list. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The middle and bottom tiers are intended, not established, and nothing on this page should be read as saying the Foundation owns, controls, or has formed CMMC.

Three mechanisms hold the intended structure together, and each is a plan with work still to do. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions under California Corporations Code sections 14620 to 14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a board can still be sued and must still act in good faith. Open Global Investment is the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation framework only: Class A, one vote and full economics, open to the global public; Class B, ten votes, for restricted aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules; Class C, 1,000 votes, control-only, reserved to the Foundation. Its suggested first tranche is a SAFE of $500,000 to $1,500,000 with a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only, and a second priced tranche whose price would be set then, not today. The PBC track and the AGI Corp SAFE are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. The Windfall Clause is a pre-commitment, hardwired before profits exist, that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through four Benefit Buckets, Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Its threshold measure, scope, share, and recipient are blank for counsel, and it can be amended only by supermajority plus Class C.

Two cautions belong in the same passage. First, the filed Foundation is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent, with control only through written mission agreements designed with counsel. The for-profit track is separate and proposed, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Second, the Foundation's fundraising as filed is donation and grant only. No suggested figure here is an offering, a price, a term, or a promise of any return, and suggested figures are never added into a family total.
