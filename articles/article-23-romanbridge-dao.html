RomanBridge.DAO

RomanBridge.DAO is W3 Series S15, a planned DAO whose name points to bridging: connection between people, places, systems, or communities that are currently apart. Very little is verified about it beyond its name and its place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and this article keeps that breadth visible rather than narrowing it by invention, while going deeper on what a bridge would have to be to deserve the name.

TL;DR

RomanBridge.DAO is W3 Series S15 in the Foundation's intended W3 network, and its name is the only verified detail about it beyond its number and place in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. Its planned focus, as the name implies, is bridging and connection, governed as a DAO if the series is ever formed. No bridge, network, member, agreement, or transaction exists today under this name. It is intended and planned, not formed, funded, or offered, and it has no anchored metaverse fabric yet.

The work

The name invites a broad reading, and the honest response is to keep that breadth visible rather than to narrow it by invention. A bridge can connect many kinds of distance: between two communities, between a physical place and a virtual one, between two systems that cannot yet talk to each other, or between people who need each other and have no route to meet. A series called RomanBridge.DAO could in principle work on any of those, but no choice among them is recorded, no community, place, system, or counterpart is named in any verified source, and this article does not pick one. The planned focus is therefore stated at the level the name supports: bridging and connection, governed as a DAO, if the series is ever formed. That restraint is not evasiveness. It is the difference between a page that records what is known and a page that invents a venture to fill the space.

The DAO form itself deserves a plain explanation, because the letters in the name are doing real work and should not be treated as decoration. A DAO, a decentralised autonomous organisation, in the Foundation's intended model means agent-managed day-to-day work under human decision authority: agents would operate the routine work of the bridge within limits set in advance by humans, while a human Steward Council decides, signs, and moves funds. The DAO label does not remove human responsibility, does not create a legal entity by itself, and does not authorise any transaction. For RomanBridge.DAO, the DAO form is planned, not operating: there is no member list, no token, no treasury, no vote, and no proposal record for this series today, and none is claimed. Any future DAO mechanism for this series would need its own legal form, its own rules, and counsel's review. The name alone creates none of those things.

The questions a future steward would need to answer are therefore about the bridge itself, before any mechanism is chosen. What exactly would RomanBridge.DAO connect, and for whom? Who is on each side of the bridge, and what do they need from each other that they cannot get today? What would the bridge carry, information, introductions, coordination, resources, and what would it refuse to carry? A bridge that carries everything carries no standard, and a bridge with no standard cannot be trusted by either side. Who would be accountable when the bridge fails, or when something crossing it causes harm? How would membership, if there were members, be decided, and what rights and duties would members have? None of those questions has a verified answer today. Listing them is a record of the work that remains before this planned series could become a real one, not a substitute for doing it.

There is also a question the name itself raises and does not answer: what does “Roman” add? It may point at endurance, at roads and aqueducts, at an empire of connection, or at nothing more than a founder's taste in names. This article does not guess. A name's flavour is not a mission statement, and treating a classical allusion as a description of a product would be exactly the kind of invention this article is written to avoid. If the person who proposed the series ever writes down what the word was meant to signal, that sentence belongs here, cited to its author. Until then the word stands as part of the name and nothing more.

What can be said without guessing is about shape rather than subject. RomanBridge.DAO is a named series in the W3 list, intended to become a liability-isolated series under W3 LLC, DAO-managed day-to-day with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council, under the Foundation's mission gate. That is the programme-wide intended model. It describes how the series would be governed if it became active; it says nothing about what it would connect. The most useful next step for this series would be the smallest one that adds real information: a written statement from a named human steward saying what the bridge would connect, for whom, and what it would carry and refuse to carry. Until that document exists, this article will remain thin, and that thinness is the accurate record.

The DAO model and stewardship

The planned governance model for RomanBridge.DAO is the model described across the Foundation's governance and operations pages, and it is worth stating in full here because it is the part of the plan that most often gets misread. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans, in the eight roles of the Venture Studio Operating Model (research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting). Those roles describe functions, not people and not owners, and no agent holds any of them for RomanBridge.DAO today.

Above the agents sits the human gate. A human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds: a DAO vote, a token vote, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act, and only a named human acting within signed, written authority can sign a contract, move money, issue securities, or enact a charter. The Council would set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable.

The hard limits bind every agent role equally. Agents NEVER sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. For RomanBridge.DAO, that model is planned, not operating: no Steward Council is verified as seated, no operating agreement is in the files reviewed, and no published set of agent limits exists.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for RomanBridge.DAO: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are verified for it, and this article invents none of them. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one.

Zero worlds are live on RP1, for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to RomanBridge.DAO. A metaverse presence would need four checkable things: published coordinates, a fabric JSON file, hosted assets that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach a reviewer can open and verify. Until all four exist, the correct statement remains no anchored metaverse fabric yet. Whether this item even needs one is an open question: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the work happens in documents, code, and human conversation and needs no world.

What exists today vs what is planned

What exists today

  • The series name and number, as listed in the April 2026 internal W3 series list (an internal printout, not a Wyoming filing).
  • The Foundation's own filing record: California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, Entity No. B20260196664, filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07.
  • This article itself: a public, honest description of a planned series, with its status stated up front.
  • No formed series company, no designation document, no bank account, no EIN for this series, no operating agreement, no signed customer or partner agreement, no site, no product, and no money raised that this article can verify.

What is planned

  • A written decision on whether RomanBridge.DAO should be formed at all, and if so, what it would connect, for whom, and what it would carry and refuse to carry.
  • Human governance defined first: who decides, who signs, who moves funds, and who is accountable, before any agent operation, membership, or treasury.
  • Membership, proposal, and decision rules in writing, with counsel, including who can join, propose, and vote, and how a decision can be reversed.
  • A governance form chosen with counsel: series designation, operating agreement, and Steward Council appointments, if formation is approved.
  • A metaverse decision only after the focus is set, under the four-part standard of coordinates, fabric, hosted assets, and a verifiable RP1 attach.

No timeline is given for any planned item, because no timeline is verified. Each planned item would need its own document and decision before it could be stated here as fact.

How it fits the mission

RomanBridge.DAO fits the mission at the level of the idea, not yet at the level of a venture. The Foundation's purpose covers safe, equitable, and socially beneficial artificial intelligence and the global distribution of its benefits, and connection is part of how benefits distribute: people, places, and systems that cannot reach each other cannot share in what is built. A bridge that genuinely connected excluded communities to useful systems would serve that purpose directly. But a bridge with no named sides, no stated cargo, and no accountable steward serves nothing yet, and this article does not pretend otherwise. The fit is potential, and it stays potential until a steward writes it down.

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), as governance and mission steward. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC, and below it the 33 named W3 sub-series in the April 2026 internal list. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The middle and bottom tiers are intended, not established, and nothing on this page should be read as saying the Foundation owns, controls, or has formed RomanBridge.DAO.

Three mechanisms hold the intended structure together, and each is a plan with work still to do. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions under California Corporations Code sections 14620 to 14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a board can still be sued and must still act in good faith. Open Global Investment is the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation framework only: Class A, one vote and full economics, open to the global public; Class B, ten votes, for restricted aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules; Class C, 1,000 votes, control-only, reserved to the Foundation. Its suggested first tranche is a SAFE of $500,000 to $1,500,000 with a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only, and a second priced tranche whose price would be set then, not today. The PBC track and the AGI Corp SAFE are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. The Windfall Clause is a pre-commitment, hardwired before profits exist, that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through four Benefit Buckets, Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Its threshold measure, scope, share, and recipient are blank for counsel, and it can be amended only by supermajority plus Class C.

Two cautions belong in the same passage. First, the filed Foundation is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent, with control only through written mission agreements designed with counsel. The for-profit track is separate and proposed, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Second, the Foundation's fundraising as filed is donation and grant only. No suggested figure here is an offering, a price, a term, or a promise of any return, and suggested figures are never added into a family total.

Open questions

  1. What exactly would RomanBridge.DAO connect, and for whom: which communities, places, or systems sit on each side of the bridge?
  2. What would the bridge carry, and what would it refuse to carry, and who decides that boundary?
  3. Who would be accountable when the bridge fails, or when something crossing it causes harm?
  4. How would membership be decided, what rights and duties would members have, and would there be a token or treasury at all?
  5. What does “Roman” in the name signal, in the proposer's own words, and does it constrain the mission in any way?
  6. Would a metaverse presence help the people this series would serve, and if so, what would it need to contain to be useful rather than decorative?
