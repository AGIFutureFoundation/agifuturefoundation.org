TL;DR

Solar DAO (W3 Series S32) is a planned DAO for solar energy: generation, storage, and the infrastructure that moves power from where it is made to where it is used. The April 2026 internal list names it and gives no further description, so everything on this page is a planned focus drawn from the name, labelled as planned throughout. No asset, site, installation, customer, or power-purchase agreement exists. The first distinction a future steward must make is between governing energy and generating it, and the first proof would be a single working installation, honestly reported — not a portfolio plan.

The work

Energy is the most physical of the W3 lanes after robotics and aerospace, and the planned focus has to be grounded in that physicality: panels, inverters, batteries, grids, permits, and the people who install and maintain them. A DAO in this lane could, in principle, do several different things — operate solar assets, coordinate a network of them, or govern how energy data and energy value are shared — and those are different businesses with different capital needs, different regulations, and different risks. No choice between them has been made, and no asset, site, installation, customer, or power-purchase agreement exists for this series. Any page that described Solar DAO as owning panels or selling power would be inventing a company. This article describes a planned focus and the decisions that would have to precede any asset: which lane, in which place, under which permits, with whose capital, and accountable to whom.

The first distinction to make is between governing energy and generating it, because the two are so often confused. A series that governs energy sets rules, keeps records, and coordinates participants; a series that generates energy owns equipment, hires installers, and sells power. The first can be built with documents and software. The second needs capital, permits, warranties, and a maintenance crew, and it carries the physical risks of a construction business: equipment fails, batteries degrade, roofs leak, sites are sold. A DAO that cannot say which of those it is cannot be diligenced, cannot be insured, and cannot be held to account. The honest sequence is therefore written scope first, choosing between governing, coordinating, and generating; then a regulatory and permitting map for one place, reviewed by counsel; then one small installation, with a named installer, a named site, a metered output, and a published account of cost, output, and anything that went wrong; and only then any discussion of a second site.

Energy also connects this series to the Foundation’s wider purpose more directly than most. The Windfall Clause framework, as planned, directs value above a threshold toward global public goods, and energy infrastructure is named among the destinations in the pitch framework — alongside healthcare access, technological self-determination, and global public goods generally. That is a framework, not a funding commitment: the threshold, the share, and the recipient are all blank for counsel, and no money exists to distribute. But the connection is real as a direction of travel. A solar series that one day helped bring power to places that lack it would be the clearest expression of the Foundation’s mission in physical form.

The practical questions for Solar DAO are grounded and unglamorous: Where would the first installation be, on whose land or roof, under which permit, and with whose consent? Who installs and maintains it, under what safety standard, and with what warranty? How is the power metered, who buys it, and at what price, under what agreement? What happens when equipment fails, when a battery degrades, or when a site is sold? And how is the community that hosts the installation involved in the decision, rather than merely informed of it afterwards? A DAO that cannot answer those questions is not an energy company; it is a name. None of these questions has an answer today, because no site, no partner, and no agreement exists.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Solar DAO would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Solar DAO. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for Solar DAO. No coordinates, no fabric JSON, no hosted 3D assets, and no RP1 attach are claimed for this series, because none is verified.

What would be needed is a short, concrete list. First, an anchor: a named place with published coordinates that the series has actually chosen. Second, a fabric: a fabric JSON file describing that world, published where it can be checked. Third, hosted assets: the 3D models and supporting files the world needs, actually hosted and reachable, so the world renders instead of failing to load. Fourth, an RP1 attach: a verified step that brings the world into RP1, confirmed by a live check rather than asserted on a page. Zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series in the network.

That standard is stated here because the Foundation’s metaverse work elsewhere shows how easy it is to overclaim: a fabric file can be live on a public host while the assets it points to are not. If the series later gains an anchor, this section should be updated with the coordinates, the fabric link, the asset hosting, and the date and result of the live render check.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Solar DAO is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. The closest planned relationships are with Aerospace DAO (Series 31) and Robotics DAO (Series 30), the neighbouring physical-infrastructure series, and SmartCiti.X (Series 10), whose proposed plans include physical sites and manufacturing. No agreements, sites, or shared systems exist between Solar DAO and any of them today. Energy infrastructure is also named as a destination in the Windfall Clause framework — a direction of travel, not a funding commitment. See Articles 33 and 34.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.

Open questions

These are genuine unanswered questions, not rhetorical ones. Each needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series can pass its first stage gate.

  1. Which lane is in scope — generating, coordinating, or governing energy — and in which place, under which permits?
  2. Who installs, maintains, and warrants the first installation, and how is its output metered and reported?
  3. How is the host community involved in the decision, and what agreement governs the power, the price, and the land or roof?
  4. What happens when equipment fails, a battery degrades, or a site is sold — who is responsible, and under what warranty or agreement?
  5. What capital would the first installation need, from whom, and under what terms — and what would the series refuse to promise about returns?
  6. What would count as validation before any second site: a metered output, a published cost account, or a community agreement — and who certifies it?
