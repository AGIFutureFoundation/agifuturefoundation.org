The work

Divine DAO is Series 7 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. That sentence, together with the name itself, is very nearly everything verified that can be said about this series. Its name implies a decentralised organisation concerned with the divine — with meaning, faith, community, and the sacred — and this article describes that as a planned focus, because no doctrine, membership, community, practice, partner, publication, or programme for Divine DAO is verified in the files. This is a thin article on purpose: where there is almost nothing verified to say, the honest article says so plainly instead of filling the space with invention.

The domain the name implies is worth sketching carefully, and it needs more care than most, because it touches questions that no agent and no landing page can settle. A DAO — a decentralised autonomous organisation, governed in part by its members through shared rules — concerned with the divine could mean many things: a community that gathers around shared spiritual practice, a forum for discussing questions of meaning and value, a steward of texts or traditions, or a way of coordinating charitable or communal work.

What can be said without inventing anything is what such a series would have to get right if it were built, and the first item on that list is a boundary. Questions of doctrine, belief, and spiritual authority belong to humans — to a community and its recognised leaders — not to agents, not to a vote weighted by tokens, and not to a landing page. The Foundation’s own materials make this boundary explicit for the neighbouring AGI Fellowship church: agents operate day-to-day work under human board and minister direction, and agents do not enact doctrine, ordain, sign, or move funds. The same boundary would have to apply here, in writing, before Divine DAO did anything substantive: who decides matters of belief and practice, how those decisions are made, and what agents may and may not do in that space. No such document exists for Divine DAO today, and no scripture is quoted or invented on this page.

There is one distinction that must be stated plainly, because a reader could easily miss it. Divine DAO is not the AGI Fellowship. The Fellowship is a separate, filed California nonprofit religious corporation (Entity No. B20260229217, Articles filed 2026-05-13, EIN assigned 2026-05-18), with its own site at agifellowship.church, and it is partnered with — not a subsidiary of, not a series of — the Foundation’s other work. Divine DAO, by contrast, is a name in the W3 internal list, with no filing verified. The two should not be confused, and this article does not present Divine DAO as a church, a fellowship, or a place of worship. If Divine DAO ever becomes a community with a stated faith or practice, that statement will need to come from its human members in writing, not from this article.

Before Divine DAO could do substantive work, its human stewards would need to settle, in writing, at least these questions: what exactly is in scope and what is out of scope; who the stewards are and how they are replaced; what agents may and may not do without a human signature; how money, if any ever exists, is held, accounted for, and reported; and how the series relates to the Foundation’s public-benefit purpose. None of those answers exists in a verified document today. A named series is not a formed series, and for this series the human decisions about belief and practice would need to come first, before any technical build.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Divine DAO would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Divine DAO. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for Divine DAO. No coordinates, no fabric JSON, no hosted 3D assets, and no RP1 attach are claimed for this series, because none is verified.

What would be needed is a short, concrete list. First, an anchor: a named place with published coordinates that the series has actually chosen. Second, a fabric: a fabric JSON file describing that world, published where it can be checked. Third, hosted assets: the 3D models and supporting files the world needs, actually hosted and reachable, so the world renders instead of failing to load. Fourth, an RP1 attach: a verified step that brings the world into RP1, confirmed by a live check rather than asserted on a page. Zero worlds are live on RP1 today, for any series in the network.

That standard is stated here because the Foundation’s metaverse work elsewhere shows how easy it is to overclaim: a fabric file can be live on a public host while the assets it points to are not. If the series later gains an anchor, this section should be updated with the coordinates, the fabric link, the asset hosting, and the date and result of the live render check.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Divine DAO is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Open questions

  2. Who decides matters of belief and practice, how are those decisions made, and what may agents never do in that space?
  3. Who are the named human stewards, and how are they chosen and replaced?
  4. What is in scope and what is explicitly out of scope for this series?
  5. How would the series relate to the AGI Fellowship without being confused with it, and what written agreement would govern that relationship?
  6. If money ever exists for this series, how is it held, accounted for, and reported — and who signs for it?
