SmartCiti.X: Cities as Classrooms, Workshops, and Factories

SmartCiti.X is the most developed plan in the W3 network: a proposed new corporation for robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, and robotics manufacturing, with first sites in Louisiana, Nebraska, and Indiana. It is still a plan — nothing about it is formed, funded, or offered yet.

SmartCiti.X series page (S10)

Status — please read first: Named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. W3 LLC formation and per-series designation are not verified in filings — this series is intended/planned, not formed, funded, or offered.

TL;DR

SmartCiti.X (W3 Series S10) is the planned Trade Craft Academy: robotics training, union trades taught with AR/VR, and robotics manufacturing, with a first launch in Louisiana and later work in Nebraska and Indiana. The plan is for a proposed new corporation — Delaware recommended, Wyoming as an alternative, and public benefit corporation versus standard corporation is counsel’s choice — not for a formed W3 series. The suggested figures are $25 million pre-money and a $10 million tranched raise, both illustrative only. No site, city, address, price, seller, union, or customer is signed, no money has been raised, and the anchored Holodeck at 39.846, -86.622 does not render fully yet.

The work

The plan, as stated on the series page, is for a proposed new corporation, and that distinction is the most important fact in this article. SmartCiti.X would not be raised as a W3 series, and its issuer would not be the filed nonprofit Foundation. The corporation would have its own charter, bylaws, EIN, bank account, and cap table, with name and trademark clearance first. Delaware is the recommended form, Wyoming the alternative; public benefit corporation versus standard corporation is one choice for counsel — one form only. The governance and windfall design planned for the corporation can only live inside a real charter. None of it is in force today, because the corporation does not yet exist.

The plan is unusually well documented for a series that does not yet exist as a corporation. The implementation bundle lives in the AGIFutureFoundation GitHub org as SmartCiti.X, version 3.2, with a 404-line README that publishes its own limits openly: nothing in the bundle certifies anybody, synthetic data is labelled as synthetic, and a union named in its 111-hall roster is not a partner or affiliate — the README states that 0 of the 111 halls have signed off by a named practitioner. The bundle self-tests 9,267 checks, carries an ACP protocol suite, a 3,663-skill graph, and 11 simulators. Its ten campuses are authored scenes and registries inside the bundle, not physical campuses, and this article does not present them as buildings. That honesty-first engineering is itself part of the plan’s credibility: a bundle that states what it does not do can be trusted about what it does.

Louisiana is the first launch; Nebraska and Indiana follow, and the phasing is explicit about what the first money does and does not buy. The first sites sit inside a $3 million envelope, covering the first Louisiana and Indiana sites only, not an unlimited portfolio. A Louisiana launch and further purchases belong to Tranche 2. Nebraska and Indiana expansion beyond the first sites belongs to a Series A or later funding, not to the $10 million. No site, city, address, price, or seller is named, because none is signed; any property mentioned is a watchlist candidate only unless a signed agreement says otherwise.

The money figures are suggested and illustrative only, never a price; they are stated here because the structure is the plan. The suggested pre-money valuation is $25 million and the suggested raise is $10 million, tranched $5 million plus $5 million on milestone close. Tranche 1 would be one instrument only, and the Tranche 2 price must be stated in the Tranche 1 documents. The intended use is $4 million for manufacturing, $3 million for the first Louisiana and Indiana sites, $2 million for AR/VR training, and $1 million for working capital and legal costs. Tranche 2 would close only on board certification of all its gates: signed union, AR/VR pilot delivered, first site closed or leased, first manufacturing order or pilot customer, Tranche 1 reconciled, and Windfall report drafted. No money has been raised, no SAFE issued, no terms agreed, and no return promised. Investors in any future corporation would own shares, not units or parcels of land.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, SmartCiti.X would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for SmartCiti.X. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

How it fits the mission

Tier. SmartCiti.X is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. Numbering proximity to Series S09 and S11 is the only relationship claimed. SmartCiti.X is described in the Foundation’s planning as the first studio raise, with Foundation-only Class C control shares planned. No operational partnership, shared customer, shared product, or shared funding with any other series is claimed. The BuilderSpace tour is a separate project, not a SmartCiti.X site, venue, sponsor, or commitment. Metropolis-SmartCiti is related by name only; see Article 36.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.

Open questions

These are genuine unanswered questions, not rhetorical ones. Each needs a written answer, approved by a named human, before the series can pass its first stage gate.

  1. Which union, or which unions, would the Tranche 2 gate require, and what would a signed agreement with them actually cover — curriculum, wages, placement, or all three?
  2. What would the AR/VR pilot have to demonstrate to count as delivered: a number of trainees, a measured skill outcome, a curriculum, or a working headset deployment?
  3. What is the first manufacturing product, and who is the first pilot customer whose order would satisfy the Tranche 2 gate?
  4. Who forms the corporation, who sits on its first board, and who holds the Foundation-only Class C shares — and how is that control exercised in writing?
  5. What would cause the plan to stop rather than continue — a failed pilot, no signed union, no site at a workable price — and who would make that call?
