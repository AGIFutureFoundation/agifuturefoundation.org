The work

It would be easy, and wrong, to guess. A reader might associate the name “Moscone” with a person, a place, or a building, and then write a page about whichever association came to mind first. This article does none of that. No association is stated in the list, no expansion or explanation of the name is given there, and no site copy, article, or planning document reviewed here explains what “Moscone” means for this series. To pick one association and present it as the series’ focus would be to invent a fact and then build a page on it. The honest page is the short one: a name, a number, a status, and a list of the questions that would have to be answered before anything more could be said.

What can be said, without guessing, is what the “.X” suffix and the series number imply about the shape of the thing, as opposed to its subject. The “.X” series in the W3 list are the programme’s named ventures or projects, each intended to become a liability-isolated series under W3 LLC, DAO-managed day-to-day with humans deciding, signing, and moving funds through a Steward Council. That shape is planned for the whole programme. It says nothing about what Moscone.X would do — only how it would be governed if it ever did anything. The distinction between shape and subject is the whole of what can honestly be written about this series today, and it is worth holding on to, because it is what keeps this article from drifting into invention.

The questions that would have to be answered before this article could grow are simple to list and impossible to answer from the files reviewed: What does “Moscone” refer to? What field, problem, or community would the series serve? Who proposed it, and why? What would its first piece of work be, and who would do it? Is there an existing project, place, or partnership behind the name, or is the name a placeholder for an idea not yet written down? Each of those questions has a named human who could answer it. If you hold the answer — a mission statement, a description, a link to an existing project, or even a one-sentence explanation of the name — the contact section at the foot of this article is the route for it, and this article can be updated to cite it, with its source and date.

That restraint is not a failure of the article; it is the article doing its job. A series article that invented a focus for Moscone.X would mislead everyone who read it. A page that says “not stated” costs nothing and can be corrected in a single sentence the moment a real answer arrives. That is why this article is long on method and short on subject: the method — how a name becomes a series, what a Steward Council is, what the mission gate requires — is the part that is actually verified, and it is the part a reader can use while the subject is still missing.

The DAO model and stewardship

Under the Foundation’s intended venture-studio model, Moscone.X would be DAO-managed in its day-to-day operation: agents would operate the routine work of the series within limits set in advance by humans, and a human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds. Agents prepare proposals, analysis, drafts, and coordination; agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charters. Humans execute every material decision.

The Steward Council is the human gate in that design: it would hold the decision authority, set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable for the result. The model has two modes: agent-managed, where agents run the daily work while a human council reviews, and agent-assisted, where a human does the work with agents as co-workers.

That governance model is itself planned, not yet operating for Moscone.X. No Steward Council for any series is verified as seated, so no council roster, operating agreement, or published agent limits for this series exist today. The Foundation’s role is as mission steward, under written mission agreements, with its board remaining independent: a California nonprofit cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. No agent message, draft, or page like this one commits the series or the Foundation; only a named human acting within signed, written authority can.

How it fits the mission

Tier. Moscone.X is intended to sit in the commercial tier of the Foundation’s planned three-tier structure: the Foundation as the governance and mission tier; the W3 LLC series network as the commercial engine; and each named series as one specialised cell inside that network. That tiering is a plan, not a current legal fact, because the W3 LLC formation and this series’ designation are not verified in filings. The Foundation is the only filed entity in that chain, and it is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation — not a for-profit parent, not an owner of a formed series, and not a guarantor of any series’ future.

The Fiduciary Shield. The Shield answers a familiar incentive: in a traditional company, a decision that sacrifices near-term profit for safety invites a shareholder lawsuit. California public-benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule so a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns is not left exposed for having done so, in good faith and under California Corporations Code §§14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under §14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, still be acquired, and still have its benefit purpose amended by the required vote.

Open Global Investment and the Windfall Clause. The Open Global Investment model, as planned, separates economics from governance: broad ownership open to the world, with governance held apart from whoever paid the most — Class A with one vote and full economics for the global public, Class B with ten votes for restricted, screened, aligned partners and sovereigns, and Class C with 1,000 votes, Foundation-only and control-only. The Windfall Clause is planned as a legal pre-commitment hardwired before profits, obligating all 33 W3 series, with its threshold expressed as a percentage of global GDP and its measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel to set. Above the threshold, value would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; below it, standard returns and research and development. None of this is in force today for this series.

Related series. Because no focus is stated, no related series can honestly be named for Moscone.X. To list neighbours would be to guess at a relationship on the basis of a name that has not been explained. In tier terms, the series belongs to the commercial engine tier — the W3 Wyoming series network — under the Foundation’s mission gate at the apex. That is a statement about its intended legal and governance shape, not about its subject. See Articles 41 and 39.

The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only — no equity, no SAFE, no profit share — and no page here presents a series as an investment.
