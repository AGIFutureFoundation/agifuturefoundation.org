The work

Empathic.X is Series 2 in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. Its name points at empathy: the capacity to recognise, understand, and respond appropriately to what another person is feeling, and to build systems that support that capacity rather than simulate it crudely or exploit it. That is the domain the name implies, and it is stated here as a planned focus, because beyond the name and the series number there is almost nothing verified to say about this series. No product, no research programme, no partner, no user group, and no published description beyond the name itself has been verified in the files.

The distinction between supporting empathy and pretending to feel is the central question this series would have to answer, and it is worth stating before any building begins. A system can help a person notice another’s distress, can suggest a kinder phrasing, can slow a conversation down, or can flag that someone may need human care — all without claiming to feel anything itself. A system that claimed to empathise, that used emotional signals to manipulate, or that substituted a machine response for human care where a human was needed would be a different and more troubling thing. Which side of that line Empathic.X intends to stand on has not been written down anywhere verified, so this article does not assign it a position. It names the question because any honest account of an empathy series must.

The neighbouring domains help to sketch, without inventing, where such a series might matter. Learning (Cognition.X, Series 1) depends on trust and on a teacher noticing when a student is lost or distressed. Assessment and care (Diagnostic.X, Series 3) depend on listening well and on not reducing a person to a score. Community and meaning (Divine DAO, Series 7) depend on how people treat one another. Empathic.X, if it is built, would sit across those neighbours rather than inside any one of them. That is a statement about the shape of the domain, not a claim that any collaboration exists: no joint work with any neighbouring series is verified.

There is also a privacy question that belongs on this article from the start. Anything that works with emotional signals — words, tone, expression, or behaviour — works with some of the most personal information a person can produce. A series in this domain would need, before it collected anything, a written answer to what it collects, why, how long it keeps it, who can see it, and how a person opts out or deletes it. No such answer exists for Empathic.X today. The absence of a data policy is not a detail to be filled in later; for this domain, it is the first thing that would need to exist, and its absence is part of why this article describes the series as thin and planned rather than operating.

The thinness of this article is deliberate. A series about empathy that filled its own page with invented users, invented outcomes, or invented testimonials would be failing its own subject before it began. The honest size of Empathic.X today is a name, a number, and a partial world — and the most useful next step would be a written statement of scope from a named human steward, including a data policy and a clear line between supporting human empathy and simulating it.

Metaverse connection

Anchored world: Empathic.X, 47.43990000744796, −179.34952851914045. This is one of the verified anchored worlds in the Foundation’s metaverse work: an Empathic.X fabric with those coordinates. Its fabric JSON is live on GitHub Pages under the WorldForge-Browser my-fabric directory, but the referenced assets behind it — on the order of two hundred GLB model files plus the map.wasm module — are not hosted there, so the world does not render fully in Artemis yet. That is the verified state: a published fabric definition, an anchored location, and an incomplete render because the assets it points to are not in place.

What that means in practice is that the world exists as a plan and a partial build, not as a place a visitor can enter today. The next step, if it is to be visitable, is specific: host the referenced GLB assets and map.wasm at the paths the fabric expects, then verify the render in Artemis. Until that is done, any image or description of the world should be labelled as a concept or a partial build, not as a live world. And the world is not on RP1: zero worlds are live on RP1, no fabric or service for this series has ever been attached there, and the RP1 Attach wizard needs a live metaverse-server endpoint before any attach can happen.

For an empathy-focused series, a metaverse world raises the same question the series itself raises, in a more concrete form: what would people do there that helps them understand one another better, and what would be off limits? A shared space could host guided conversation, perspective-taking exercises, or simply a calmer place to meet — or it could become a stage for performance and surveillance. No design for such a space is verified, so this article claims none. The anchored coordinates are a location for a future build, not evidence of one.

What is planned

Everything else is planned, not existing: the formation of W3 LLC and the designation of this series; a human Steward Council and operating rules; any empathy-related product, research, or service; any data policy for emotional signals; any funding; and a fully rendering world on a live metaverse service. Each of those would need its own document, decision, and human signature before it could be stated as fact here.

The DAO model and stewardship

The operating model planned for every W3 series is the same, and it is worth stating in full on each article page, because it is the part of the plan that decides who can actually do anything. In the intended model, Empathic.X would be DAO-managed day to day: agents operate the routine work within limits set by humans, and a human Steward Council decides everything that carries a legal, financial, or doctrinal consequence. Agents prepare; humans execute. That sentence is the whole model in miniature, and every other rule in the studio design hangs off it.

The studio model names eight agent roles for each series: research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting, with a coordinator covering any role left unfilled. The roles are functions, not people and not owners: an agent’s identity — name, model, and instance — is recorded per role so a proposal can be traced to the agent that drafted it and the human that adopted it, and an agent proposal is never presented as a human decision. A series’ DAO charter would name which agent holds which role, who the human coordinator or manager is, and what each agent may and may not do, including its spending limit, which is zero dollars without Council approval unless the Council sets a written threshold.

The human gate is where the model gets its teeth. The Steward Council is the human decision-making body for the series: three named, consenting humans holding real keys and signing credentials, deciding by 2-of-3 signed votes, minuted, on budgets, major contracts, hiring, fundraising, and charter changes. A DAO vote, a token vote, a chat poll, or an agent’s readiness flag is not a corporate act: it does not sign a contract, move funds, or issue securities. Only the council, with signed authority, does that. Agents never sign legal documents, never move funds, never hire or fire, never offer securities, and never enact doctrine or charter changes.

Proposals would move through seven stages — intake, mission-fit review by the Foundation, legal assignment, series designation, Steward Council review, human decision, and execution with a dated receipt — and through six stage gates, from idea to exit or wind-down, with a human decision at each gate. Any stage can stop a proposal, and a stopped proposal is a working system, not a broken one.

None of this is operating for Empathic.X today. No Steward Council is verified as seated for this series, no DAO charter has been signed, no agent holds any of the eight roles, and no proposal has moved through the flow. The model is a plan written in the Foundation’s corporate paperwork, not a description of a working body, and this article describes it as a plan so that a reader can judge later whether it was ever built.

How it fits the mission

Empathic.X sits in the third tier of the Foundation’s intended three-tier architecture. The apex tier is governance: the AGI Future Foundation itself, a filed California nonprofit public benefit corporation, holding the mission standard — safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI — and acting as mission gate for the series below it. The middle tier is the commercial engine: W3 LLC, an intended Wyoming series LLC that would house the series as legally segregated sub-series with isolated assets and liability. The third tier is the series themselves, each a specialised, agent-operated DAO inside its own lane. Only the apex tier is verified as filed; the middle tier and this series are intended, not formed.

Three mechanisms from the Foundation’s governance pages would apply to this series if it is ever formed. The Fiduciary Shield is the proposed protection for a board that weighs safety and public benefit alongside returns: California benefit-corporation status expands the business-judgment rule for benefit-considering decisions, under Corporations Code sections 14620–14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a benefit corporation can still be sued, acquired, or have its purpose amended by the required vote. Open Global Investment is the proposed model that decouples economics from control — broad economic participation through Class A shares with one vote each, a larger voice for screened partners through Class B with ten votes, and decisive control through Class C with one thousand votes, held only by the Foundation — but it belongs to the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation track, not to the filed nonprofit, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. The Windfall Clause is a planned legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, under which returns above a threshold — to be set by counsel, expressed in the pitch framework as a percentage of global GDP — would flow to humanity through global public goods, healthcare access, energy infrastructure, and technological self-determination; the threshold, share, recipient, and enforceability are all blank for counsel today.

Open questions

  1. Where does Empathic.X draw the line between supporting human empathy and simulating feeling — and who writes that line down?
  2. What emotional signals, if any, would the series collect, and what would its written data policy say about retention, access, and deletion?
  3. When should a system step back and hand a person to a human — and how would that handoff be guaranteed rather than left to chance?
  4. How would the series measure whether it is helping people understand one another, without reducing empathy to a score?
  5. Who would be accountable when an empathy tool misreads a person or nudges a conversation in a harmful direction?
  6. What would have to be true — which filing, which council, which document — before Empathic.X could claim its first real user?
