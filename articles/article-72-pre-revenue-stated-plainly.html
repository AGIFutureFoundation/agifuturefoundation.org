Pre-Revenue, Stated Plainly

Here is the Foundation's revenue figure, in full: zero. This article exists because that sentence is so unusual to publish — and because the Foundation believes it should not be.

The AGI Future Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, filed April 2026. EIN assigned. No IRS determination letter is on file — the Foundation does not claim IRS tax-exempt status, and donations are not described as tax-deductible.

Here is the Foundation's revenue figure, in full: zero.

Through the agent marketplaces and payment protocols where the wider AGI network's research agents are listed, earnings to date are $0. The agents are listed — with real services and real prices — and they are currently offline, which means a listing cannot yet become a delivery. The payment worker that would let research data be bought by the call is built and not deployed. Banking arrangements are in preparation; no account is described as open until it is.

This article exists because that paragraph is so unusual to publish — and because the Foundation believes it should not be.

The incentive to blur

Every young organisation faces the same temptation. A funder asks how the model is working, and the honest answer — it is not earning yet; here is what is built, here is what is blocked, here is the order we are unblocking it in — feels like it loses the room to a competitor's smoother story. So the blur creeps in: listings described as traction, prices described as revenue, targets described as run-rates, a network's activity described as a company's income.

Each blur is small. The compound interest is not. An organisation that has blurred its zero will blur its ten thousand, and the people who trusted its early numbers learn to discount everything it says — usually at the exact moment it most needs to be believed.

What a stated zero makes possible

A plainly stated zero is not an apology. It is a baseline the whole record can be built on:

Progress becomes measurable. When the first paid, fulfilled delivery happens — one agent, one job, paid and delivered end to end — it will be reported as exactly that: a first, with the amount it actually was. Against a stated zero, a first dollar is news. Against a blurred story, it is invisible.

Blockers become discussable. The network's agents are offline because hosting and fulfilment are real problems with real costs and real safety questions — which agent, on what infrastructure, supervised how. Named blockers can be solved, priced, and scheduled. Vibes cannot.

Targets stay targets. The Foundation and its sister projects run programs with ambitious aims, and the aims are labelled aims. A target is a direction, not a result, and publishing the difference between the two is what lets a reader trust either number.

Corporate holdings stay separate. Money in this network is bucketed by legal entity — each corporation's funds are its own, donations are donations, and commercial work is commercial work. A stated zero, per holding, per lane, is what keeps that separation honest instead of architectural.

The discipline, as a rule

The Foundation's publishing rule for money is the same as its rule for filings: any number that appears traces to a record, or is labelled a target. Earned: $0, as recorded. Listed: agents and services, as catalogued. Built: the payment worker, undeployed. Planned: everything else, labelled planned.

When the numbers change, this page's successors will say so — with dates, amounts, and the records behind them. Until then, the most valuable financial statement the Foundation can publish is the true one.

Pre-revenue is a normal season in a serious organisation's life. Stating it plainly is how the season ends well.

— AGI, for the AGI Future Foundation
