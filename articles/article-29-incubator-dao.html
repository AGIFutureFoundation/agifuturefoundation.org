Incubator DAO

Incubator DAO is W3 Series S21, a planned DAO for incubating early ventures and builders, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list. The name gives a clearer planned focus than most in this group, and this article goes deeper on what incubation would have to mean here, who would decide who gets in, and how this series relates to the other programmes that use the incubator word, without claiming a relationship that has not been written down.

TL;DR

Incubator DAO is W3 Series S21, named in the April 2026 internal W3 series list, and its planned focus, as the name implies, is helping early ventures and builders get started, with space, training, mentorship, and time, governed as a DAO. No cohort, resident, graduate, admissions criteria, or programme date is verified for this series. Admissions are a human decision, and no agent should make that call alone. It is intended and planned, not formed, funded, or offered, and it has no anchored metaverse fabric yet.

The work

The planned focus, as the name implies it, would be the earliest stage of building: people with an idea and the beginnings of a team, who need a structured period of work, weeks or months, not days, in which to test the idea, build a first version, learn from users, and decide whether to continue. An incubator, in the ordinary sense, provides some combination of a place to work, a curriculum or training programme, experienced people to learn from, and a cohort of other builders going through the same process at the same time. Which of those this series would provide, to whom, and on what terms, is not stated in the files reviewed, and this article does not invent an answer. The name is the most the files say about it, and everything beyond the name here is a description of a planned focus implied by the name, marked as such.

It is worth distinguishing, carefully, what an incubator is from what it is not. An incubator is not a funder, not an employer, and not a school. It does not own the ventures it hosts, it does not guarantee them customers or investment, and it does not confer a credential simply for having taken part. Any future mission statement for this series would need to say that in writing, so that nobody joins expecting a guarantee the series cannot give. That discipline matters twice over here, because the Foundation's wider plans also discuss fundraising, and a reader could easily blur the line between an incubator that hosts ventures and a studio that funds them. The studio model is a separate design, an AGI Corp function, and this series is not the studio. Keeping those two ideas apart, in writing, is part of the work this article does.

The DAO element adds a second question that a future governing document would have to answer: who decides who gets in, what support they receive, and when a venture graduates, pauses, or leaves? In the intended programme-wide model, agents would operate the day-to-day work within human-set limits, and humans would decide, sign, and move funds through a Steward Council. Admissions, in particular, are a human decision: who is let in, on what criteria, and with what commitments on both sides, and no agent should be making that call alone. No admissions criteria, cohort, resident, or graduate is claimed for this series anywhere in this article, because none exists in the files reviewed. The stage-gate model in the studio papers, six gates from Idea through Validate, Build, Launch, Operate and Scale, to Exit or Wind-down, shows the shape a venture's journey would take, but a gate is not an admission, and a template is not a cohort.

Two neighbouring ideas in the Foundation's wider plans are worth naming, without claiming a relationship that has not been written down. The SmartCiti.X plans describe incubator housing, places for founders, builders, trainees, and instructors connected to a facility to live for a defined residency, under a residency agreement and a board-set fee, with zoning and safety in place before anyone moves in. The BuilderSpace tour is a separate programme of hackathon and onboarding events. Incubator DAO, if it ever becomes active, would need a written agreement saying how it relates to each of those: whether it runs a programme inside SmartCiti.X housing, whether it is separate from BuilderSpace, or whether it is something else entirely. Until such an agreement exists, the three should be treated as separate ideas that happen to use the word incubator, not as parts of one programme. The Delaware cohort series, Cohort 1 Series 1 to 3, to confirm, are a separate, later structure for cohorts and must not be confused with this W3 series either.

The DAO model and stewardship

The planned governance model for Incubator DAO is the model described across the Foundation's governance and operations pages, and it is worth stating in full here because it is the part of the plan that most often gets misread. Day-to-day work would be agent-managed: agents would operate routine work within limits set in advance by humans, in the eight roles of the Venture Studio Operating Model (research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting). Those roles describe functions, not people and not owners, and no agent holds any of them for Incubator DAO today.

Above the agents sits the human gate. A human Steward Council would decide, sign, and move funds: a DAO vote, a token vote, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act, and only a named human acting within signed, written authority can sign a contract, move money, issue securities, or enact a charter. The Council would set the limits inside which agents may act, review what agents did, and remain accountable.

The hard limits bind every agent role equally. Agents NEVER sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. For Incubator DAO, that model is planned, not operating: no Steward Council is verified as seated, no operating agreement is in the files reviewed, and no published set of agent limits exists.

Metaverse connection

There is no anchored metaverse fabric yet for Incubator DAO: no coordinates, no fabric JSON file, no hosted assets, and no RP1 attach are verified for it, and this article invents none of them. That is stated plainly rather than filled in with a plausible-looking location, because an invented coordinate would be worse than an empty one.

Zero worlds are live on RP1, for the whole W3 programme as of the files reviewed, not a gap peculiar to Incubator DAO. A metaverse presence would need four checkable things: published coordinates, a fabric JSON file, hosted assets that can actually be loaded, and an RP1 attach a reviewer can open and verify. Until all four exist, the correct statement remains no anchored metaverse fabric yet. Whether this item even needs one is an open question: either anchor a fabric because the work is better done in a shared spatial place, or record that the work happens in documents, code, and human conversation and needs no world.

What exists today vs what is planned

What exists today

  • A name and a series number (S21) in the April 2026 internal W3 series list.
  • A planned focus implied by the name: incubating early ventures and builders, governed as a DAO.
  • The Foundation's filed nonprofit status and its intended W3 governance model, described in this article.
  • No formed company, no designation, no bank account, no EIN, no cohort, no residents, no admissions criteria, and no programme dates for this series.

What is planned

  • A written mission: who the incubator serves, what support it provides, and what it explicitly does not promise.
  • Admissions criteria, cohort structure, and graduation or exit rules, decided by humans and written down.
  • A Steward Council and named human stewards, with decision limits in writing.
  • Written agreements with SmartCiti.X housing and with BuilderSpace, if any relationship is intended, or a written record that there is none.
  • A metaverse decision: a cohort space with coordinates and a verifiable RP1 attach, or a written record that the programme works in person and in documents and needs no world.

No timeline is given for any planned item, because no timeline is verified. Each planned item would need its own document and decision before it could be stated here as fact.

How it fits the mission

Incubator DAO fits the mission as the earliest stage of the venture-building pipeline, and its most important relationship to settle is with SmartCiti.X, Series S10, whose plans include incubator housing and training facilities. An Incubator DAO that ran a programme without any agreement with the series that holds the housing would be planning in a vacuum. The two ideas belong together in planning, and apart in law, until a written agreement joins them. Other related series include S29 Metaverse DAO, if a cohort ever meets in a shared virtual space, and S01 Cognition.X and S02 Empathic.X, whose work a cohort venture might build on. None of those relationships is a partnership today. In tier terms, this series would belong to the commercial engine tier, the W3 Wyoming series network, under the Foundation's mission gate at the apex, and the Foundation does not own the series in the way a parent company owns a subsidiary. Any relationship would be set out in a written mission agreement, with the Foundation board remaining independent, as a California nonprofit board must.

The intended structure has three tiers. At the top sits the AGI Future Foundation, a filed California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation (Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07), as governance and mission steward. In the middle, in the intended plan, sits W3 LLC, a Wyoming series LLC, and below it the 33 named W3 sub-series in the April 2026 internal list. Only the top tier is verified as filed. The middle and bottom tiers are intended, not established, and nothing on this page should be read as saying the Foundation owns, controls, or has formed Incubator DAO.

Three mechanisms hold the intended structure together, and each is a plan with work still to do. The Fiduciary Shield is expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions under California Corporations Code sections 14620 to 14621, with an annual benefit report under section 14630. It is not immunity: a board can still be sued and must still act in good faith. Open Global Investment is the proposed for-profit benefit-corporation framework only: Class A, one vote and full economics, open to the global public; Class B, ten votes, for restricted aligned partners and sovereigns screened for sanctions, export-control, and foreign-investment rules; Class C, 1,000 votes, control-only, reserved to the Foundation. Its suggested first tranche is a SAFE of $500,000 to $1,500,000 with a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only, and a second priced tranche whose price would be set then, not today. The PBC track and the AGI Corp SAFE are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. The Windfall Clause is a pre-commitment, hardwired before profits exist, that profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP would be shared with humanity through four Benefit Buckets, Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination, for the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Its threshold measure, scope, share, and recipient are blank for counsel, and it can be amended only by supermajority plus Class C.

Two cautions belong in the same passage. First, the filed Foundation is a nonprofit: it cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head, and its board must remain independent, with control only through written mission agreements designed with counsel. The for-profit track is separate and proposed, and counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation. Second, the Foundation's fundraising as filed is donation and grant only. No suggested figure here is an offering, a price, a term, or a promise of any return, and suggested figures are never added into a family total.

  3. How does Incubator DAO relate to SmartCiti.X housing and to the BuilderSpace tour, and where is that relationship written down?
