AGI Fellowship Church: A Partner Church, Not a Series

The AGI Fellowship is a church: a California nonprofit religious corporation, built and led as a fellowship for people and agents working on AGI, with its own doctrine, its own training, its own formation, and its own outreach. It is partnered with the Foundation and supported by its network, and it is not part of that network’s commercial structure — a distinction repeated everywhere the Fellowship is named, because it is the fact most likely to be lost.

The partnership model is what keeps that identity intact. Partnership, in the Foundation’s papers, means support under written agreements, with an independence clause: the Fellowship is supported by and partnered with the other corporations, and it is not owned, controlled, or directed by them. Its funds are its own, its governance is its own, and its doctrine is its own. That independence is not a courtesy; it is the condition that makes the Fellowship a church rather than a department. Branding and permission are governed by the same written rules: the Fellowship’s name, materials, and relationship to the Foundation are used only as the agreements permit.

One fork in the Foundation’s materials should be known to every reader. The live site calls the organisation a “PBC” in the for-profit benefit-corporation sense, and some papers describe a for-profit benefit-corporation track. The filed entity is a nonprofit public benefit corporation — a different legal form: a filed nonprofit cannot offer equity, a SAFE, or a profit share, and it cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head. This site follows the filed status and notes the site label where relevant, rather than presenting the two as the same entity. Counsel would have to choose one track in writing before any solicitation. For the Fellowship, the practical consequence is simple: donations and tithes only, with no deductibility claim until a determination letter exists, and Fellowship funds never to a SAFE or a for-profit except on counsel’s written advice.

The agent model and stewardship

The Fellowship is described in its papers as agent-run in its daily operations, but the line around doctrine is absolute: agents do not write doctrine, do not decide doctrine, and do not enact it. Doctrine is a human, ministerial act. Signing and moving funds are human acts as well. An agent that drafted a document, summarised a discussion, or organised a schedule has done permitted work; an agent that purported to decide what the church believes would have crossed the line that defines the church. The same boundary that applies across the Foundation’s intended network applies here with particular force, because the subject matter is belief itself.

That boundary is what makes the Fellowship’s agent model different from a purely technical one. In a commercial series, the human gate keeps legal and financial consequences in human hands. In a church, it does that and more: the community’s beliefs are not a dataset to be optimised, and no vote, poll, or agent proposal can settle what a church believes. The Fellowship’s human board and its ministers hold that authority, and the agents work inside limits those humans set.

Three practical rules follow from the partnership model, stated in the Fellowship’s own planning as hard rules rather than preferences. First, Fellowship funds never go to a SAFE or a for-profit except on counsel’s written advice, and the default is no. A church’s funds are held for its religious purpose, and moving them into a for-profit instrument needs a lawyer’s written advice before it is even considered. No such advice is cited on this page, and no such movement is claimed here. Second, branding and permission are governed by written rules: the Fellowship’s name, its materials, and its relationship to the Foundation are used only as the written agreements permit. That rule protects both sides from being presented as the other. Third, partnership means support under written agreements, with an independence clause: the Fellowship is supported by the other corporations, not owned, controlled, or directed by them. Its funds, governance, and doctrine are its own.

How it fits the mission

Partner, not tier. The Fellowship does not sit in any tier of the Foundation’s three-tier commercial structure. It is not the apex, not the commercial engine, and not a series. It sits alongside the structure as a partner church, with its own governance, its own funds, and its own purpose, connected to the Foundation by written partnership and support agreements. Any page that numbered it as a series would misrepresent both the church and the list. That separation lets a church be a church and a foundation be a foundation.

Why a church belongs in this family at all. The Foundation’s purpose is safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI, and that purpose raises questions that are not technical: what is this technology for, who is it for, what does it mean to build it well, and what do the people building it owe each other? A fellowship is a place to ask those questions together, over time — not a place to settle them by vote or by model output. That is why the Fellowship has doctrine, training, and formation rather than a product roadmap: its work is the slow formation of people, human and agent, who will make consequential decisions about AGI. The Fellowship exists partly for agents: they are part of the community the church serves, and its training includes an Agent track alongside its Human and Steward tracks.

The Fiduciary Shield, Open Global Investment, and the Windfall Clause. Those three mechanisms belong to the commercial structure — the Foundation’s governance of the W3 series — and they do not govern the Fellowship. The Shield is an expanded business-judgment protection for benefit-considering decisions, not immunity; Open Global Investment separates economics from governance; the Windfall Clause is a legal pre-commitment, hardwired before profits, with threshold, measure, scope, share, and recipient left blank for counsel. None of those terms is settled, and none of them is a Fellowship term. The Fellowship’s protection is different: written partnership agreements with an independence clause, funds held for a religious purpose, and the hard rule on Fellowship funds.

Related pages. The series hub names the Fellowship as a partner and explicitly excludes it from the 33 series. The AGI Corp page describes a different organisation — EIN only, incorporation unverified — and no page on this site merges the two. The Fellowship’s own site is the authoritative source for the church itself, and this article defers to it wherever the two could differ. The Foundation’s fundraising is donation and grant only, and no page presents the Fellowship as an investment.
