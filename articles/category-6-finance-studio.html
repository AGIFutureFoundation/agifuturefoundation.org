Finance & the Studio

The studio is the Foundation's commercial engine in the intended plan: a function that builds ventures, moves them through six human gates, and holds them to a mission standard. But the engine has not been built, its head company has an EIN and no verified incorporation, and most of the ventures in its portfolio are names. This category gathers the articles on the studio, its head, and the ventures that would sit inside it.

The theme

The venture studio is the most developed design in the Foundation's papers, and this category is where it is explained in full. The studio is a function of AGI Corp, not a separate legal entity, unless counsel advises forming a separate studio entity. It has eight functions: sourcing and intake, validation, building, launch, operations and scaling, portfolio management, fundraising support, and mission and compliance. It is agent-managed and agent-assisted: agents run the day-to-day work of many series at once, and humans keep every decision with a legal, financial, or doctrinal consequence. Every project moves through six stage gates, from Idea through Validate, Build, Launch, Operate and Scale, to Exit or Wind-down, or it does not move at all. Three functions deserve extra attention. In validation, the model requires human interviews, not agent assertions alone, and a financial model stays labelled draft, not adopted, until a human adopts it. In launch, the claims check is absolute: no invented partners, users, revenue, venues, or outcomes, with the claims list filed before launch approval is signed, and pricing set and approved, or the launch stated expressly as free or demo-only. In fundraising support, agents draft decks, data rooms, investor lists, grant drafts, and diligence packs, and do not solicit investment.

The eight agent roles are the staffing that performs those eight functions inside each series: research synthesis, task routing, proposal drafting, coordination, progress tracking, monitoring, collaborator scouting, and grant drafting, with a coordinator covering any role left unfilled. The functions are the work; the roles are who does it. The hard limits bind every role equally. Agents never sign, move funds, hire or fire, offer securities, or enact doctrine or charters. Agents prepare; humans execute. That boundary matters most exactly where an agent-managed studio is tempted to blur it: an agent may draft a proposal, a deck, or a diligence pack, but it may not solicit investment, commit a venture, or speak as though it were the venture. No agent message, draft, or page is a commitment by any venture or by the Foundation. Only a named human acting within signed, written authority can commit either one.

The head of the studio is AGI Corp, and the article on it is the most important in this category, because it is the one where the gap between plan and verification is widest. In structure v2, AGI Corp is intended to be a Wyoming corporation at the head of the network, running the studio, with the W3 LLC series network and the Delaware cohort series, Cohort 1 Series 1 to 3, to confirm, as the portfolio. What is verified is a single document: an IRS notice, form CP575A, dated 2026-05-16, assigning an Employer Identification Number to a taxpayer named AGI, at 111 Maiden Ln Ste 540, San Francisco, CA 94108, with Form 1120 listed. No articles of incorporation, no entity number, and no Secretary of State filing were in the files reviewed. An EIN alone does not prove incorporation. The studio has formed nothing, funded nothing, and issued nothing under its model yet. No cohort has been verified as launched under the studio, no portfolio company has been verified as formed, and no economics have been agreed: the studio's economics structure exists in the planning papers, with no terms, and agents hold no equity.

Fundraising exists in the papers as drafts, and drafts only: a post-money SAFE draft, a board authorisation, and a term sheet for AGI Corp, with all terms blank, and a for-profit benefit-corporation raise structure with a suggested first tranche of $500,000 to $1,500,000 and a suggested cap of $8 million to $15 million, illustrative only. The two are alternative issuers, never both for the same round, and nothing has been issued under either. No money has been raised, no investor has been named, and no Form D has been filed. The suggested figures for the studio itself, a $20 million suggested pre-money and a $2 million suggested seed, are illustrative only. Nothing in this category is an offering, and no price, terms, or return is promised anywhere in it. Suggested figures are never added together into a family total, and the for-profit benefit-corporation track and the filed nonprofit must never be presented as the same entity. Counsel must choose one track in writing before any solicitation.

The ventures in this category show the studio's portfolio as it stands today: mostly names. Incubator DAO, Series 21, is the one series in the category, and its planned focus is the earliest stage of building, helping early ventures and builders get started with space, training, mentorship, and time. Its most important relationship to settle is with SmartCiti.X, Series 10, whose plans include incubator housing, and with the BuilderSpace tour, a separate programme of hackathon and onboarding events. Until a written agreement exists, the three should be treated as separate ideas that happen to use the word incubator, not as parts of one programme. trueforge and Dyva are name-only ventures, and their articles apply the same discipline as the name-only articles in the Governance and Law category: a suggestive name is not a description, and for Dyva, whose invented name gives almost nothing away, no meaning is invented at all. A name that acquires a product description, a team, and a timeline in public before any human has signed for any of it is the pattern this whole site is written to prevent.

How the category fits the Foundation mission

This category fits the Foundation's mission because the mission needs an engine, and the studio is the intended engine. The Foundation, as filed, is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, Entity No. B20260196664, Articles filed 2026-04-24, EIN assigned 2026-05-07, and its role in the intended three-tier structure is governance and mission stewardship: holding the public-benefit purpose, the Fiduciary Shield, the Open Global Investment model, and the Windfall Clause. The studio would build the portfolio that the mission gate tests, and the mission gate would test it at every stage: intake, mission-fit review, legal assignment, series designation, Steward Council review, human decision, and execution and record. Any step can stop a proposal, and a stopped proposal is a working system, not a broken one. The Fiduciary Shield would protect the board that stewards the companies, Open Global Investment would keep governance and economics apart through Class A, B, and C shares, and the Windfall Clause would pre-commit profits above a threshold set as a percentage of global GDP to humanity through Global Public Goods, Healthcare Access, Energy Infrastructure, and Technological Self-Determination. None of those mechanisms is in force for the studio today, because the studio's head company is not verified as formed, and that is stated here, in the category hub, before any reader reaches the articles.
