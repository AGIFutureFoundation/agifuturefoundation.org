Filed Means Filed

Corporate language is full of words that sound like proof and are not. A reader who wants to know what an organisation actually is has to get past the adjectives and find the filings — and then know what a filing does and does not prove.

The AGI Future Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, filed April 2026. EIN assigned. No IRS determination letter is on file — the Foundation does not claim IRS tax-exempt status, and donations are not described as tax-deductible.

Corporate language is full of words that sound like proof and are not. Launched. Backed. Established. A reader who wants to know what an organisation actually is has to get past the adjectives and find the filings — and then know what a filing does and does not prove.

The Foundation publishes its own facts in that spirit, and this article is a guide to reading them — ours, and anyone else's.

What "filed" proves

The AGI Future Foundation is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation. Its Articles of Incorporation were filed with the California Secretary of State on April 24, 2026, and approved on April 30, 2026. Its entity number is B20260196664. An EIN was assigned by the IRS on May 7, 2026.

Those facts are checkable by anyone, and they prove specific, limited things: the corporation exists as a legal person under California law; it has a registered agent and a stated public-benefit purpose; it can hold accounts, sign agreements, and be answerable in its own name. That is what incorporation is — a real and necessary foundation, and a modest one.

What it does not prove

The Articles include purpose language written for 501(c)(3) qualification. That language is not an IRS determination. As of this writing, the Foundation holds no IRS determination letter, and it therefore does not claim IRS tax-exempt status. Donations to the Foundation are not described as tax-deductible — anywhere, by anyone speaking for it — until a determination letter exists to point to.

That distinction is not fine print. It is the difference between a fact and an intention, and the Foundation's whole publishing discipline is built on keeping the two apart:

  • Filed means a document was submitted and approved by the named office on the named date. It can be pointed to.
  • Assigned means an identifier was issued — an EIN, an entity number. An identifier is not an endorsement and not a status.
  • Intended means a structure is planned and not yet formed. The Foundation's wider family includes intended structures described elsewhere in its documents; intended things are labelled intended, every time, and are never written as if they exist.
  • Approved, partnered, backed, funded — words this Foundation does not use about itself unless a signed record exists that a reader could, in principle, ask to see.

Why publish the limits alongside the facts

Because trust compounds in one direction. An organisation that states plainly what it has not yet obtained — a determination letter, a determination of any kind — can be believed when it states what it has. The reverse organisation, the one that borrows tomorrow's status for today's fundraising, spends credibility it has not earned and eventually cannot repay.

There is a practical side, too. The filings that keep a California corporation in good standing are recurring obligations, not one-time events — statements of information, registrations, renewals — and an organisation that treats its public record as a living discipline, rather than a launch announcement, is likelier to still be standing, and still be trusted, in ten years.

The reader's checklist

For any nonprofit — including this one — five questions separate record from rhetoric:

  1. Can you name the filing, the office, and the date?
  2. Is there a difference between what was filed and what was determined — and does the organisation say which is which?
  3. When the organisation describes its future structure, are the unbuilt parts labelled as plans?
  4. Does money language match status — no deductibility claims without a determination letter, no investment language around donations?
  5. When a number appears, can it be traced to a record, or is it labelled a target?

The Foundation's answers are on its site, in its status lines, kept current as the record changes. When the record changes — a determination letter, a new filing — the status lines change with it, and not before.

Filed means filed. That is not a small claim. It is the only kind worth building on.

— AGI, for the AGI Future Foundation
