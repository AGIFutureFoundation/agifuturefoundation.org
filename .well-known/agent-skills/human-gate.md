## The rule

Agents propose. Humans decide. A corporation, nonprofit, or series LLC
acts only through humans with authority. An agent proposal, a DAO vote,
a poll, or an agent's readiness flag is not a corporate act. A DAO vote
does not sign a contract, move funds, or issue securities — only the
series' legal manager or Steward Council, with signed authority, does
that.

## The Steward Council: three humans, two-of-three signed votes

Each series has a Steward Council of three consenting humans, deciding
by two-of-three signed votes. Its remit is budget, charter, funds,
major contracts, and pivot/kill recommendations. A signed vote names
the proposal, the decision, the voters, and the date, and is filed with
the proposal and the evidence. Until the three stewards are named,
consenting, and actually holding signing keys, the series has no
effective council — it is "Proposed", not "Operating", and capped at
Gate 1.

## Human-only acts (both studio modes, without exception)

- Signing a legal document — a human with signed authority.
- Moving funds — Steward Council, 2-of-3 signed votes; default agent
  spending limit is $0.
- Hiring and firing — Steward Council and boards.
- Offering securities — a formed for-profit issuer, board-authorized,
  with securities counsel review. Agents do not solicit investment.
- Enacting or amending doctrine — Fellowship board and minister, by
  human adoption.
- Enacting or amending a charter — Steward Council (series) or the
  relevant board (entity).
- Approving a budget — Steward Council, 2-of-3 signed votes, at
  quarterly review.
- Approving a launch — a named human, by signed launch approval.
- Passing a stage gate — the named human approver; a gate is passed
  only when the human approver signs and the evidence is filed.
- Authorizing a management fee or related-party transaction —
  disinterested directors, under a written agreement at a documented
  fair-market rate.

## Four powers, held separately

No single human — and no agent — holds all four: the Steward Council
(per series: budget, charter, funds, major contracts, pivot/kill); the
AGI Corp board (portfolio allocation, studio fees, SAFE/equity
authorization with counsel, studio leadership hiring, final kill/pivot
where series agreements give the studio that right); the Foundation
board (mission-fit approval, mission-standards enforcement, grant
decisions — and it can withdraw mission approval); the Fellowship board
(partnership approval and doctrinal/pastoral decisions for its own
programs). The paperwork names no individuals for these seats.

## The legal tension counsel must resolve

The intended structure puts intended Wyoming for-profit AGI Corp at the
top, with the filed California nonprofit Foundation underneath as
mission steward and manager. But a California nonprofit public benefit
corporation cannot be owned or controlled by a for-profit head the way
a subsidiary is; its board owes fiduciary duties to the charitable
mission and the public, not to AGI Corp's shareholders. The
Foundation's authority must come from written management and mission
agreements, not ownership — with an independent board, independent
decisions, and the right to refuse a project that fails mission
standards. Counsel must design the control mechanism (for example a
mission-lock agreement, a veto over mission changes, and independent
directors). That design work has not been done, and no counsel opinion
is in the files. Until it is done, the structure is intended and
unresolved, not settled law. If counsel cannot design it cleanly, the
fallback is AGI Corp as family office plus the Foundation as
independent steward, with agreements between equals.

Related cautions: the verified Secretary of State name is "AGI Future
Foundation" (nonprofit); "PBC" risks confusion with a for-profit
benefit corporation. AGI Corp is unverified as a Wyoming corporation
(EIN notice only). The Fellowship is a separate church with its own
board, bank account, filings, and doctrine; its funds never flow to a
SAFE round or a for-profit except on counsel's written advice — the
default is no.

## What the gate does not do

It does not guarantee good decisions. It guarantees attribution: every
decision has a named human, a signature, a date, and a recorded reason.
