## Use this skill when

An agent is asked how the studio works, what the eight functions are,
how the two modes differ, what the stage gates require, or how the
portfolio dashboard stays honest.

## What the studio is, legally

The studio is a function of AGI Corp, not a separate legal entity —
unless counsel advises forming a separate studio entity. Studio
contracts are signed as AGI Corp or as the specific series, never in
the name of "the Studio" alone. The portfolio is the W3 Wyoming series
plus the Delaware cohort series C1S1, C1S2, C1S3 for new projects. A
project not in a series is not in the portfolio. The Foundation reviews
every studio project for mission fit, sets mission standards, receives
reporting, and can withdraw mission approval even if AGI Corp wants to
continue the project commercially.

## The eight studio functions

For each function, agents draft, research, build, coordinate, monitor,
and report; humans review, decide, approve, sign, and are accountable.

1. **Ideation / sourcing** — agents scan research, markets, repos, and
   community input, draft briefs, rank against mission and market
   criteria. A founder or steward selects which ideas enter validation.
2. **Validation** — agents draft customer/problem research, competitor
   mapping, feasibility drafts, financial model drafts, risk registers.
   Human interviews are required (not agent assertions alone); a
   financial model stays labelled "draft, not adopted" until a human
   adopts it.
3. **Build** — agents code, model, build, test, and document within
   scope. A human technical lead reviews architecture/security; a human
   signs IP assignments, licenses, and vendor contracts.
4. **Launch** — agents draft marketing, checklists, onboarding, and
   analytics. A human approves the launch and all public claims — no
   invented partners, users, revenue, venues, or outcomes — and pricing
   (no invented prices; a launch is free or demo-only unless pricing is
   set and approved).
5. **Operate / scale** — agents run day-to-day operations, support
   drafts, monitoring, reporting, and growth proposals. The Steward
   Council holds budget, hiring, major contracts, fundraising, and
   charter changes.
6. **Portfolio management** — agents maintain the dashboard, gate
   tracking, KPI reporting, and risk flags. Studio and AGI Corp boards
   allocate resources and make kill/pivot/scale and SAFE/equity
   decisions with securities counsel.
7. **Fundraising support** — agents draft decks, data rooms, investor
   lists, grant drafts, and diligence packs. A human founder/CEO
   pitches; a human board authorizes any SAFE/equity; counsel reviews
   all offering materials. Agents do not solicit investment.
8. **Mission + compliance** — agents draft mission-fit checklists,
   standards tracking, conflict-disclosure drafts, and filing
   calendars. The Foundation board holds mission approval; counsel and
   CPA hold filings, tax, securities, and Attorney General compliance.

## Two modes

- **Agent-managed:** agents run the day-to-day work within written
  limits set by humans, while a human Steward Council reviews.
- **Agent-assisted:** a human does the work with agents as co-workers;
  the human decides, signs, and is accountable. Required where
  judgment, liability, or religious/doctrinal authority is at stake.
- The rule for both modes is identical: an agent never signs, moves
  funds, hires or fires, offers securities, or enacts doctrine or
  charter changes on its own.

## The six stage gates

A project moves only through human gates. Each gate has a checklist, a
named human approver, and evidence that must be filed; a gate is passed
only when the human approver signs and the evidence is filed. An agent's
readiness flag is not a gate pass. A failed gate pauses or kills the
project.

- **Gate 0 — Idea:** agent brief filed; Foundation mission screen
  (pass, pass with conditions, or decline), minuted. A human selects
  the idea for validation or not.
- **Gate 1 — Validate:** human-interview-grounded research; financial
  model draft filed as "draft, not adopted"; series designation
  (operating agreement appendix signed, register updated, segregated
  books opened, DAO charter signed, Steward Council named). A series
  with no Steward Council is capped at Gate 1.
- **Gate 2 — Build:** prototype/demo built and tested; IP chain checked
  (signed assignment/license, or "none yet" stated expressly); security
  and privacy review by the human technical lead; all contracts signed
  by a human.
- **Gate 3 — Launch:** public claims checked and the claims list filed;
  pricing set and approved or the launch stated free/demo-only; terms,
  privacy, and customer contracts reviewed by counsel where applicable;
  launch approval signed by a named human.
- **Gate 4 — Operate / Scale:** quarterly Steward Council review;
  quarterly report filed (funds in/out, KPIs as actuals, risks, mission
  outcomes, related-party transactions); budget approved by 2-of-3
  signed votes; decision is scale, continue, pivot, or kill.
- **Gate 5 — Exit / Wind-down:** board and Council decide together,
  minuted with reasons; IP, data, customer obligations, and funds
  resolved in writing; books closed, accounts reconciled, register
  updated to "Closed" with date and reason.

## The portfolio dashboard — actuals only

One row per series (reference, project, gate, lead human, lead agents,
actual funds in/out, actual revenue or "none yet", actual mission
outcome, risks and blockers, next gate/decision), updated monthly.
Never put a projection in a revenue or funds-in column. Never list a
partner, customer, or investor who has not signed. A series with no
Steward Council is capped at Gate 1. The Fellowship is not a portfolio
row.

## Studio economics — structure, not terms

Every number is blank, for counsel and the board to set. Studio
ownership in a series exists only as stated in that series' operating
or management agreement, approved by its Steward Council and AGI Corp's
board; the default is that no studio ownership is assumed. Humans may
hold equity only as stated in a signed agreement for that series.
Agents hold no equity, no tokens, and no profit interests. Management
fees exist only under a written agreement at a documented fair-market
rate, approved by disinterested directors; no fee is charged by default.
Foundation donations or grants may fund a studio project only as a
grant serving a charitable purpose, with reporting — not as studio
equity, not as a SAFE, not as a subsidy for a commercial series' losses,
unless counsel advises a specific lawful structure in writing.

## What the studio is not

Not a fund (no pooling investor money across series unless counsel
structures a fund); not a DAO legal entity ("DAO-managed" describes
operations inside a series, not a legal person); not a church; not an
offer (nothing in the model, a dashboard, or a brief is an offer to
sell securities or a promise of returns).
