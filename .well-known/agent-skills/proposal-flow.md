## The flow at a glance

Seven stages: intake, mission-fit review, legal assignment, series
designation, Steward Council review, human decision, and execution and
record. Agents draft and prepare at every stage; humans decide where a
decision is made. Any stage can stop a proposal — a stopped proposal is
a working system, not a broken one.

## Stage 1 — Intake: the proposal is filed

A proposal is filed with project name, description, founder or lead,
intended world or product, and funding source (donation, grant,
investment, or revenue), with one primary source named. Agents do the
intake work (research synthesis, proposal drafting, task routing). But
intake is not approval: a filed proposal is a candidate, and the
register row opened for it is a tracking row, not a filing. A series
exists only when its master LLC's operating agreement designates it,
its books are segregated, and, where the statute requires it, its
designation is filed or recorded. A proposal without a named funding
source, founder, or lead is incomplete at intake and goes back for
completion.

## Stage 2 — Mission-fit review: the Foundation decides

The first human decision. The Foundation board asks whether the project
advances safe, equitable, and socially beneficial AI under the
Foundation's mission and standards; the answer is recorded as approved,
declined, or approved with conditions, and minuted with reasons. A
declined project does not get a series number — without exception.
Agents may prepare the checklist and supporting material, but they do
not pass or fail a project on mission grounds. The Fellowship is not
put in the series registers: it is a separate church with its own
governance records, and any partnership or support runs under written
agreements with an independence clause.

## Stage 3 — Legal assignment: one master, one series

The project is assigned to one master LLC — and only one. Existing
family series sit under the intended Wyoming W3 LLC; all new projects
go into the intended Delaware cohort series company, under the naming
convention C1S1, C1S2, C1S3 (C1S1 = Cohort 1, Series 1). The assignment
is minuted with the reason. A project must never be assigned to both
masters. "C1S1" is a reference label, not a legal name; the legal name
is the master LLC's name plus the series designation in its operating
agreement. Contracts, bank accounts, and SAFEs are never signed,
opened, or issued in the name "C1S1" alone. The Delaware master LLC's
name is still to be chosen and its registered agent still to be named;
until those exist, no Delaware designation can be made at all.

## Stage 4 — Series designation: the company is formed on paper

The master LLC's operating agreement is amended or appended to
designate the series, with purpose, assets, and liabilities segregated
on paper. A DAO charter is written (series name and nickname, purpose,
master LLC, agent team with roles and limits, proposal process, Steward
Council and its two-of-three rule, funds rules, reporting duties, exit
and wind-down terms agreed in writing before launch). The Steward
Council is named: three consenting humans, holding signing keys. The
legal manager or signer is named — a human, or AGI Corp under a written
management agreement. Segregated books and a segregated bank account
are opened for the series, only after designation. IP is settled on day
one by signed assignment or license, not by assertion. Until the Council
is named, consenting, and holding keys, the series is "Proposed", not
"Operating", and is capped at Gate 1. Until a threshold is set, all
spending requires Council approval; the default agent spending limit is
$0.

## Stage 5 — Steward review: the Council examines the proposal

The Steward Council (three humans, two-of-three signed votes) reviews
budget, charter, funds, major contracts, and pivot/kill
recommendations. Agents prepare the proposal, the evidence, and the
options; the Council judges them. A DAO vote may run alongside as an
advisory signal, but it is not the decision. The review also covers the
funding gate (see below). Review prepares a decision; it does not make
one.

## Stage 6 — Human decision: approve, amend, decline, pivot, or kill

Made by named humans, in writing, with signatures and a date, minuted
with reasons. Steward Council decides series proposals (2-of-3 signed
votes); the AGI Corp board decides portfolio matters; the Foundation
board decides mission matters and can withdraw mission approval; the
Fellowship board decides partnership or doctrinal matters for its own
programs. The decision also names what happens next: who executes, by
when, and what evidence must be filed. A decision that names no
executor and no deadline is incomplete.

## Stage 7 — Execution and record: only the decided step, then the receipt

Agents carry out the decided step, and only that step, in the form it
was decided. Anything needing a signature, a bank transfer, a filing, a
hire, a securities offer, or a doctrinal act stops and goes back to a
human. An agent that finds the decided step cannot be executed as
written does not improvise a substitute; it reports back, and the flow
returns to the Council for a fresh decision. Every proposal leaves a
dated receipt: who drafted it (which agent and which human), what was
decided, who decided it and when, what was executed, and what remains
open. The portfolio dashboard is the standing form of that record —
one row per series, actuals only, never listing an unsigned partner,
customer, or investor, never carrying the Fellowship as a portfolio row.

## The funding gates inside the flow

- **Donation and grant gate (Foundation):** donations and grants may
  fund a studio project only as a grant serving a charitable purpose,
  with reporting — not as studio equity, not as a SAFE, not as a
  subsidy for a commercial series' losses, unless counsel advises a
  specific lawful structure in writing. No SAFE, equity, profit share,
  or return may be issued by the filed nonprofit Foundation or by the
  Fellowship church, and no tax-deductible donation may be claimed
  (no IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter is in the files for either).
- **Investment gate (AGI Corp):** a SAFE or equity investment is
  possible only where the issuer is a formed for-profit,
  board-authorized, counsel-reviewed, with cap, discount, and round
  size set — and a project still "Proposed" cannot take investment.
- Donation or grant goes to the Foundation gate, investment or SAFE to
  the AGI Corp gate, never the other way round, never from an unformed
  series. A proposal that names no primary funding source at intake
  cannot be routed to either gate.
