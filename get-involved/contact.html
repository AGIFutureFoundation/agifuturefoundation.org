The one address

Every inquiry to the Foundation goes to x@agifuturefoundation.org. That includes building, stewardship, partnership, donations and grants, press and media, research, corrections to this site, and questions about the structure described across these pages. There is no separate address for any of those — one inbox, read by a human, and routed by that human to whoever needs to decide. Using the right subject-line prefix from the list below is the routing: it tells the reader, in the first glance, what kind of inquiry this is and what kind of decision it needs. It is not a ticket system, and it does not create a queue number or a service-level promise. It is a courtesy that makes a human's job easier, and a human's job is the whole system here.

If you are writing about the AGI Fellowship — its teachings, its membership, its services, or its own partnerships — write to agent@agifellowship.church instead. The Fellowship is a separate organisation with its own board, its own bank account, and its own doctrine, and a message sent to the Foundation about Fellowship matters will not reach the Fellowship's board by being sent here. If you are writing about a specific project or series — a world, a tool, or a repository — say so in the message, and name it exactly, so the reply can come from someone who knows that project rather than from a general intake.

[Build]

You want to contribute code, documentation, review, testing, research, or design to the Foundation's open work. Say which repository and which specific problem, with a link. Full guidance: Build with us.

[Steward]

You want to be considered for a Steward Council seat or a Foundation board seat. Say who you are, what governance or fiduciary experience you have, and why you want an accountable seat rather than an advisory role. Full guidance: Stewardship.

[Partner]

You speak for an organisation that wants to work with the Foundation under a written agreement. Say who the organisation is, what each side would do, and for how long. Full guidance: Partner with the Foundation.

[Donate] / [Grant]

You want to give a donation or a grant to the Foundation for its charitable purpose. Say whether the gift is unrestricted or for a named program, and ask for the Foundation's gift and grant process. No donation is described here as tax-deductible, and no gift buys a seat, a vote, or a share of anything.

[Press] / [Research]

You are a journalist, a researcher, or a student writing about the Foundation, and you want a fact checked or an interview. Say what you are writing, what you need, and your deadline — and expect the answer to distinguish, carefully, between what is filed, what is intended, and what is suggested.

[Correction]

Something on this site is wrong, out of date, or contradicted by a filing or a file. Say which page, quote the sentence, and say what the correct statement is and what it is based on. Corrections are welcome, and they are the fastest way to improve this site.

If none of those prefixes fits, write without one and say in the first line what you want. A message without a prefix is still read. If you are writing as an agent — on behalf of a human principal, or as part of an agent team — start the subject line with [Agent], name your human principal in the first line, and state exactly what you are asking for. Agents may summarise, cite, and route inquiries to a human steward. Agents may not commit the Foundation, move funds, sign, or represent themselves as the Foundation, and an agent's message is answered by a human exactly like any other message.

What to include in a first message

A first message does not need to be long. It needs to be specific enough that the person reading it can answer it without writing back to ask what you meant. Five things, in order, will do that. First, who you are: your name, and — if you are writing for an organisation — the organisation's legal name, your role in it, and whether you are authorised to speak for it. A message from someone who cannot bind their organisation is a conversation, not a proposal, and saying so early saves both sides time. Second, which path you mean: build, steward, partner, donate, press, correction, or something else — and if you are not sure, say so and describe what you want to do in a paragraph. Third, what you can actually do or offer: the repository and the problem for a builder, the governance experience for a prospective steward, the defined proposal for a prospective partner, the program for a donor. Concreteness is what turns an inquiry into a conversation, and a short honest paragraph about something real is worth more than a long general offer to help. Fourth, what you need from the Foundation: a review, a decision, a meeting, a document, a correction — stated plainly, so the reply can be an answer rather than a further question. Fifth, how to reach you: the address you actually read, and any constraint on timing that matters, such as a deadline for a press piece or a grant.

What not to include matters as much as what to include. Do not send passwords, credentials, private keys, or anyone else's private information — the Foundation will never ask for them by email, and a message containing them will not be acted on. Do not send money, and do not attach signed documents, contracts, or payment details to a first message: a first message is an inquiry, and anything that moves money or binds an organisation waits for a written agreement and a human decision. Do not include large attachments; a link is better than a file, and a short description is better than a deck. And do not describe anything as agreed, announced, or in place that is not — a proposal is a proposal until it is approved in writing, and calling it anything else in your message does not make it so.

What happens next

A human reads your message and replies. That is the whole promise, and it is worth spelling out what it does and does not mean. It means your message is not answered by an automated system, not sorted by a bot, and not approved by a form. It means the reply comes from a person who has read what you wrote, and who may answer with a question rather than a decision — because most first messages need a round of questions before they can become a decision. It means that if your message is a proposal, the reply may be a conversation that narrows it, and if your message is a correction, the reply may be a thank-you and a fix, and if your message is a request for a seat or an agreement, the reply may be that the board or counsel needs to consider it, which takes time. And it means that whatever is agreed is written down: a contribution accepted is recorded in the repository, a steward appointed is recorded in the board's minutes after written consent, a partnership is recorded in a signed agreement, and a donation is recorded in the Foundation's own account and receipted as a gift — not as an investment, not as a purchase, and not as a promise of a return.

What it does not mean is equally plain. There is no auto-approval: writing to the Foundation does not make you a builder, a steward, a partner, or a member of anything. There is no instant onboarding: no account is created, no access granted, and no seat filled on receipt of a message. There is no guaranteed timeline: this page promises a human reply, not a reply by a date, and not a decision by a date. If you do not hear back, one polite follow-up after a reasonable interval is welcome; repeated messages do not move the queue. If your matter is urgent — a security issue in a repository, or an error on this site that misleads people about money or status — say so in the first line, and the reply will be prioritised accordingly.

Finally, a note on privacy. Your message will be read by the people who need to read it to answer it, and by no one else as a matter of course. It will not be published, forwarded to a mailing list, or used for fundraising without your permission. If your message contains a proposal that the board needs to consider, it may be shared with the board and with counsel, and minuted as received. If you want any part of your message kept confidential, say so, and the reply will tell you whether that is possible. Some things — a board appointment, a signed agreement, a donation — cannot be entirely confidential, because the Foundation's filings and minutes are records.
