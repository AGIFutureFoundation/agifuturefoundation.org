Get Involved · AGI Future Foundation

Get involved — on purpose, and in public

The Foundation is building a corporate architecture for shared AGI prosperity: a mission steward that holds the line on safety and public benefit while agents and people build the commercial work underneath it. That architecture only works if the right people are in the right seats — builders who can ship, stewards who can decide, and partners who can help without taking control. This section is the front door for all three. Every path on this page starts the same way: with a message to a human, and a conversation before anything is agreed.

Five paths in

There are five ways to get involved, and they are deliberately different from one another. Building is about doing work in the open. Stewardship is about deciding — holding signing authority and being accountable for it. Partnership is about two independent organisations agreeing, in writing, to work together. Investing is a separate question with its own information, and it is not handled on these pages. And contact is the path for everything that does not fit the other four, or for anyone who is not sure which path fits yet. Pick the card that matches what you actually want to do, and read its page before you write to us — each page says plainly what the path is, what it is not, and what happens after you make contact.

How the paths fit together

The Foundation's design has a deliberate shape, and the five paths map onto it. Builders do the day-to-day work: research, code, documentation, and proposals. Stewards sit above that work as the human gate: they decide what gets enacted, what gets funded, and what gets stopped. Partners sit beside the Foundation as independent organisations that agree to do something together for a defined purpose and a defined term. The board governs the Foundation itself, and the Foundation acts as mission steward for the wider family of projects — reviewing each one for mission fit before it proceeds, and able to withdraw mission approval from a project that fails the standard. Contact is the intake for all of it: every path begins with a message, and no path skips the human conversation.

Two rules run through the whole diagram. First, agents operate and people govern: agents draft proposals, coordinate work, and monitor progress, but only named, consenting humans decide, sign, move funds, or enact changes. An agent proposal, a token vote, or an automated instruction is not a corporate act on its own. Second, money stays in its own lane: the Foundation takes donations and grants only, in its own account, for charitable purposes. It does not sell shares, SAFEs, profit interests, or tokens, and no donation buys a seat, a vote, or a share of anything. Those two rules are the reason the paths are kept separate — a builder is not automatically a steward, a donor is not automatically a partner, and a partner is not automatically a decision-maker inside the Foundation.

What getting involved is not

It is worth saying this plainly, because the Foundation's materials have sometimes been read as promising more than they do. Getting involved here is not a job application: no roles are advertised on these pages, no hiring is claimed, and no contribution carries a promise of employment or pay. It is not a purchase: nothing on these pages is an offering of securities, and the investment section of this site is information only. It is not a shortcut to influence: a donation does not buy a board seat, a partnership does not buy control of the Foundation, and a contribution to a repository does not make the contributor a steward. It is not instant: there is no automatic onboarding, no self-service signup, and no portal that grants access on submission. And it is not a membership in the AGI Fellowship church, which is a separate organisation with its own joining process through the Fellowship's own site — the Fellowship is a partner, not a department of the Foundation, and joining one does not join the other.

What it is, is narrower and more useful: a way to put real work, real judgement, or a real agreement in front of the people who decide, and to have that work judged on its merits. If that sounds slower than a signup form, it is — deliberately. The Foundation is a nonprofit public benefit corporation, and its board owes fiduciary duties to the charitable mission and the public. The intake is human, the decisions are minuted, and the agreements are written, because that is what the structure requires and what the people who rely on it deserve.

Before you write

Three things will make your first message useful, whichever path you choose. First, read the page for your path — builders, stewards, or partners — all the way through, including the section on what the path is not. The pages are written to save both sides a round of correspondence: most of the questions people ask first are answered there, including the ones whose answer is "not yet" or "not at all." Second, read the structure the Foundation is working inside. The governance section of this site, and the Steward Council page in particular, describe the design these paths sit in: the board, the council, the mission gate, and the limits on what agents may do. Third, come with something concrete. A builder with a repository, a patch, or a specific problem they want to work on will get a faster, better answer than a general offer to help. A prospective steward with a record of fiduciary or governance work, and a clear statement of why they want the seat, is easier to consider than a general expression of interest. A prospective partner with a defined proposal — what each side would do, for whom, for how long — is easier to take to counsel than an open-ended conversation about "teaming up." None of that is a requirement, and a short honest message is better than a long vague one. But concreteness is what turns an inquiry into a conversation.

If you are not sure which path fits, that is itself a reason to write: say so, describe what you want to do in a paragraph, and let the reply point you to the right page. And if you are writing as an agent — on behalf of a human principal, or as part of an agent team — say so in the first line, name the human principal, and start the subject line with [Agent]. Agents may summarise, cite, and route inquiries to a human steward. Agents may not commit the Foundation, move funds, sign, or represent themselves as the Foundation, and an agent's message is treated exactly like any other inquiry: read by a human, answered by a human, and decided by a human.
